We are part of the Folk Alliance International approved house concert venues. As members, we are exempt from paying royalties to BMI and ASCAP. We ask that artists who perform here should avoid reporting their playlists if they belong to these organizations.
We accept independent artists as well as artists affiliated with BMI and ASCAP.
We are unable to accommodate artists affiliated with SESAC, nor can music licensed by SESAC or BMI be performed.
Musicians who wish to be considered for a performance at Riverdog should first consider the type of roots-Americana music that we focus on before contacting us. 
(progressive bluegrass, folk/Celtic, old-time, roots-rock/blues/jazz, songwriter)
We are also focused mostly upon artists who create music, rather than simply perform it.
Although creative interpretations of the music of others is welcome as a part of any show, we generally do not consider "cover" bands or "tribute" bands. We have nothing against them, but it is not our thing.
We also mostly have artists who have a "focus" rather than willing to play any genre we wish. If your regular gig is a winery or wedding, it will probably not work here.
Please correspond by email: [email protected]
Please do not call with booking questions!
"The best answer after 'yes' is 'no'." Ray Bonneville
Unless you are locally known, it is unlikely that you will obtain a booking here unless you have the following:
​
1. Website
2. High quality photos
3. High quality videos.
4. Social media links.
5. A clear definition of what you are as an artist.
​
​Although slick music videos may help us promote a show, for booking purposes, we prefer a video taken at a live performance. We highly recommend hiring a professional videographer who can record directly off the soundboard to minimize venue noise. We strongly recommend shooting the performance from multiple angles and have it professionally edited. Although this can cost some money, we believe it will pay off in the end with higher quality bookings.

Upcoming Events
 Artists from around Northern California and the country will be playing everything from Americana and folk to pop and rock. Robin's Nest features award-winning singer-songwriters and some of the best musicians in Northern California. Don't miss this amazing season at Robin's Nest. Be sure to get on our email list for notice of each new concert. 
Photo courtesy of Steve Martarano.
A backyard community music series bringing together neighbors, musicians, and friends to enjoy local acoustic music, fine wine, and good food. 100% of the donations go to the musicians. 100% of the fun is shared by all.
Please Note: For everyone's enjoyment, we ask that you please bring low-back lawn chairs or position tall back chairs on the perimeter of the lawn. Please bring a bottle of wine or something to drink to share with others. Reservations are required. Go to the current year's concerts page to register for each event. A confirmation email will be sent with the address of Robin's Nest. Space is limited. Please notify us if you cannot make the show so others who have been wait-listed can attend. Doors open at 5:00. No early lawn chair placements are allowed. Only two reservations per person, please. This helps to reduce the number of no-shows and reduces the waitlist. 
NEST NOTES
Dinner options will not be available this year. Please consider bringing your own. 
​
​This season's concerts are dedicated to the memory of Bill Wagman, a much loved music lover, promoter, radio show host, and supporter of the arts. We miss you, Bill.
Contact Us: Interested in playing at Robin's Nest or have questions about the concerts? Contact us: Robin at robindd49@gmail.com or (for emergencies only please) call Robin at 530-400-2392.

 House Concerts
 WHAT is a House Concert? A house concert is a chance to experience music in a warm and intimate environment. It's when someone opens up their home and invites you into their living room to share in a performance by one of their favorite musicians. It's a chance to meet the performers and get them to sign their CD. A house concert is also a great social evening of friends and neighbors.
 WHY would anyone do this? We open up our home to our friends and neighbors because we want to share great music with them. In the area where we live -- like most of the world today -- there are too few venues where people can go to experience great music in a close and friendly environment. Great music makes us happy and we want to share that with others around us. These events allow us to give exposure to some incredible musicians whose talents we truly believe in and wish to help promote.
 Please note: "Russ & Julie's House Concerts" is simply the name we choose to describe our private parties, in our private home. These parties are NOT a business nor are they a business related activity. They are strictly a hobby for us, and are simply gatherings of our friends and guests to enjoy live acoustic music, fellowship, food (a potluck dessert buffet) and fun. Our concert parties are officially free (or else it might be considered a business), although voluntary contributions (suggested donation of $25 per person) are accepted at the door on behalf of the artists. 100% of all contributions go directly to the performers. By invitation only.

 Rita Hosking
Suggested Donation: $25 per person
 In a wavy world where we often struggle to swim, singer-songwriter Rita Hosking paddles up and canoes us through black holes, sinks of dirty dishes, demo-derbies, and wildfires until we’re safe at shore. She's got the spirit, and she knows how to reach ours through her grace, heart, chops, and spunk. With award-winning music spanning 9 albums and 20 years of touring, this country-folk troubadour is a heavy hitter in the Northern California songwriting scene.
 Rita's style of country-folk has been lauded for story and sense of place, and her performances praised with capturing the audience. She hails from Hatchet Mountain -- a mountain between Montgomery Creek and Burney, CA. A descendant of Cornish miners who sang in the mines of Cornwall and Grass Valley, CA, Rita grew up with deep regard for folk music and the power of the voice.
 In 2024, Rita released a set of what she calls “public service songs” on climate change adaptation and mitigation.

 The Refugees
Suggested Donation: $25 per person
 The trio of artists that is The Refugees emerged on the music scene as a verifiably unmatched force of talent, diversity, and experience. Each successful in their own right as a solo artist, Cidny Bullens, Deborah Holland and Wendy Waldman formed their unique and innovative group in 2007 and since that time have been wowing audiences, radio DJs, and music critics alike with their soaring harmonies, indelible musicianship, and unforgettably humorous stage presence. Individually, The Refugees have logged almost five decades in the industry, with more than twenty solo albums and multiple Grammy Award nominations to their credit, featuring musical styles that blend country, rock, folk, blues and Americana. To describe them as eclectic would be an understatement. These three artists have utilized their intense drive and relentless passions to create an entirely new sound.

Mission Statement of the Boston Area Coffeehouse Association
The Boston Area Coffeehouse Association was founded in May 2000 to help independent, non-profit musical performance venues in the greater Boston area to:
Increase communication, coordination, and cooperation between the various coffeehouses in the greater Boston area.
Work together on common projects which would spread the awareness of folk and acoustic music.
Share resources and knowledge.
Provide a focal point for communication between the Boston area coffeehouses and other organizations in the music business and media. (e.g. booking agencies, news media, etc.)
Provide an up-to-date calendar of events to help folk music lovers find concerts near where they live.

Second Sundays at Solamente
Always fans of house concerts, we looked around our Plaster Gallery one day and Kent Gurley looked at Sally Gurley and said we can have music here! Second Sundays at Solamente was born in 2019! Kent is a singer/songwriter at heart and hosts the music, singing a few and presenting the professional, featured musicians for each month. All money from ticket sales goes to the featured musician. 
An added bonus the Plaster Gallery brings to the table is the acoustics! Natural clay and lime plaster acoustically soften a space whether it is Solamente’s or yours. Guest musicians are constantly talking about the acoustics. 
Second Sundays at Solamente normally happens on the second Sunday of each month beginning at 7 P.M, doors open at 6:30 PM. Please sign up to subscribe to the newsletter to get all the pertinent information each month. Second Sundays focuses on acoustic music performances in the genres of Singer/Songwriter, Americana, Folk, New Folk and the occasional Western/Country.
We have been fortunate to attract some amazing musicians in the 6.5 years that Solamente Natural Plaster has hosted Second Sundays - that is 64 performances! My how time flies!

Frequently Asked Questions
Where are you located? 
We send out the address to each attendee a couple of days before the concert! 
We have an end unit townhouse with a small “Shoeless Sounds” garden flag in the front yard and a large flag hanging from the 2nd floor deck. The concert and food are up on the second floor. We don’t wear shoes inside, so we ask that you leave your shoes on racks at the door and come on upstairs. If you need help getting up the stairs, just ask. 
No need to ring the doorbell. Just come in!And wear your craziest socks! We’ll have a crazy sock contest with a prize for the winner. 
Where can I park? 
 There is plenty of parking behind our house! 
What time should I arrive? 
The house doors will open around 7pm. The music starts around 7:45pm. If you need to come earlier, be prepared to help us set up! 
What should I bring? 
Who doesn’t love dessert? It is a dessert/drinks potluck.Bring whatever you like – a bottle of wine, fruit, cookies…anything you’d like to share. 
You’re a guest in our home. We will be collecting a donation ($25 – $30 per person) in our “teddy bear jar” in the kitchen. One hundred percent of the donations go straight to the musicians! 
The artists usually bring merchandise so have a little extra cash or Venmo on hand! 
Do you have pets? 
Much to Will’s chagrin, we do not have dogs or cats, but we do have birds. If you see feathers or birdseed, you’ll know Bonnie and Clyde or their baby, Evonne, were rocking out to some music before you arrived. They will be hidden upstairs during the concert, though.

About the performers: 
Growing up, Joe Newberry and April Verch absorbed traditions of home and hearth - in his Missouri Ozarks and her Ottawa Valley of Canada. Although they are on the road much of the year, the two are fond of saying that they are rarely homesick, because their music means they always have a bit of home with them wherever they go. 
Newberry comes from a family of singers and dancers. He took up the guitar and banjo as a boy and learned fiddle tunes from great Missouri fiddlers. Verch grew up listening to her Dad’s country band play for dances in the Ottawa Valley. She started step dancing at age three and fiddling at age six.
In a Newberry & Verch show, delighted audiences see first-hand the roots of their music, their love of performing, and their strong musical connection. Original songs join timeless classics. Stories warm the heart, and give audiences a chance to understand where the music comes from. Lively fiddle and banjo numbers combine with traditional dance steps to illustrate happy times when people made their own fun.

South Bay House Concerts is a meet up launched in 2014 by Tami Mulcahy. The goal was to build a unified fan base from south of San Francisco (the Peninsula) to San Jose region. We are now over 1650 members strong with a network of hosts that put on an event once a year or every month. It's a labor of love but once the music starts, it's all worth it.
This is an all volunteer effort. There is camaraderie in the region (East Bay, Marin, San Francisco, South Bay) to coordinate when possible and also to be a resource to each other. 
If you do not live in the San Jose metroplex, research house concerts in your area. If nothing is there, built it!
The following is from the SBHC meet up website:
If you build it they will come…a South Bay network of house concert and small live music venues, and all us fans who love the up close and personal music experience. This site is for music lovers of all genres to check out what's happening on the San Francisco peninsula down to San Jose. The artist talent pool is amazing. Let's build the audience!
Never been to a house concert? You're in for a treat! Defined as "true listening environments", they are typically small gatherings in the privacy of a home or small stage. Usually 30-50 people. Occasionally, like if someone has a super cool back yard, it's easy to fit in more. Every host runs things a bit different. Typically, seating is provided. But you may be asked to bring a chair or a blanket. Typically it's BYOWhatever. But, there might be social time and/or a potluck.
Guests are asked to be very respectful of the house. Stick to the concert area, be clean and no smoking.
The artist reflects the flavor of the host, from classical to singer songwriter. Don't' worry if there are just a couple rsvp's. Most experienced hosts have cultivated a mailing list. This site is an adjunct to that and to help new house concert providers get started by establishing a shared pool of fans. The more house concert venues, the more fans, the more artists who get to share their amazing talents.
Want to become a host? It really is easy. But it is work and the artist depends on you to be dependable. Here are a couple resources: http://www.americansongwriter.com/2014/02/songwriter-u-make-real-money-donation-based-house-concerts/http://www.concertsinyourhome.com/CIYH_HouseConcertGuidex.pdf
PLEASE NOTE!!!! Seating is limited. No shows are a big deal. Attendance is taken. The reputation of this meet up will be one of dependability, as if you paid for your ticket in advance.

Brian Whelan
BRIAN WHELAN made playing with country, rock, and Americana stars, such as Houston’s own Mike Stinson, Nashville roots godfather Jim Lauderdale, and Los Angeles rocker Chris Shiflett. WHELAN put his unmistakable mark on the national scene as the prolific sideman behind urban cowboy Dwight Yoakam, playing guitar, keys, pedal steel, and anything else that was required or requested. From budding fan to established keystone, WHELAN is now at the center of that Los Angeles Americana scene he discovered years ago.
Brad Colerick
Brad has been writing and performing music since he was a kid. He has had the good fortune of singing on many commercials over his career to become a vested SAG-AFTRA Union member. He is one of the most ubiquitous Los Angeles performers, performing from Pasadena through Northern California. His warm, sunny, homey tenor voice brings alive the songs of his experiences.
In 2009, he started the weekly “Wine and Song” concert series to present the music he loves to his South Pasadena community. Additionally, he had been the Music Director of the annual South Pasadena Eclectic Music Festival.
Jeff Kossack
Jeff had been making music all his life, and always had some sort of recording rig, but always knew his engineering and production savvy would take off once he focused his attention on his songs.
After 2006, Jeff began focusing on production, and has produced and recorded songs for more than 150 artists, with 20 of them being full CD projects. All of this work was done either for free or on sliding scale because everyone knows artists rarely have disposable income. He did so because he knew it would be easier to master the production side of the desk if he wasn’t also the “talent”.
Stephen Michael Schwartz
Stephen Michael Schwartz, a composer/recording artist who performs live music throughout the world to a wide range of audiences both young and old, joins a unique group of musicians who have made a living with their music for over forty years. Stephen’s reputation as a superlative singer-songwriter-performer came into focus as one of the three original members of the award-winning children’s musical group, PARACHUTE EXPRESS. The first group ever to be signed to Walt Disney Records Music Box Series, PARACHUTE EXPRESS released six albums and performed on some of the biggest stages while associated with Disney.
With JaKL
JaKL, (Jaynee Thorne, Karen Tobin, Leslie Barker) The Los Angeles-based singer/songwriters with numerous recording credits, have joined forces, talents, songs, and harmonies to create a compelling vocal blend. Their sound is acoustic, organic, and unique. Their well-crafted original songs, as well as choice selections from other esteemed writers, involve the listener in their arrangements and the labor of love.

ABOUT US 
Hosting the creative spirit and acoustic music since 2005…
North Hills has the coolest gathering place for live music UNDER THE STARS. Nestled in the heart of the San Fernando Valley is the Stardust House Concerts series, complete with a guitar-shaped fire pit in the middle of the stage! Admission is by donation, and all proceeds go to the performers.
A house concert s a music concert performed inside or outside a private home in front of an intimate audience. And there is a POTLUCK dessert, drink and snack bar shared by all. Stardust Concerts came about in 2005 as a work of love for the musical arts when finding a quality non “pay-to-play” venue to perform in Los Angeles became virtually impossible. Come join the artistry and the community of local and world artists.
Doors open at 6:15 P.M. Start times are at 7:00 P.M. sharp.
PLEASE RSVP TO: stardustconcerts@gmail.com
An email will be sent to you with the address and directions.
Please note: “Stardust House Concerts” is simply the name we choose to describe our private parties, in our private home. These parties are NOT a business nor are they a business-related activity. They are strictly a hobby for us and are simply gatherings of our friends and guests to enjoy live acoustic music, fellowship, food (a potluck dessert buffet), and fun. Our concert gatherings are officially free (or else it might be considered a business), although voluntary contributions (suggested donation of $20 per person) are accepted at the party on behalf of the artists. 100% of all contributions go directly to the performers. By invitation only.

About Us
Stone Room Concerts began in 2012 as a series of private house concerts. The initial venue was in the basement of a private home, a stone house located in the Seven Corners area of Falls Church and built in 1941. From 2013-2016, we held shows approximately once a month on Sunday nights at Bikenetic, a bicycle shop in Falls Church. From the Fall 2014 until the pandemic, we hosted shows in collaboration with The Falls Church Episcopal church, and occasionally at the Unitarian Universalist Church of Arlington.
As of July 31, 2014, Stone Room Concerts was incorporated in the Commonwealth of Virginia, and has received 501(c)3 nonprofit status with the IRS, also effective as of that date. In 2024, we began a partnership with Hill Center at the Old Naval Hospital in Washington, DC, to host concerts there.
Stone Room Concerts includes as part of its mission support for charitable causes, including acting as a fiscal sponsor for the Niñas Arriba! college scholarship fund for young women in El Salvador and supporting the Mayan Kakchiquel musician Sara Curruchich. House Concerts
Stone Room Concerts -- now a 501c3 -- began in 2012 as a series of private house concerts. The initial venue was in the basement of a private home, a stone house located in the Seven Corners area of Falls Church and built in 1941. These occasional concerts are 
carried out privately and not under the auspices of Stone Room Concerts.
 Stone Room Concerts can also bring music to other homes anywhere in Maryland, DC or Virginia. We always have more artists than we're able to host, so we're able to book artists in other, less regular house concert venues. We bring the chairs, sound system (if needed), and arrange the dates with the artists - you provide the guests. (You could call this the Rolling Stone Room Concerts!) Folks interested in hosting a house concert should be in touch with David at stoneroomconcerts@gmail.com.
House concerts will normally begin at 8 pm, with the doors open 30 minutes beforehand. Attendees will be able to reserve their seats on arrival. BYOB. An appetizer and dessert are always welcome but not required -- some light refreshments will be provided.
Look for the link on the website to reserve your spot or purchase a ticket.
Before the concert, we will email each of our confirmed guests all of the details, including directions. We will also appreciate it if you can let us know if you have to cancel a reservation, so that we can make room for others.

What inspired you to start doing this?
Before starting this series, the only two house concerts I'd ever attended were with Beth Wood and Bethany & Rufus. I still vividly remember those musical experiences, and they inspired me to try to recreate similar moments for others. In 2004, I arranged a tour for a college a cappella singing group of which I was once a member (back in 1979-80), and brought them to El Salvador and Guatemala. It was a great experience for the singers, but, more importantly, I received great feedback and karmic pleasure from introducing their musicmaking to a wider audience. 
Can I reserve in advance?
Yes! I'll take reservations for any concert that's listed on the website. If we reach capacity for any given concert (45-50), I'll send out an email to confirm that you're still interested and able to attend the concert. I kept the subscription current for another ten years and discovered hundreds of emerging artists via the monthly magazines and accompanying disks. One such artist was Josh Rouse who, in 1998 released his debut album “Dressed Up Like Nebraska,” the title track of which appeared on the monthly CMJ disk. I loved the song and bought the album. About a year or two later, Josh released his second album, “Home,” the single from which, “Directions,” was also included on a CMJ compilation disk. How it Started
Trevor and Allison Whiting are music lovers, through and through. Whether making their way out to see various artists at festivals or solo shows, or getting out to play a little music themselves, it’s a shared love that’s only getting stronger. Music brings them happiness and it brings people together. 
Around the same time they were building their home they went to the Levon Helm Ramble to see the first of a few concerts. It was those shows that inspired them to create their own version of the Ramble—that beautiful energy and appreciation for live music—in their home, while supporting artists the best way they know how -- paying a fair wage for their talents, feeding them a good meal or two, and offering them a safe space to rest before they head back out on the road. 
​
The Mission
When Covid-19 hit, they began thinking about how they could amplify the good energy fellow music lovers brought to the space in a way that aligned with their commitment to being “100% for music” and paying musicians a fair wage. When we couldn't be together, they hosted "pay what you can" virtual events to support musicians whose gigs had been cancelled and their futures uncertain. They also personally contributed to fundraisers for musicians with mounting bills and families to support without steady gigs to make a living.

What is the Trash Moon Collective?
The Trash Moon Collective is an organization run by Melissa Chilinski that organizes inexpensive (often free) grassroots concerts, dances, workshops, and jams. The Collective itself is made up of musicians and music fans who love, play, and listen to bluegrass, old-time, and folk music – and want to welcome other people to share it, too. Sound like fun? Join us! Since 2023, Trash Moon has been fostering spaces where people can come in and feel welcome to dance, sing, listen, promote their art, and hang. Trash Moon has hosted intimate listening room shows for amazing bluegrass, old time, and folk artists including Jake Workman, Brittany and Natalie Haas, Rakish, Big Richard, Maya DeVitry, Jeffrey Foucault, Brad Kolodner, and more! Learn more about the Trash Moon story, and check out the event calendar.
'Join' as in...play, too?
Yup.
What if I'm a beginner?
We want people to feel part of something. So instead of offering only shows (and we have a crop of amazing bands who put on some great shows) we invite people to participate, too. Do you already play an instrument? Pick up your fiddle/guitar/banjo and come to one of our jams. Want to learn an instrument? Come to the slow jam, one of our workshops, or reach out to Trash Moon Collective Members in our Teacher Directory. You have never seen a more welcoming group of people who will help you get started. So much more fun that doing it alone.
What kind of events?
Favorites include the Wednesday Jam at the Woodbine Food Hall (all skill levels), the weekly Bluegrass Night at Gracie’s, the monthly honky tonk dance at Fisher Brewing, our seasonal house concert series at Lois Lane, and our annual Hootenanny at the Commonwealth Room. Frequently Asked Questions
Q: Can I bring snacks & drinks?
This is a casual BYOB event. No alcohol or food will be provided or sold. The Treehouse will provide plastic cups and napkins. You are welcomed to bring your own small cooler and snacks.
Q: What to wear & bring?
The Treehouse can get a bit chilly in the evenings at times unless the weather is super hot that day so it's recommended you bring layers of clothing or at least a light jacket. Feel free to bring a blanket for your comfort. We will have some folding chairs available as well on a first come-first served basis.
Q: Can I bring my pet?
While we’re lovers of animals we appreciate you not bringing your pet to the event.
Q: Smoking?
There will be a designated smoking area.
Q: Tickets?
​Tickets are available online and non-refundable. NO tickets will be sold at the door.

Ian Moore, the Austin-based blues-rock guitarist and songwriter, draws inspiration from a diverse range of artists, including The Beatles, Curtis Mayfield, Stevie Wonder, and Sly & the Family Stone. He honed his skills on the road as a guitarist with Joe Ely, an experience that eventually led to solo tours opening for the Rolling Stones and ZZ Top. Moore released his debut album in 1993, followed by his final Capricorn release, Modernday Folklore, in 1995. The album marked a creative shift, expanding beyond the blues-rock traditions of his Texas roots and incorporating elements of Southern soul, gospel, and Beatles-influenced pop alongside the powerful blues riffs that had defined his early sound.
While Moore continues to explore the blues in his recordings, it's his live performances that have earned him a devoted following. From blistering electric blues to deeply personal songs that showcase his more soulful side, Moore brings an unmistakable energy and emotion to the stage. Still touring extensively, he continues to captivate audiences—and his loyal fans eagerly anticipate every performance.

Giulia Millanta
 Florence-born and Austin-based, singer-songwriter Giulia Millanta brings a worldly voice and fearless creativity to Under the Oaks.
A prolific artist with nine solo albums, Giulia is an accomplished guitarist and multi-instrumentalist who sings in four languages. Her music blends folk, Americana, blues, and artful pop with what critics have called “psychedelic grooveability” and a soul-baring lyrical depth. Whether on acoustic or electric guitar, ukulele, or with unexpected textures woven into her arrangements, Giulia creates music that feels both intimate and expansive.
Since moving to Austin in 2012, she has become a respected presence in the city’s songwriter community, collaborating with and recording alongside renowned musicians including Charlie Sexton, Marc Ribot, Gabriel Rhodes, and Glenn Fukunaga. She has performed in premier venues across the U.S. and Europe, from Club Passim in Cambridge to Rockwood Music Hall in New York and the Teatro Romano in Florence.
Thoughtful, poetic, and musically adventurous, Giulia Millanta delivers performances that are immersive, emotionally rich, and uniquely her own.

Mission Statement of the Boston Area Coffeehouse Association
The Boston Area Coffeehouse Association was founded in May 2000 to help independent, non-profit musical performance venues in the greater Boston area to:
Increase communication, coordination, and cooperation between the various coffeehouses in the greater Boston area.
Work together on common projects which would spread the awareness of folk and acoustic music.
Share resources and knowledge.
Provide a focal point for communication between the Boston area coffeehouses and other organizations in the music business and media. (e.g. booking agencies, news media, etc.)
Provide an up-to-date calendar of events to help folk music lovers find concerts near where they live.

Chances are likely that you'll find something new has been added or improved upon each time you come to The Yard! Mark and Sharon are continuously building, creating, decorating and planning for even greater improvements so that your experience at The Yard is always the best!

General Questions
Does 20 Front Street serve alcohol?
20 Front Street does not serve alcohol. In an effort to deliver the most pure music forward experience possible, we have elected to keep the venue alcohol free. We have a wonderful café on-site, Wee Bean Coffee Co., that serves extraordinary coffee and tea drinks, as well as, an assortment of fresh pastries. We also have an eclectic assortment of restaurants and bars in Downtown Lake Orion that are all within walking distance of 20 Front Street.
What should I wear?
We think you’d be most comfortable looking your best for a night out! We do not have a strict dress code, although most of our patrons appreciate the opportunity to dress a little nicer for the occasion.
Where should we park?
Please find the Lake Orion Downtown Development Authority’s parking map HERE.
Is 20 Front Street Handicap Accessible?
Yes. The venue, café, and ice cream shop in the rear of the building are all handicap accessible. If you require further assistance, please do not hesitate to give us a call at (248) 783-7105.
What time do doors open on show nights?
Doors open 30 minutes prior to your scheduled show time. If you have an exclusive VIP Meet and Greet ticket, a representative from 20 Front Street will be in contact prior to the Meet and Greet date to give detailed instructions as scheduling for these opportunities fluctuates.
Can I take photos or videos during the show?
As a listening room, 20 Front Street engages guests with an intimately immersive sonic experience unlike any other. To help create this experience, we kindly ask that all electronic devices are silenced and securely stored out of sight unless the artist instructs otherwise at the beginning of the show.

About Us 
The 443 Social Club & Lounge (formerly The Listening Room at 443) is an eclectic live music venue located in the historic Hawley Green neighborhood.
We have created an intimate home for live, original music boasting a top-notch sound system and a comfortable, unique, and funky vibe.
We host regional and national singer-songwriters in our cozy room without all the usual distractions. Our guests enjoy music the way it’s meant to be experienced, learn the stories behind the songs, and get up close and personal with our performers.
We are passionate about live music, our community, and creating a venue where all are welcome.
We fill our calendar with talented touring musicians from around the country – many who have never visited Syracuse before – and the very best regional performers. The connection between artists and storytellers with an appreciative, attentive audience creates a unique and powerful magic.
The flipside of our “up close and personal” vibe is the financial constraint of our limited square footage – our seated capacity is only 75 people. We depend on the support of our community to survive.
If you’d like to help us keep original music coming to Syracuse and become part of our passionate community of live music lovers, consider becoming a member – you can find details, price levels and all the groovy perks on our website HERE.
We would be honored if you would join us.

Who We Are
Five Points Music is a nonprofit organization dedicated to exploring the power of sound in all its glory. We produce engaging concerts, workshops, and events that not only celebrate diverse musical talents but also serve as a platform to develop and promote our core community programs.
Our Mission & Vision
Our aim is simple yet profound: we want audiences of every ability to enjoy the magic of live music without barriers. By combining live events, outreach initiatives, and strategic partnerships, we’re able to support three key areas:
Hearing Loss Advocacy
We work to create a more sound-friendly world by managing safe decibel levels, offering free hearing protection for all age groups, and providing hearing screenings at festivals, concerts, and community gatherings.
Accessibility Initiatives
Our goal is to expand ADA access and inclusivity—both at our own shows and in partnership with other venues, organizers, and artists. When it comes to live music, we believe no one should be left on the sidelines.

the heartbeat of our nonprofit is a growing community of artists and art lovers celebrating the power of dynamic visual, auditory, and taste experiences to explore place and bring communities together. 
ændre directors Sarah Farrell and James Mackessy bring together a diverse range of experience in the service industry, performance art, design-build, installation art, gallery and event curation. ændre’s board is dedicated to supporting the arts through collaboration, production, and promotion.
join us at our next art happening and participate as we develop and curate an artistic gathering space set on an old 16 acre farm site in the natural splendor of the Driftless region.
ændre arthouse is a non-profit organization 501(c)3. As such ændre arthouse does not discriminate based on gender, age, or race. We are an equal-opportunity organization that seeks to serve the community through enriching arts, music, and cultural events.
