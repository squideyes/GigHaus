Our Story
The Drake is a one-of-a-kind, world class live music and performing arts venue in the heart of downtown Amherst, MA. Our mission is to support arts, culture, and community as a 501c3 non-profit run by the Downtown Amherst Foundation. 
Founded in the spring of 2022 the Drake was an answer to a town-wide call for a long needed live music, nightlife, and arts space. As an economic driver The Drake is a regional destination to compliment the area’s long established academic climate, rich arts culture, and local New England history.
In our initial years of operation the venue has been a home to prominent national and global acts (from Dinosaur Jr. to Tank & the Bangas, Regina Carter, the Lemonheads) as well 
as beloved local talent. 
Much more than just a club, The Drake provides ongoing support and programming with Amherst Regional public schools and students, and special curated music series with partners Amherst 
College and the UMass Fine Arts Center. 
Supported in part by grants and public donations, The Drake serves as a partner for other area non-profit organizations as a site for benefit events and activities.
The venue belongs to the people - fully accessible to all, including open mic nights and monthly jazz jam sessions, free to students. 
And at the heart, The Drake is a pillar of community for the people of Amherst and beyond. 
 Why Save the Drake ?
Our name is owed to piece of local Amherst lore. Generations ago The Drake was a beloved basement tavern in what was once the Perry Hotel, one block away on Amity Street. As time went on the building was destined to change hands which meant the closing of the storied haunt in 1985, much to the chagrin of Amherst locals.

FAQ
Is there a box office to buy advanced tickets in person? What about those pesky service fees?
Box Office hours are Mondays from 3-6pm! 
Also, you can always buy tickets in person (without fees) for future shows any time the Drake is open for a ticketed show. See event calendar for this week’s show dates, but typically doors open at 7PM four to six nights per week. The service fees are often assessed by various promoting partners we work with, and not directly by the Drake. Keep in mind, we are a non-profit, and the dollars you spend here support the arts and those presenting the arts.

School Show Series 2026-27
CALLING ALL TEACHERS AND HOMESCHOOLERS!
The arts are an essential part of the learning process; they enhance creativity, teach
lessons in nontraditional ways, and give youth new avenues of expression. Our School
Show Series provides a way for students to get out of the classroom and visit this
community resource for a shared experience. For some students, it’s their first time in a
theater! Shows are presented by local actors, relate to a range of topics, and may also
include curriculum guides.
-Tickets are available for schools and homeschool groups only. These shows
are not for public sale.
-Cost: $14 each (Please note: teachers and chaperones must purchase a
seat).
-Cost for Title 1 Schools: $10.
– All shows require a non-refundable 40% deposit at the time of booking, while
the remaining fee is due two weeks prior to the performance date.
-Shows available for pre-school – middle school. Please consult the
individual show for age guidelines.
-All shows are weekday performances. Most are at 9:30am & 11:30am.
Transportation Grants Available! The Firehouse Center for the Arts is thrilled
to provide $300.00 transportation grants to help schools meet the costs of
educational field trips to the Firehouse Center for the Arts’ School Show
Series.
For Transportation Grant Program Guidelines and to request an application,
contact Megan Blouin-Little directly at [email protected]

Get to know us 
Flat Iron Crossroads is a 501(c)3 non-profit performing art center in Gloucester, VA. The venue is organized by an all-volunteer team who dedicate their time to the enlightenment and well-being of its community and visitors through culture, entertainment, and education by providing a diverse selection of performing arts that bring local, national, and international experiences to Eastern Virginia 
 The venue was revitalized and transformed from a 100-year-old gas station into an intimate, modern performing arts center. Just as gas stations have long prepared travelers for journeys, Flat Iron Crossroads strives to help artists and patrons discover avenues of musical adventure both familiar and untraveled.
Since its inception in 2019, FIX has added an 800-square foot outdoor stage on a sweeping lawn that seats 500 guests. The indoor stage is surrounded by intimate , one-of-a-kind portraits and posters, with a stocked bar, patio access, and seating accommodations with a capacity of 140 guests.
Flat Iron Crossroads exists to celebrate the joy of music and the arts for all ages. We work with the community to put on educational events and provide music-based program sponsorship. The Floyd Country Store 
Our Story
Nestled just a stone’s throw from Floyd County’s only traffic light, The Floyd Country Store sits at the very heart of our vibrant community. For well over a century, this historic space has served as a gathering place for locals, travelers, and passersby alike.
First opening its doors as a downtown general store in the early 1900s, the space underwent a cultural transformation in the early 1980s. Then known as Cockram’s General Store, it became the epicenter of the local Appalachian music and dance scene when musicians and dancers began gathering weekly to share tunes and camaraderie—a tradition that blossomed into the famous Friday Night Jamboree.
Today, the Store remains a proud beacon of Appalachian culture, celebrated for bringing people together through traditional music, dance, art, and food. Whether you are a lifelong resident or a curious wanderer, we invite you to join a workshop, share a delicious house-made meal, step back in time at our old-fashioned Soda Fountain, or experience one of our many live events.
Owned and operated by Dylan Locke and Heather Krantz since 2014, The Floyd Country Store is dedicated to cultivating and strengthening community through experiences rooted deeply in Appalachian traditions.

Music & Dance Traditions 
Since the early 1900s, the site of The Floyd Country Store has been a hub for people. Originally called Farmer’s Supply, it was a place where farmers, craftsmen, laborers and travelers all converged to trade wares and discuss the topics of the day. When the workday was done, these people of all ages and backgrounds would converge here to dance, and share the music and lore traditional to the mountainous area known today as the Blue Ridge Plateau.
The music of this time and place — Mountain Music — was prolific in its development. Traditional English, Scots-Irish, German, French, Spanish, African and Indigenous cultures coalesced to invariably trade ideas and culture, producing a music that itself made no delineations between origin, race or gender, absorbing anything in its path. It grew in abundance, creating a richly mixed heritage that is still respected and admired today, in part due to its role in establishing the foundations of earliest recorded Country music. This notion is best encapsulated by Kinney Rorer, who describes this particular region of Southwestern Virginia as “a fertile crescent” for old-time music – one of a number of areas in North America that served as a hub for musical and cultural exchange.

About Us
WSLR+Fogartyville is a center for creative expression and community engagement that amplifies the voices of our diverse community, and promotes peace, sustainability, democracy, and economic & social justice. 
WSLR, Inc. was established in 2004 to launch and oversee a 100-watt low-power, non-commercial community radio station dedicated to amplifying local voices. In August 2005, WSLR LP 96.5 FM took to the airwaves, made possible by the dedication and hard work of a small group of volunteers committed to creating an independent, grassroots media platform for Sarasota. Today, WSLR LP 96.5 FM is a listener-supported, nonprofit, noncommercial FM radio station serving the Sarasota community on the radio dial and streaming worldwide at WSLR.org. Gibson Community Music Hall
Home to performers & fans of original Live music! 
Gibson Community Music Hall is located in the historic Gibson building in the heart of downtown Appleton. In 1929, after a fire destroyed a tire store on the property, the building was reborn as an automobile dealership and rebuilt in 1931 using classic “Machine Age” Art Deco style, as is still seen today. We are honored to be part of the history of this great building and city.
We are a top-notch live music venue that showcases original music artists representing a variety of music genres. Our small, close-knit team prides itself on bringing you great music in a beautiful historic building with a friendly environment which values an active listening experience. Hope to see you at a show soon!

Chuck Suchy
Chuck Suchy’s life and music are inseparable from his roots. He was born, raised, and continues to be an active farmer on his family’s land south of Mandan, North Dakota, where he operates the farm alongside his wife, Linda, an accomplished musician who often performs with him. Though he performed in local clubs and lounges throughout his youth, a pivotal moment came in 1982 when he realized the demanding life of a working farmer was deeply worthy of song, inspired by the Canadian folk classic “Field Behind The Plow” by Stan Rogers. This realization ignited a songwriting career focused on chronicling the regional experience.
His career highlights include performing on the prestigious Kennedy Center’s Millennium Stage and making multiple appearances on the nationally syndicated radio show, “Prairie Home Companion.” Over his career, he has released eight studio albums, with themes expanding from farm life to deeper explorations of human relationships and North Dakota’s diverse cultures.

About Us
Since 1976, Godfrey Daniels has been considered one of the best listening rooms around, where people come to appreciate live music, and to celebrate a rare place where both the artist and the audience rise to the occasion. This wonderful reputation makes it possible to see some great acts that one ordinarily could only see on larger concert stages, as well as catching emerging stars on their way.
Thanks to our Members, Volunteers, and the Artists as well, we continue to evolve as a community resource, with our successful monthly jams, open mikes, story circles and youth outreach.

Alice’s Restaurant isn’t around anymore. But, as the song says, “Alice didn’t live in a restaurant. She lived in the church nearby the restaurant…” And the old Trinity Church, where Alice once lived and where the saga began has become home to The Guthrie Center. 
Arlo Guthrie sought to provide a place to bring together individuals for spiritual service, and founded the Guthrie Center, an Interfaith Church, in 1991. It fulfills Arlo’s aim to meet the ongoing needs of the community, and support cultural preservation and educational achievement. The Trinity Church where the song “The Alice’s Restaurant Massacree” began and where the movie “Alice’s Restaurant” was filmed, continues to service the local and international community. 
 Read more ... 
Originally built as the St. James Chapel in 1829, the structure was enlarged in 1866 and renamed Trinity Church. Ray and Alice Brock purchased the property in 1964 and made it their home. The building has had several owners since the early 1970s. 
The Brocks had been on the staff of a small private boarding school in the area – The Stockbridge School. Ray Brock, trained as an architect, taught a shop class and Alice worked as the school librarian. They were wonderfully creative people and attracted a number of students with whom they shared their idealism and their creativity. Arlo Guthrie was one of them. 
After four years of high school in Stockbridge, Arlo graduated in the spring of 1965 entering Rocky Mountain College in Billings, Montana the following fall. His college career was short lived, however, and he returned to the Berkshires in November of 1965. He stayed with his friends, Ray and Alice, at the church during the Thanksgiving holidays. The rest, as they say, is history. 
The Guthrie Center is dedicated to all those around the world who believe that there is one truth and infinite ways to approach it. As the world becomes smaller we must find ways to embrace the spiritual journeys of those whose traditions are different, without abandoning our own. We must also seek ways to preserve our greatest cultural heritage and find ways to support one another in difficult times. The skills needed for a healthy future are different from the ones needed to preserve the past, and both are required to live in the moment.

What is
Holla Holler?
Our mission is simple: to bring artists and audiences closer together in a space built for deep listening and real connection. Limited to 40 VIP guests, our intimate live and streaming venue offers pristine sound, thoughtful curation, and performances that feel personal, powerful, and rare. This is live music the way it’s meant to be experienced.​

Built for the artist
At Holla Holler, everything we do is built for the artist and in service of the music. We take pride in creating a live experience that honors both the performer and the listener—where artists are supported, heard, and given the space to do their best work.
Qualifying touring artists can ask about our Stay & Play Songwriter Lodging, which includes:
Deeply discounted studio and practice space
A private Songwriter Suite
Lodging for the full band (up to 5 people)

Located in the historic Firehouse #21 in South Minneapolis' Longfellow neighborhood, The Hook & Ladder Event Center is a community-rooted, artist-driven venue built around three unique stages and a simple belief: live music brings people together. 
Home to The Theater, The Zen Arcade Stage & Lounge, and Under The Canopy, The Hook offers a diverse range of experiences, from intimate listening-room performances and canna-forward lounge events to full-production concerts and outdoor summer festivals. Whether you're discovering a new favorite artist, celebrating a milestone, attending a fundraiser, or dancing under the stars, The Hook is a place where connection happens. 
Operated by Nobool Presents, The Hook has been serving Minnesota's creative community since 2016 by providing performance opportunities, event space, and a platform for artists, organizers, nonprofits, and community leaders. As proud members of both NIVA (National Independent Venue Association) and MNIVA (Minnesota Independent Venue Association), we remain committed to supporting independent artists and preserving vibrant local culture. 
The Hook presents a wide variety of programming, including live music, comedy, film, dance, visual arts, literary events, culinary experiences, educational workshops, community gatherings, and multidisciplinary performances. We are dedicated to maintaining an inclusive, welcoming, and accessible environment for artists and audiences of all backgrounds, identities, ages, and abilities.

About
The Hybrid Gallery is a third space. 941 W. 3rd Avenue boasts a music venue, art gallery, and a space for creative expression in all its forms. It is a space for creatives by creatives, hosting workshops, local makers’ markets, performances, community focused open mics, and countless other opportunities to gather, foster creativity, and build and strengthen relationships. 
The Hybrid Gallery offers a queer and neurodivergent friendly container that is low barrier, inclusive, and accessible and fosters creative culture in Eugene.
To enter the Hybrid Gallery is to enter a place of possibility. Located in the historic Whiteaker district of Eugene, the Hybrid has existed for the last decade, serving as a venue, a gallery, events space, and a community hub. In the last couple years, the focus on the arts has been revitalized with new passion and vision. The arts team has invested in creating a safe space for queer and neurodivergent community, and the visual and performing arts. Curating art shows and open calls to artists of all experience levels. Supporting individual artists with guidance and coaching in sharing their work with the public. Providing networking opportunities between experienced professional artists and new to the scene creatives. Programming Support
There are many programming support funding opportunities available. 
These are avenues to support specific things you like to do or experience at the Hybrid such as:
Arts + Creativity (Arts Workshops, Art Shows, Opening Receptions, Artist Talks)
Performing Arts (Dance, Theater, Comedy)
Storytelling, Books + Writing (Eugene Poetry Club and Rainbow Writers’ Guild)
Music (Musician Meetups, Live Music, Open Mics, Jams)
Community Focused Gatherings (such as Book Clubs, Peer Support Groups, Dances, Trivia)
Mind + Body + Spirit Offerings (such as Liminal Space, Yoga, Meditation, Herbalism, Sound Baths)
Local Artisan in our Retail Space (sell your goods and help pay for staffing)
There are two other tiers currently available:
Free Popcorn Tier $10 per month, self explanatory
Open Mic Regular $25 per month - your contribution to help us put on our Community Open Mic
Scholarship Fund
We are planning to launch a non-profit arm of the Hybrid in early 2027 that will help fund scholarships. This will enable us to provide low barrier access as well as pay instructors and facilitators fairly.
Most of our offerings are low cost, accessible, and/or sliding scale. What is sliding scale? Sliding scale is a tool used to offer access to goods and services at multiple price points so that financial resourcing is not the deciding factor for access. Live house (ライブハウス) is a small live music club concept which originated in Japan. The term is a Japanese coinage (wasei eigo) and is mainly used in China, Japan and South Korea. It most frequently refers to smaller venues, which may double as restaurants or bars, especially featuring rock, jazz, blues, and folk music. 
American livehouse - an East Asian concept, tailored to the tastes of a discerning western audience. A new incarnation for a venerable Philadelphia night spot. Jamey's House of Music is a multi-award winning, superbly crafted intimate 60 seat venue for live music set in a comfortable, air conditioned, world class listening room, with a taproom and full-service restaurant. JHOM boasts sound, lighting, video and musical equipment worth over a quarter million dollars - second to none in a venue this size! Take a look here to read about our recent upgrades and renovations, including the extensive mitigation measures to keep you safe while you enjoy your night out. 
Our stunning sound and intimate layout means guests will enjoy live performance in a whole new way. The space in the main room has stage-facing cushioned seating for 32 at long tables and 2-tops, a comfy "producer's" couch for 3, with a stage-side dining/listening "Little Gallery" with a video monitor focused on the stage, with cushioned seating for an additional 24. The gallery features a monthly rolling exhibit of music related paintings and photography produced by fine artists or musicians who are also artists. The bar area has seating for an additional 9, and another video monitor. We have been called "the best small venue on the east coast", "a listening room on steroids" and "a music lover's paradise." Utilizing a separate ticketing structure, we pay-per-view live stream all our shows in professional quality full HD stereo so performances may be watched remotely as they happen as well. This unique capability gives artists a world-wide reach for fans and audiences that are unable to attend in person.

We get many requests from agencies and independent touring groups that have no local fan base or name recognition and are unable to draw a reasonable audience, therefore we only book groups that can guarantee a minimum draw. This is a cooperative effort. We rely on you to work to bring people in the door, and we will provide you with a unparalleled place to perform your music. We look for openers to guarantee a 10 seat draw, co-bills to draw 20 each, headliners 30 seats, and solo acts to draw 40 minimum. If fewer than 10 tickets have been sold two days before the date of the performance, we reserve the right to cancel the show, and refund advance sales, although we rarely will do so. We do not often have opening acts for logistical reasons. If you are a booking agent, we expect that you will not overexpose your act in this market. For example, we recommend a 1 month/30 mile lockout when routing, unless it is a big name with big draw potential. Where Michigan Flavor
Meets Inspired Design. 
JDs Stage Bistro offers a distinctive setting for private gatherings of all kinds. With flexible spaces designed for both intimate celebrations and larger events, including the intimate wine cellar, the relaxed vinyl lounge, and The Newsroom, guests can host receptions, corporate gatherings, milestone celebrations, and community events in an environment that blends elevated dining with the spirit of live performance. Each space provides a unique backdrop, creating memorable experiences for every occasion.

A REFINED HIDEAWAY 
Vinyl Lounge 
The Vinyl Lounge is an intimate, design-forward space that pays tribute to the warmth and ritual of recorded music. Featuring state-of-the-art sound and thoughtfully considered acoustics, it is designed for unfiltered, pure vinyl listening – a testament to a commitment to music in its most authentic form. ~ capacity 20 
A Versatile Gathering Space 
The Newsroom 
The Newsroom is a distinctive private space shaped by storytelling and the exchange of ideas. Set within one of the venue’s unique vaulted rooms, the space is enhanced by chandeliers and adaptive lighting that create a dynamic yet refined atmosphere.
Designed for both professional and social gatherings, it accommodates everything from meetings and presentations to intimate dinners and celebrations. Paired with JDs culinary and service experience, The Newsroom offers a setting that feels both purposeful and memorable. ~ capacity 70 (seated)

Our Board of Directors
Kelly Music for Life, Inc. is a 501c3 non-profit, pure public charity.
Since 2012 the organization has been producing Music Festivals with Crafter Villages, Park Concerts, Dance Events and Art Exhibitions to benefit the community. The Kelly Center in Havertown, PA offers exciting programming including music genres such as Blues, Folk, Rock, Celtic, Comedy, Bluegrass, Open Jams and more. There is something for everyone to enjoy.
Kelly Music for Life serves Haverford Township and surrounding communities through its network of strategic partners, local community leadership, sponsors, the music industry, performers, local businesses and regional media outlets. Our events and fundraising festivals, concerts and music programs help to fund grants for the benefit of music education, child development, senior citizens and many community initiatives.

About 
The Kennett Flash is a nonprofit performing arts organization which operates a 100-seat, all-ages, stage-centered, BYOB, café-style listening room. The venue showcases professional national, regional, and local talent for diverse events in music, comedy, children’s programs, and more.
Mission
The Mission of the Kennett Flash is to be a leader in southern Chester County in providing diverse, quality performing arts programming and education that is accessible for all people.
Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion 
The Kennett Flash is committed to creating a warm, welcoming environment that celebrates the diversity of its staff, its audience, and its artists. We are committed to a healthy environment of mutual respect and appreciation for all humans regardless of age, race, sexuality, or gender identity, with the goal of creating a more inclusive space that represents all the members of our wonderful community.
Ticket Sales
The Kennett Flash sells tickets exclusively through Etix.com or at the door. We do NOT use any third party sites. These are scams and scalpers.

Membership FAQs 
 Why is the Kennett Flash doing this now?
The Kennett Flash, like many other non-profit organizations, needs to diversify its revenue stream beyond ticket sales and donations. Your contribution provides the Flash with additional financial stability, allowing us to focus our attention on growing our audience, booking a wider variety of performances, and improving our outreach to the underserved populations in the Kennett Square community.
As a member of The Kennett Flash, you will join a community of music lovers and arts supporters, enjoy benefits at each show you attend, and serve as an ambassador of local music and the arts.
 How does a membership help with the financial stability of the Kennett Flash?
Membership is a source of revenue to the Flash, in addition to ticket sales and corking fees. Members of the Flash will create a great network — helping to grow our audience.
 What is the time period for Memberships?
The time period is one year from activation. You can sign up anytime during the year, and you can renew after 12 months. You can also choose to upgrade your membership during renewal time. 
 Can I change (upgrade or downgrade) my membership during my membership year?
You can upgrade at any time, but you’ll have to wait for the membership renewal if you want to downgrade.

Frequently Asked Questions
 How do I book an event?
If you are a booking agent or want to perform on Kenyon Hall’s stage, please reach out to kenyon@kenyonhall.org. Kenyon Hall does not require radius clauses for touring acts and loves to get artists on as many stages as we can help with! We get many booking requests and may not be able to respond to all emails, but we will keep you in mind for the future.
Alternatively, you may contact us through our contact page with a subject line that includes the name of your act and proposed performance date.
For private rentals please refer to our Rentals Page for more information
 Where can I buy tickets?
You can buy tickets by selecting an event here. There, you can either reserve or purchase tickets through our ticket sales affiliate, donorbox. 
You may also purchase tickets on-site at the door—but with limited capacity, we may not be able to guarantee you a seat.
Kenyon Hall accepts cash, check, and all major credit cards including American Express.
 Where can I park?
Kenyon Hall is located on 35th Avenue Southwest, which is a busy arterial with restaurants on either side, making parking sometimes hectic. There is plenty of parking in the neighborhoods around Kenyon Hall that is safe, quiet, and easy to find.
If needed, there is an ADA-accessible ramp located at the back of the Hall with access from the alleyway off of Kenyon Avenue.
 Can I volunteer?
Definitely, we love our volunteers! Click here to learn more and sign a volunteer application form to begin volunteering now! Our general guidelines for volunteering are here.
 Do you still sell your famous rootbeer floats?
Yes, of course! Despite inflation, they remain at $2 per float.

We’re Listening isn’t just a 501(c)(3) organization; it’s a cultural experiment aimed at addressing one of the most insidious problems of our time: the loneliness epidemic. In an era where everyone is supposedly “connected,” more people than ever feel like they’re floating through life without an anchor. Enter We’re Listening, a nonprofit dedicated to combating this emotional isolation through the arts—specifically, original music, songwriting, and storytelling.
Operating under the name The Listening Booth, this isn’t just another venue at the Delaware beaches. It’s a place where melodies become a shared experience, where lyrics act as a salve for collective solitude, and where people aren’t just entertained—they’re seen. We’re Listening doesn’t simply aim to entertain; it seeks to remind us all that we’re part of something bigger, using the universal language of music to cut through the noise of modern disconnection.
“I think people who truly can live a life in music are telling the world, ‘You can have my love, you can have my smiles. Forget the bad parts, you don’t need them. Just take the music, the goodness, because it’s the very best, and it’s the part I give most willingly”
― George Harrison

MEET ME AT THE BOOTH
The Listening Booth isn't just a venue; it's a sanctuary for those who believe music is more than background noise. Nestled in Lewes, Delaware, this space is where melodies meet meaning, and every note tells a story.
​
Founded by Marissa Levy—a singer-songwriter who traded Brooklyn's bustle for Lewes' laid-back charm—the Booth emerged from a desire to fill the void in the local original music scene. Marissa's journey from sharing stages with Matt Nathanson and Lisa Loeb to creating a haven for artists and audiences alike is a testament to her commitment to authentic musical expression.
​
At The Listening Booth, the focus is on original music that resonates. It's a place where artists can share their narratives, and listeners can find a piece of themselves in the songs. With a capacity of about 100 seats, the venue offers an intimate setting that fosters genuine connections between performers and the audience.
​
The Booth's calendar is a curated mix of acoustic open mic nights, songwriting workshops, and performances by both emerging talents and seasoned musicians. It's a space where the community comes together to celebrate the art of storytelling through music.
​
For those looking to be part of this musical journey, The Listening Booth offers opportunities to support its mission. Whether through sponsorships, donations, or simply attending a show, every contribution helps keep the music playing and the community thriving.
​
In a world where music often feels commodified, The Listening Booth stands as a reminder of its true essence—a shared experience that connects us all.

Breedlove - 
The Jeff Bridges - Signature Model
With his new Breedlove signature model guitar, Academy Award®-winning actor, musician and environmentalist Jeff Bridges sings a song for unity, ecology and peace.
The instrument, which bears Bridges’ “All in this Together” motto inlaid along the fretboard, is made entirely from sustainable woods, proving, the acclaimed star says, that “we can build beautiful instruments to make beautiful music that will resonate for all time, and preserve the trees, the lungs of our planet, which temper our climate, replenish our fresh water and provide shelter for our wildlife.” 
“We can make our instruments with salvaged or selectively harvested wood from sustainable forests, while still protecting habitat and sustaining the local community. We can make a difference.”

 How it started
In 2019, Chris & Kim Pelonis opened Lost Chord Guitars in downtown Solvang. Part music venue, part art and photography gallery, and one part boutique guitar shop. 
It was our vision to create a space where people can experience the magic that happens when music is the conduit. 
 Chris is very involved in the design of many of the guitars available for purchase, putting heart into every step—from choosing and matching woods, specifying shapes, fingerboards, etc. 
He designed Lost Chord as a multi-media studio capable of multi-track recording and multi camera video shoots with a stage, seating, and a ton of vibe. The pristine acoustics and aesthetics at Lost Chord Guitars provide an environment like no other where everyone in the room becomes part of the band somehow!
