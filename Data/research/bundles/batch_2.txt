These guys are apostles of hillbilly boogie.

The High & Wides make exuberant string-band music, with one foot in bluegrass and the other happily wandering all over the musical landscape.With roots in Baltimore and Maryland’s rural Delmarva peninsula, the quartet draws on old-time, rockabilly, western swing, new wave, and more—imagining a world in which the string band never ceded popular music to electric guitars and drums.Marc Dykeman (guitar, vocals), Sam Guthridge (banjo, mandolin, vocals), Nate Grower (fiddle), and Mike Buccino (bass) bring fiery instrumentals, tight harmonies, original songs, and plenty of drive to music that feels at once deeply rooted and entirely their own.Learn more about The High & Wides!

Known around the world for his clawhammer banjo playing, Joe Newberry is also a powerful guitarist, singer, and award-winning songwriter. Creek House Concerts​
Creek House has become the desired listening room for many of the national touring acts out on the road today. Word has gotten out of the wonderful audiences that come to listen to these national musical treasures. Creek House has just upgraded its sound system and has asked sound director Chris Vnak to join us so that we can bring you the best music experience you will ever have! Because word gets out among the artists....Creek House has now become known as "The Carnegie Hall of House Concerts" in the United States. Thank you for your kind affirmation!
The musical mission at Creek House is to present the audience with artists playing folk, blues, country, western, roots, as well as comedians, storytellers...with a focus on nationally recognized songwriters that have created an extensive body of original works. Creek House director Glenn Elvig's desire is to have every artist leave feeling that they just played the best gig of their lives.
Shows are generally on Friday and Saturday nights. Some shows sneak up on Thursdays and Sundays. Saturday and Sunday shows have doors at 6:30. Thursday and Friday shows have 7:00 doors.
Located 20 minutes north of both Saint Paul and Minneapolis, Creek House is easy to get to... even die-hard bicyclists join us year round, with indoor parking facilities for these two wheeled friends. Concerts are usually once a month (October - June) with a break over the summer months. Shows are by reservation only! 
Because this is a house concert series we ask that you become a subscriber for more details and directions.
​Also, because we are a house concert series in a residential neighborhood....please be most respectful of our wonderful neighbors. You may meet them at the concerts....they are the best!
We usually fill every show, so please reserve early! Please plan to join us!
​We recently lost a dear friend...Mr. Lonnie Knight. Not only was Lonnie a best friend, he was a Minnesota Music Hall of Fame musician...guitarist extraordinaire and singer/songwriter...Lonnie was THE artist that inspired the creation of Creek House. Lonnie was also the creator and webmaster for creekhouseconcerts.com. The series will continue in his honor. We miss you dear friend.
On January 13, 2018, we formally dedicated the Richard Howard O'Brien Stage and the Lonnie Knight Theater at Creek House.
This website is dedicated to my dear friend Kay Nelson of Healdsburg, CA for her development of this site! Kay passed away from this world...her light and humor and love continue to guide.

 Giulia Millanta
April 11, 2027 3 pm
Singer-songwriter, Giulia (Julia) Millanta, is a native-born Italian from Florence who now calls Austin, Texas home. A creative and prolific artist, she has released seven solo albums, touring nationally throughout the USA and internationally. An accomplished guitarist, Giulia also plays ukulele and sings in four languages. She has been called smart, pensive and cool and credited with psychedelic grooveability whilst "baring her clairvoyant soul" to "deliver musical mojo." Giulia began her life in music as a child of eight years when taught to play guitar by her father she began to perform traditional folk songs. She continued to sing and began writing songs and making records. Performing at the Acoustic Guitar Meeting in Sarzana in the spring of 2010 her accomplished guitar style and songs earned her the "New Sounds Of Acoustic Music" award. This led to an endorsement by the renowned guitar makers Eko, choice of the most famous 60's-80's era singer-songwriters throughout Italy. In the past few years Giulia has performed in some of the best rooms in the US and Europe such as: The Kessler Theatre in Dallas, TX (opening for 10,000 Maniacs), The Heights Theatre in Houston, TX (opening for 10,000 Maniacs and Squirrel Nut Zippers), The 04 Center in Austin, TX, Club Passim in Cambridge, MA, Tin Angel, Philadelphia PA, An Die Musik, Baltimore, DE, Rockwood Music Hall, NYC, The Blue Door, Oklahoma City, OK, The Mucky Duck, Houston, TX, Opera House, Telluride, CO, Walnut Room, Denver, CO, In the Woods, Leusden, NL, De Harmonie, Edam, NL, Mandy's Lounge, Homburg, DE, Teatro Romano di Fiesole, Florence, IT and more... 
About Our House Concerts
Music has been part of human life since time immemorial. Before the technology of recorded sound, music was shared only in a live setting. Audiences listened to the music, not to each other. Today, house concerts are growing across America and the world as an age old method of providing a venue for performing artists where invited patrons can donate directly to the artist with 100% of their donations going to the artist. Creekbottom Music House Concerts is proud to be a part of this network and honored to be among the patrons of our performing musicians. 
We have a small private venue, our home studio room with a small stage and seating for 40, ensuring an intimate acoustic experience with the artist. Concerts are private, by invitation only, and reservations are required. Our artists are generally singer/songwriters performing their own original music and will have CDs and other merchandise available for purchase. We feature only the musicians whom we personally have enjoyed performing live and believe our audiences will enjoy them too. No alcohol will be offered, however, if you prefer to BYOB, you may do so. 
How to Attend a Concert
Our concerts are private events. Invitations and reservation instructions are sent to those folks on our email list for the periodic "Newsletter and Invitation". To get on the email list for the newsletter, please send an email to cmhc@cm-hc.org asking to be added. If the concert is not sold out, you can make reservations from the "Reserve Now" link in the email newsletter.

You drive down a dirt road and turn in to Deb’s long driveway that drives thru a field, and you instantly feel welcome. You will park alongside the driveway and enter the house along with up to 50 other people for a pot luck dinner one hour before the concert. You will be greeted by friendly warm people, some regulars and many new to the concept but all there to appreciate some good fine home cooking and the music in a warm loving home that provides the musicians with a listening room full of music lovers. It’s all about the music that brings these fine folks all together for an evening of entertainment.
House concerts bring people together for the love of the music in an intimate, relaxed and self-serve, friendly environment, in a listening room, while always remembering you are in someone’s home and will treat it as if it were your own. These concerts are held in the spring and fall. If the weather is lovely, most folks enjoy eating out doors, roaming around the yard and gardens and some walk down to the stream. Dear Friend,
I first became aware of Peterborough Folk Music nearly 25 years ago when, as regular attendees of many Boston-area folk venues and coffeehouses, we would see our favorite artists' upcoming shows in Peterborough, NH, and think, “We wish it were closer – they have some awesome shows!”
Five years ago and two moves later, we landed in PFM’s backyard. Many factors came into play in deciding on the Monanock Region for our new home, but don’t think for a moment that there wasn’t a twinge of excitement about the possibility of culture and folk music right nearby.
After attending several shows, I offered my services – moving chairs or whatever was needed. I didn’t hesitate when Deb called this spring to ask if I’d be interested in volunteering at the door, nor did I hesitate not long after when she asked if I would join the board.
Peterborough Folk Music is a hidden gem in a quiet corner of New Hampshire. There are few rural areas where you can see established legends like Tom Rush, Chris Smither, or Iris DeMent; perennial favorites Rani Arbo & daisy mayhem, Catie Curtis, or Peter Mulvey; or rising indie-folk artists like Low Lily, Antje Duvekot, and Seth Glier. That level of live music in a small community doesn't happen by accident.

PFMS Board Members: Diane Goodman, Jim Van Valkenburgh, Yvonne Gifford, Kate Coon, Bill Goodwin, and Deb McWethy
The Peterborough Folk Music evolved out of the well-known Folkway, a wonderful coffee house founded in 1975 and unfortunately permanently closed its doors on January 1, 1996. At that time Deb McWethy, a board member, announced her vision to keep the Folkway's legacy alive and to continue bringing folk music to the Monadnock Region. The new Society made up of some Folkway members, got busy acquiring a non-profit status, looking for new board members, corporate sponsors, web and graphic designers, volunteers, and looking for a good place to hold folk concerts.

Thanks to another generous grant from Denver Arts & Venues, we were thrilled to present the 2nd Ever Five Points Live! Jazz & Roots Celebration again this year on Saturday, October 10. Tickets were just $15, and the show sold out. When our original headliner was unable to travel, we tapped drummer Jaik Hakkarinen to lead his band through a hot set of jazz. Before them, Frontier Ruckus delivered tunes from their 7 albums, and local singer Dr. Linda Theus-Lee opened up the show. Laurie Dameron's Jazz Duo played on the mezzanine between sets, and Maiz Foodtruck and Thick's Gelato fed the crowd.
Five Points Live Jazz & Roots Celebration is grateful to Kawai Pianos' Denver Showroom for the instrument played at this concert.
Five Points Live Jazz & Roots Celebration is proud to be a KGNU Presents concert, endorsed by KGNU Community Radio.

Maggie & Doug's Suggestions for Grabbing a Bite to Eat and a Drink Before a Show 
Our house concert venue is located near many great places to eat and drink, and we're often asked to offer recommendations for where folks can grab a bite before a show. These are some of our favorites that are closest to home: (Click on the name of each establishment for more info:)
Famous Original J's Pizza & 715 Club - walk-up window for slices, beverages next-door, outdoor seating, at the 5 Points. 
Taco Uprising - new favorite on Welton Street, the birria taco is fabulous and great cocteles complete a meal.
​Work & Class - Lively, yummy.
Cart - Driver - next to W&C but not affiliated; a quirky favorite, we enjoy the oysters, pizzas, and negronis.
Xiquita - highly anticipated new restaurant bringing the vibrant flavors and culture of Mexico City to the Mile High City.
Las Delicias + La Pasadita Inn - a couple of nearby, traditional Mexican restaurants.
Walters 303 - our go-to pizza, plus wings, salads, calzones, cocktails and robust beer list in a sit-down Uptown location.
​Campfire - focus on Colorado-sourced ingredients, beers, and spirits make this a good pre-concert option.
Coperta - Southern Italian fare from an accomplished chef in a warm setting with a full bar.
Yazoo BBQ - slow cooked Memphis Style BBQ and a rooftop bar.
Uchi - this Austin, TX, export is the closest sushi to our place. It's a unique approach to the cuisine, and rather high end.

Welcome to Folk ‘N Great Music 
Welcome to Folk ‘N Great Music house concerts in Rockville, Maryland, where you can enjoy talented singer/songwriters in an intimate, un-amplified, convenient, smoke-free listening room.
Since 2003, Folk ‘N Great Music has been welcoming neighbors and colleagues to enjoy national touring singer/songwriters. 100% of the suggested per person minimum donation goes directly to the performers. In addition, at many shows, we collect items like canned food, cleaning products, school supplies, or winter coats to support various local nonprofits.
Folk ‘N Great Music is proud to have been the very first unionized house concert series in the country with a Collective Bargaining Agreement with Local 1000’s American Federation of Musicians. You can learn more about it here.

We host compact concerts with remarkable musicians in our living room three or four times a year.
100%… 
…of the proceeds of concerts at our house go to the artists. Many outstanding musicians are working really hard to keep making music. We want to support them, get to know them, and keep them playing, singing, and writing songs.
Come join us and discover music and artists you might not encounter otherwise. Think of it as escaping the algorithm and feeling the music in real life.

 Vocal Technique and Harmony Workshop
Vocal Technique and Harmony Workshop
November 3, 2026 7:30 pm – 8:30 pm
In this monthly workshop hosted by Nick Cameron, you will learn the fundamentals of proper vocal technique and practice singing in harmony with others. Each class will focus on different vocal exercises and aspects of vocal technique, including breath control, diaphragmatic support, upper and lower register, vocal placement and blending, vocal agility, engaging vocal performance, and more. Additionally, in each class you will learn a folk song to practice singing harmony together. Whether you are new to singing or a seasoned chorister, there is something for you to learn from this class!

 Concert – Rita Bliss
Concert – Rita Bliss
November 6, 2026 7:30 pm – 9:30 pm
Rita Bliss is an Austin-based folk musician who captures moments, memories, and conversations in song. A Rust Belt native, her melodies are rooted in the old-time banjo sounds of Appalachia and sung with a vaguely upstate-New York accent. From the open landscapes of Texas to the bitter winters of the Northeast, Rita has collected truth, fiction, and many musical influences along the way. Queer love stories, painful goodbyes, and rowdy gatherings unfold in her songs with sincerity and adopted Southern charm.

About
The Frog Pond at Blue Moon Farm in Silverhill, Alabama is a private outdoor house concert in a private home. We are a Pro Member of The Folk Alliance International and many of the country’s best singer songwriters and musicians have graced our humble little “front porch”.
The venue is as distinctive as the performers. The Frog Pond itself, is an outdoor stage under a 200 year old Cedar tree on Blue Moon Farm, a property owned by Cathe Steele, who formerly managed the entertainment at Pirates Cove in Josephine, Alabama. Cathe, and her songwriter friend Grayson Capps, took inspiration for the Frog Pond house concerts series from the famed “Midnight Rambles” conducted by drummer Levon Helm. As she describes it, the core idea is to bring musicians and serious listeners together in an informal but respectful house concert setting on the farm where the focus is firmly on the music and community built through artists and listeners. It’s a magical place where music is brought back to it’s roots, in a true listening atmosphere where songwriters and musicians alike come together to “wood shed” and network. The Frog Pond offers each artist the opportunity to play with others that they would not normally have the opportunity to perform with, while sharing fan bases & introducing their music to new ears within an intimate venue of true music lovers. We are a not for profit house concert with original music by the performers. All proceeds go to our songwriters.
At Blue Moon Farm, patrons bring their own chairs and beverages and are encouraged to participate in a communal covered-dish dinner. Overnight camping is allowed for out of towners. The house concerts are intimate, unique, and you must be on the guest list to attend as it is not open to the public. Please feel free to contact us for details and an invite to experience the musical magic and community of The Frog Pond at Blue Moon Farm.

Fulshear House Concerts
Our House Concerts are hosted in private homes and considered listening rooms during the sets. In between sets you can mix, mingle and purchase merchandise. Your RSVP is your commitment to attend and donate $30 per person for a great evening of music, friends and fun. *Some shows are priced higher.
Each concert features a quality live music performance by professional musicians funded through donations from supporters in attendance. House concerts have become a primary source of sustainability for many musicians in today's changing digital music environment, and they love the intimate performance setting found only in living rooms. The donation amount is established by the artist, and 100% of all donations go directly to them. 
​
​
Intimate Concert Setting
House Concerts are a laid back venue -- shows are held in our family room with the furniture cleared out the day of the show. Doors open 30 minutes before each show for us to socialize, share food, and mingle with each other and the artists before the music starts. Address and food details are shared with committed attendees prior to each show, and food is pot-luck -- bring a dish to share and we'll provide a chair! There are generally two 45 minutes sets while we are in "listening room" mode for the performance, and there's a break in the middle to refresh beverages, use restrooms, reconnect with artist or buy cd's and other merchandise. You may not recognize the names of all of our performers, but when you hear them, you will wonder why they are not "big stars." We hope they all will be some day, but for now, we're just happy they come play great shows in our living room. Come join us and see what it's all about!
​
Parking: You will be sent parking instructions with your invitation confirmation.

I'VE NEVER BEEN TO A HOUSE CONCERT BEFORE. EXACTLY WHAT DO YOU MEAN BY "HOUSE CONCERT?"
Although the simple answer to this question is "a concert at our house," in truth there's a lot more to it. Do you enjoy live music in a small, intimate setting? Do you enjoy meeting new friends who also share a similar love of live music performances? Do you enjoy LISTENING to the music while it's performed, rather than having other audience members annoy you with obnoxious chatter while you're trying to pay attention to the performance? Do you enjoy meeting the performer(s) and interacting with him/her/them to not only share what you liked about their performance, but perhaps even get to know them a bit better as people? If you've answered "YES" to these questions, then attending our house concerts is FOR YOU!

The Habitat is the private home of Ken and Heather Settlemyer who are passionate about the importance of nurturing both local plant and animal wildlife and independent musicians.
The concept of the House Concert is to provide a venue where musicians can come to be heard by those whose primary intention is to listen rather than to have music in the background. We will be inviting musicians to perform at The Habitat approximately once per month.
We are proud to maintain over 1/4 acre of wilderness within an urban environment, providing much needed habitat for a variety of native species. We plant only native species and manage or eliminate the non native species in that area. We are delighted to play host, not just to excellent musicians, but to a plethora of beautiful butterflies.
The Habitat is BYOE and a donation for the musicians. This is completely not for profit, all donations go in entirety to the artists, some of whom will have traveled from out of state! Food to share is always welcomed, but not required.
We respect our neighbors and all noise ordinances and time the concerts in consideration of both. We sincerely hope you join us when you are able.
You are also welcome to stop by and ask Ken for a tour, he will be proud to show you our work in progress as we make a miniature wildlife preserve in the middle of Bradenton, a haven for bees, birds, bats and butterflies!

WE HOST PRIVATE INVITATION HOUSE CONCERTS, FEATURING ARTISTS WE LOVE!
CAPACITY: We host shows in a variety of spaces. Seated capacity is generally 50 to 100. 
QUALITY PA: QSC, K12 speakers for mains. Four additional speakers for monitors.
The Who, What, Why and Where of Seymour House Concerts
Who: Professional, nationally touring musicians… playing heartfelt, original music. Over the past ten years, we have hosted Jason Eady, Birds of Chicago, Charles Wesley Godwin, Sierra Ferrell, The Steel Wheels, Rayland Baxter, Steve Poltz, Cris Jacobs, Will Kimbrough, Gabe Lee, Grayson Capps, Eilen Jewell, The Ballroom Thieves, Andrew Duhon, Charley Crockett, Lindsay Lou and many more great musicians.
What: An awesome concert experience. Every seat is like a front row spot. And because it’s a casual atmosphere, the artists tend to share stories between songs. You’ll hear great music, and you’ll likely enjoy a few laughs too. There’s nothing else quite like it. 
My brother and I traveled to a three-day Songwriters Festival in January 2015. We were unfamiliar with almost all of the musicians, and I was concerned that we might grow tired of the ‘guy with a guitar’ presentation. Boy, was I wrong. I have been to hundreds of concerts over the past 20 years, and the songwriter’s event was so unique and laid back, we couldn’t get enough. When I found out we could create a similar experience right here in Seymour, I had to try it. 
Why: I LOVE music... especially live, original music. But, to see acts of this caliber, I typically have to drive to Indianapolis, Louisville, Bloomington, or Cincinnati. And, I do so frequently. That’s at least an hour’s drive any way you slice it. So, when I found out about house concerts, I invested in a quality sound system and starting inviting some of my favorite artists to play for us in Seymour. 
Where: Our shows are private, and we have hosted performances at eight different spaces around Seymour - basements, backyards, sunrooms, and more. Take a look at some of the photos...

Concerts in the barn happen in spring and fall, and we plan for four concerts every year. We’ve been working with the LRN/Concerts In Your Home network to connect with artists, and we are listed as a house concert venue on their website: https://my.listeningroomnetwork.com. We are also listed with Undiscovered Music. 
We like to think of ourselves as a venue and respite for traveling musicians. Concerts are intimate “listening room” performances before an attentive and enthusiastic audience, two sets with an intermission for socializing and merch sales.
Concerts happen from early May to early June and early September to early October.
The unheated barn is cold in the winter, so there are no concerts November through April. 
And it can get mighty hot during a mid-summer heat spell, so we avoid scheduling concerts in July and August.

about heber city limits
Hi! I’m Alicia. I’m a singer-songwriter and touring folk musician. I’ve performed at venues across the country, and some of my absolute favorite shows I’ve ever played were House Concerts. After playing in people’s living rooms and backyards for small but passionate audiences, I came home to find the Wasatch Back was missing out on this concept. I was determined to pay forward the kindnesses of hosts who helped me launch my career. I wanted to create a space for music community in my hometown and thus Heber City Limits was born.
What is a house concert?
Unlike traditional concerts where the focus might be on socializing or background noise, a house concert is a listening room—an environment where the music is meant to be experienced and appreciated with undivided attention.You'll find yourself surrounded by fellow music enthusiasts, all eager to soak in the sounds of talented artists up close and personal. Please be respectful of your neighbors and the performers.
This year, we’re introducing a free ticketing system to help us stay in touch with guests and better plan for each show. Tickets are free and reserve your spot. When you arrive, we’ll invite you to contribute a suggested donation to the artist.
Heber City Limits house concerts run on a “suggested donation” model. These donations go entirely to the artists to support their work—none of it goes to the host. It’s our way of making sure musicians are compensated and appreciated for sharing their songs.
some things to know
Please reserve a free ticket for each show you plan to attend. It helps me know how many folks to expect—and honestly, I like to know who I’m sharing my home address with.
Bring a chair. I have extras if you don’t have one or if you forget.
Don’t park like an A-hole. Please be respectful of my neighbors’ driveways and homes. There is ample street parking next to the park half a block away.
Cash or Venmo are king. Some artists accept credit cards, but cash and Venmo are safe bets. Don’t forget to bring a little extra for sweet merch.
FAQs
Do I need a ticket to attend?
Yes—please reserve a free ticket in advance for any show you plan to attend. The ticket acts as your RSVP, and once reserved, you’ll receive the house concert address via email. Tickets are free, and we’ll invite you to make a suggested donation to the artist at the show.
What if I change my plans?
No worries—plans change! If you’ve reserved a ticket and can no longer attend, just reply to your confirmation email or shoot us a quick message. This helps us open up your spot for someone else on the waitlist.
can i bring my fur children?
Please leave your fur babies at home. We have 3 fantastic dogs at the venue: Patty, Arnie and Kai who are easily bribed with ear scratches.
can i bring my human children?
All shows are all-ages friendly, however, there’s a lot of sitting still involved. We ask for a quiet and attentive listening environment. If your progeny is into that kind of thing, they’re welcome to join. 
what if it rains?
We have a backup location on deck in case of adverse weather. We’ll try to make the call by 5:00 day of show so we can move locations and get setup. Sign up for text alert

About Homegrown House Concerts
Hi! We love live music! That's the short story for why we do these house concerts. Our first house concert was in 2015 when we hosted Hayley Reardon, so we're celebrating our 11th year of hosting house concerts. Originally, it was an opportunity to support a young, local artist and we thought it sounded like fun - we had no idea what we were doing or that it would be the start of something, yet everyone had a good time...now we've built a stage for outside shows, we have lights, an email list, and even a name and a logo. It's been an organic evolution and it feels really good to have found a way to support artists while providing another music venue for our community in both our living room, backyard, and the garage. We think it's a win for everyone! 
If you want to learn more, please get in touch with us at homegrownhouseconcerts@gmail.com.
Warm wishes,
Leah & Mike

 About the Newton Cultural Alliance 
The Newton Cultural Alliance (NCA) is a membership organization and consortium of arts and culture organizations dedicated to promoting, enhancing, and sustaining art and culture in Newton, MA. NCA was incorporated in 2009 by a group of nonprofits that sought to promote arts and culture in the City of Newton. NCA was designed as a resource for the entire community which seeks to inspire and educate. Collectively, NCA members reach an estimated 60,000 patrons annually with their programs, concerts, performances, festivals, art installations, classes, and workshops.
In 2012, NCA purchased the Nathaniel Allen House—a Newton landmark on the National Register of Historic Places—and began the careful process of preservation and restoration for its future as a dynamic cultural center. Today, as NCA's premier facility, The Allen Center supports a range of innovative, cross-disciplinary programming and events, including exhibitions, performances, lectures, classes, workshops, and demonstrations, as well as private parties and events.

Develop collaborative cultural facilities for small nonprofit organizations and the community. Part of this effort is to ensure the permanence and vitality of the historic Nathaniel Allen House in West Newton and provide a year-round community space for a range of events and programs.

In Cahoots House Concerts 
The remarkable musicians, artists and truly American characters we have 
 met in our travels around the country inspired the idea for In Cahoots House Concerts, an ongoing series that funnels an eclectic stream of musicians off the road and through our home town of Manitou 
 Springs. We are constantly amazed at the level of talent we cross paths with and have dedicated ourselves to finding a way to present them to our audiences in Colorado. These are great artists still flying under the national radar that have moved us, and consequently inspired us, to assist in their promotion in Colorado. We believe they are all exceptional and unique artists worth your attention and the suggested donation. 
We�d like to thank Bob and Nikki Weizenecker, co-producers of this series, for opening their home in Manitou Springs and hosting these concerts. Their love of good music and gathering with friends has evolved into the perfect venue for us to share these unique musicians with both the Colorado Springs and Manitou Springs communities. 
Booking 
 Criteria: 
Chris and I do the booking for 
 In Cahoots House Concerts. We book four shows a year; one winter show and three summer shows. We also rotate artists back through every couple of years, which only leaves room for one or two new artists each year. Because we book so few shows one of our criteria for booking is to see the artist live. Most of our bookings happen organically as a result of artists we personally cross paths with.

 We do not book through solicitation.

The Story of
Jimmy's Roadhouse
Jimmy’s Roadhouse isn’t just a venue...
​
...it’s a celebration of music, laughter, and community, founded by Jimmy “Round Man” Kujala in 2017.
​
Jimmy poured his love of music and people into creating this special space, where independent musicians could share their talents and audiences could experience the magic of live performances up close. 
From the scrawled signatures of past performers on the door to the intimate 40-seat setting, every detail of the Roadhouse reflects Jimmy’s passion for music and connection.
​
Born on June 1, 1954, in Fox, Montana, (just a few miles from Red Lodge) Jimmy was a man whose life revolved around music. 
From his first paying gig at the age of nine at the Snag Bar in Red Lodge to decades spent playing with bands like Billy Waldo and the Flying Grizzlies, The Hound Dogs, with his son, Levi or his trio, Big Jim and the Twins, Jimmy was a storyteller, a music maker, and a joy-bringer.
​
His life adventures were as colorful as his music career—whether wheeling and dealing cars as “The Round Man With the Square Deal,” cooking up new business ideas, or cracking jokes. 
If he’d been paid a nickel for every laugh he caused, he’d have been a millionaire many times over. (And if we had a quarter for every time he used this saying WE would be millionaires!)
​
In the last decade of his life, Jimmy found a new passion: creating spaces for independent musicians to thrive. 
Through his internet radio station and the intimate music venue he ran with his wife, Lauri, and his friend, Greg Creasy, he gave countless artists a platform to share their talents.
​
After Jimmy’s passing, Lauri and Greg wanted to keep his dream alive. Together, they now run Jimmy’s Roadhouse, continuing his vision of a welcoming space for live music. 
​

James and Pam Johnson produce acoustic concerts in the folk, blues, jazz, soft rock, and Americana genres with original music by the singer-songwriter. Since 2010, we have had over 250 events with over 350 artists plus a few dogs on over ten different stages. We ran Thoreau Woods Coffeehouse in Huntsville, TX from 2010 - 2020. We are a smoke-free listening room. Suggested donation varies depending on artist but ranges from $20-35. We are non-profit and all money goes to the artists. We only take cash, but electronic payment information will be given to pay the artist directly. 
We are members of Folk Alliance International and are an Official House Concert. We are also a member of the Listening Room Network. 
Currently we have two venues: Johnson Studio House Concerts in our home in east Galveston, and Galveston Folk Family Concerts. 
The house concerts are private events and we only allow 25 people. To request attendance, email Pam at pjgalveston@gmail.com. Address will be given with acceptance. We open doors at 5:30, potluck at 6 pm, and concert at 7 pm. 
Galveston Folk Family Concerts is a public concert series held at the UU Fellowship of Galveston County at 502 Church St. in Galveston (enter on 5th Street). Doors open at 6 pm, and concert is at 7 pm. Refreshments will be available for donation, BYOB. We do not take reservations or presell tickets. 
We usually have one concert per month at either Galveston Folk Family Concerts OR Johnson Studio House Concerts.

FAQs & Details
What are house concerts?
House concerts are private events in the homes (or in this case on the decks) of music fans. Friends, neighbors, and acquaintances are invited to attend a two-set performance. The performer is paid via your donation. It’s a fun, intimate way to enjoy music.
How do I get an invitation?
Since these house concerts are private events, you must join our invitation list in order to receive an invitation to the shows. Note, space is super limited and the shows will fill up fast, so when you receive your invitation, be sure to reserve your seat right away. 
How long are the concerts?
Usually, the artist performs two 45-minute sets and we have a 20-30 minute intermission.
Doors open one hour prior to showtime for you to enjoy food & beverages and socialize with your fellow music lovers.
Shows begin promptly at the starting time, so please do not arrive late.
How much is the ticket donation?
It can vary, but generally, the ticket donation is around $40 per person, made in advance to reserve your seat. 3% per ticket is added to cover the credit card fee. If you would rather pay via cash or check, contact us. If tickets are still available, we will hold them for 5 days so you can get us your donation in advance. 
Note: You may also want to bring cash to purchase artist merchandise.
Why is the ticket donation non-refundable?

About Us
From the Ranch House to the Kelly House: Angela has always loved live music, especially in her own home! Her previous series, Ranch House Concerts, hosted its first concert in 2007 and hosted twenty-six shows from 2007-2011. The last show in the Ranch House series was Butch Hancock on September 9, 2011. What are the "ticket" prices? 
Kelly House Concerts doesn't sell actual tickets. Our “ticket” price is a suggested minimum amount to contribute to the artist for their performance. All that we collect goes directly to the performers. We have had people contribute more than the minimum in the past, and all of the extra money goes directly to the performers as well. Once you RSVP by email, we encourage you to pay for your “ticket” in advance. This gives us a more accurate count of how many are coming, and we don’t have to spend as much time worrying about the money on the night of the show. The email invitations include instructions on how to pay either by check or paypal. We keep a list of people who have RSVPed/paid at the door and will check you off as you arrive.
