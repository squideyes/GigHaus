EAST HAMPTON IS AN EXTRAORDINARY TOWN
One of the oldest and most historic in America, a famous colony for artists and writers,
a place of exceptional beauty, renowned in summer for its beaches and high end real estate
yet still a year round home to fishing and farming families, Native Americans and independently minded “ex-pats” from the city.
The LTV Archives reflects it all with more than 25,000 shows taped by and for our viewers since 1980 when the first show Save Our Beaches was logged into the library. We also collect vintage film on local life that pre-dates LTV, the earliest from 1927. 
In our collection, you can see the meticulous restoration of a windmill, visit the studios of dozens of well-known artists, witness haulseining and trap fishing by 10th-generation local fishermen, listen to talks by local historians and nationally acclaimed writers, hear the stories behind the landmarks and experience over thirty years of debate on the environmental and political hot topics of our changing town.

SUPPORT THE MAINSTAY
Attending live music performances is not just a recreational choice, especially when you have confidence in the music venue’s commitment to superior talent and appealing variety. These events are the golden threads that enrich the fabric of our lives and linger in our memories.
They help us maneuver through the stress and tedium that invariably comes our way in these challenging times. They provide the opportunities to connect with others on common ground, to feel safe and strong.
Can you imagine if circumstances hit the mute button and turned this special music off? The Mainstay has been part of people’s stories around here for decades. The generous support of our friends and visitors has kept the music playing even through difficult times. Please help us to meet these challenging times with good will, a strong back, and a stable financial footing.
Please consider a meaningful donation to our organization (“meaningful” is different for everyone). Donate online by clicking here or send us your check to P.O. Box 238, Rock Hall, MD 21661.

Mountain Top Music Center
At the heart of the Mount Washington Valley’s cultural landscape is Mountain Top Music Center, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization dedicated to enriching the community through exceptional music education and live performance.
Our History & Growth
Founded with a deep commitment to music education in the region, Mountain Top reached a historic milestone when it acquired the fire-damaged Bolduc Block in downtown Conway. Through an ambitious, multi-year fundraising and restoration effort, the organization rescued the landmark from its post-fire ruin and brought the historic building back to life. Today, that permanent home at 36 Main Street serves as a thriving cultural anchor for the community.
Our Family of Programs
Mountain Top proudly operates several key pillars under one roof:
The Majestic Theatre: Our historic, state-of-the-art 250-seat performing arts center, hosting major musical acts, touring artists, theatrical productions, and community events.
The Majestic Café: An intimate, vibrant street-level listening room and piano bar that hosts a popular weekly Friday night jazz series and serves as a lively gathering space during mainstage events.
Mountain Top Music School: Located on the second floor of our vibrant campus, our community music school offers comprehensive instruction, lessons, and ensemble programs for students of all ages and skill levels.
By uniting education, performance, and community hospitality under one roof, Mountain Top Music Center is committed to keeping the arts accessible, vibrant, and thriving right here in the White Mountains.

Majestic Café
UPCOMING SHOWS
The Majestic Café is an intimate 40-seat live music venue in the storefront beside the Majestic Theatre. The Café takes inspiration from 20th-century jazz clubs and hosts several live performances in a variety of styles every week. We serve an impressive selection of craft cocktails & mocktails, beer & wine, and dinner options including charcuterie & panini.
Reservations are recommended to guarantee your seat and to indicate a seating choice. Walk-ins are welcome, space allowing.
The Majestic Café prides itself as a dedicated listening room. We will never expect complete silence from our patrons for the duration of a performance, but we ask that you be considerate of the musicians and your fellow patrons – please keep the focus on the music!

Metamora Performing Arts Inc is an Indiana non-profit membership organization formed to produce and support various cultural events in the community of Metamora.
Metamora, Indiana's Canal Town, is an unincorporated village on US 52, about halfway between Indianapolis and Cincinnati. It is home to the Whitewater Canal State Historic Site managed by the Indiana State Museum.
MPA maintains a performance venue, The Opry Barn, in an old tomato cannery warehouse on the west edge of the village. Where US 52 crosses the Whitewater Canal and the Whitewater Valley Railroad. Music events, community theater, and other activities take place throughout the year.

Our Mission 
Our mission is to encourage, promote and support original music and the local artists that make it. Music brings people together and together we make a difference. It starts with a safe place for like-minded creative folks to connect. 
Through common interests, we find common ground. We learn how to become better neighbors and take better care of our neighborhoods. The Monarch brings people together to foster a stronger, more cohesive and vibrant creative community in Louisville and beyond. 
About us 
A PLACE WHERE WE CAN FIND EACH OTHER AND FIND OURSELVES​ 
The Monarch is a nonprofit creative hub, social club, and resource center that’s home to a community of artists, musicians and music lovers in Louisville, KY. Our coworking venue and listening room is a safe space where musicians can create, collaborate, perform, and grow. We offer writing, rehearsal, and performance space, communal tools of the trade, and assistance programs to support local artists and musicians.
Together, our artist members and patron members are building a stronger, more cohesive and vibrant creative community. Our physical space gives artist members informal, at-will key-card access to a comfortable environment, abundant with the creative energy inspired by the peers and patrons around them. Monarch members are connected by a mutual love for original music and the artists that create it. Our artist and patron members have access to some of the most magical musical experiences in town.
Louisville is home to an incredible amount of musical talent. The Monarch amplifies the sounds of our city by connecting artists with the audiences that make them grow.

Saturday, November 14, 2026, at 10 am to 3 pm

Georgian Harmony Shape Note Singing
A LETTER OF WELCOME from Michael Spencer (from earlier meetings):
"Please come and join us for a Shape Note Singing ... from 10 am until 3 pm. This annual singing is held the same Saturday each year, and all are invited to listen and to participate. It's community singing, singers take turns calling songs and leading from the center of the "hollow square." Bring food to share at "dinner on the grounds" at noon."
"I would remind anyone who would like to hear or join to enter the back of the room quietly and be respectful of the space. Feel free to find a loaner book and join in. Loaner books are usually laying on a table - Mike Spencer or another singer will likely notice and find you a book. There will be a large poster that will give you a quick orientation and will show which side of the square has what voices - tenor (men and women), alto (women), bass (men), and treble (men and women)." 
Today's singing will use The Georgian Harmony by Raymond C. Hamrick, published in hardback edition in 2010 and then as a second edition in 2012. Co-editor John Hollingsworth has a narrative of its creations at sacredharp.com. There is an annual "Georgian HarmonyAnniversary" sing in Georgia, not to be confused with today's event here. Older songbooks were originally published in the 1800's by Georgia composers - The Social Harp by John G. McCurry of Hart County and The Sacred Harp by B.F. White of Hamilton County. There are other organizations for singers using the various editions of The Christian Harmony and other songbooks.
The group sing today will be from 10:00 am to 3:00 pm with a potluck luncheon at noon (dinner on the grounds). All are welcome. This group frequently sings at No. 3 Railroad Street and is associated with atlantasacredharp.org. Questions about today can be addressed to Oscar McGuire at 478 955 6135 (text preferred) or Michael Spencer, fishman.mds at gmail dot com.

What is Nova
About Nova
Nova is a nonprofit arts space at 48 Emerald Street in downtown Keene. We book touring and local artists on the same bill, we host residencies that make art with the community instead of for it, and we keep the door open to people who can’t pay full price.
See what’s onSupport Nova
Mission
Nova Arts is a nonprofit, collaborative arts initiative focused on emerging artists, underrepresented voices, and unexpected juxtapositions. We present and partner with creative individuals, organizations, and ideas.
Nova presents, incubates, and promotes arts and culture. We make room for performances, arts education, and community collaborations that have a social impact, enhance culture, and encourage creative inquiry.
We’re interested in the combinations nobody else would try, which is how the Sun Ra Arkestra ended up on a bill with an experimental cellist, and how Yo La Tengo ended up here on a weeknight in September.
The room
Capacity. The room feels great with 20 people seated or 200 people standing.. Seating is limited and first come, first served.
Ages. Every show is all ages.
Tickets. Usually $20, general admission, with a name-your-price option on every night.
Artists. Paid fairly, and told what they’re getting before they load in.
Also here. Coffee, beer, Brewbakers, records, flowers, and more. Make a day of it.
What we run
Shows. Touring and local artists, most weeks of the year. See the calendar.
Community Collaborations. We partner with creative organizations and individuals to make an impact in our community.
The Thing in the Spring. The festival founded in Peterborough in 2008, which now calls Nova home.
SPAR(K). Our annual social practice artist residency, bringing socially engaged artists to Keene to make work with the community.
House rules
A safe space. Racism, sexism, homophobia, transphobia, xenophobia, or ableism will not be tolerated.

Join us at the Pocahontas County Opera House
We Are the Cultural Heart of the Community
The Pocahontas County Opera House is a 250 seat historic theatre located in Marlinton, West Virginia. The Pocahontas County Opera House Foundation puts on an annual performance series to bring in both local and international musicians, theater groups, and other performers to Pocahontas County, West Virginia.
THE JUDY CHOPS
SATURDAY, NOVEMBER 21 AT 7PM
Virginia’s The Judy Chops mix swing, blues, soul, rock and Americana with strong vocals and a lively horn section. The Shenandoah Valley band brings polished musicianship and a loose, celebratory energy to the stage.
 BUY TICKETS
BLACK MOUNTAIN BLUEGRASS BOYS
SATURDAY, DECEMBER 12 AT 7PM
A beloved Pocahontas County tradition returns with West Virginia’s longest-running bluegrass band, led by Richard Hefner. Hard-driving banjo, fiddle and mandolin, traditional harmonies and familiar faces make this December concert feel like a homecoming.
 BUY TICKETS

OUROS WITH DANNY KNICELY
SATURDAY, NOVEMBER 7 AT 7PM
Ouros and Virginia multi-instrumentalist Danny Knicely bring Appalachian and Latin American traditions together through acoustic playing, rich vocal harmonies and improvisation. Songs in Spanish and English connect flamenco, bluegrass, jazz and global roots.
 BUY TICKETS
AMR Bluegrass Sunday
Join us Sunday, October 25, from noon to 4 p.m. at the Pocahontas County Opera House for Bluegrass Sunday, a special fundraiser supporting Allegheny Mountain Radio! Enjoy live music from Mudhole Control, Viney Mountain Boys, and Richard Hefner & Friends. Admission is by free-will donation, so come enjoy some great music and support your local community radio station!
PRESSING STRINGS
SATURDAY, OCTOBER 24 AT 7PM
Annapolis trio Pressing Strings blends roots rock, blues, folk and soul with expressive vocals and three-part harmonies. Guitarist Jordan Sokel, bassist Nick Welker and drummer Justin Kruger bring a warm, groove-filled sound to the Opera House.
 BUY TICKETS
KELTON BOBLITS
SATURDAY, OCTOBER 10 AT 7PM
West Virginia pianist Kelton Boblits brings ragtime, blues, boogie-woogie and early jazz to the Opera House. Expect rolling rhythms and lively old-time piano from this two-time junior world champion.
 BUY TICKETS

A fast-paced mystery-comedy filled with mistaken identities, deception and unexpected twists. Performances were September 11, 12, and 13.

About the Lantern
The Quilcene Lantern is a proudly family-owned and operated business. Every piece of the property has been lovingly cared for with our own hands, and we can’t wait to share it with others. We look forward to building upon that legacy for many years to come.

Hidden in plain sight, the Rabbit Box offers an intimate experience for those in the know from within the heart of Seattle’s famous Pike Place Market. A treasured secret for locals, and a storied destination for travelers, the Rabbit Box serves as a gathering place and venue for storytellers, writers, poets, performers, comedians, and musicians to revel in the company of each other, explore new ideas, build community, and share their work—all the while enjoying simple but exquisite food, cocktails, and aperitifs.
Always open to the traveler but never pandering to tourism, the Rabbit Box serves as a window into the world of music, local art, and culture, while offering a welcome moment of reprieve from a bustling world or busy itinerary and as a connection point to home and friends abroad.
For all, the Rabbit Box is an invitation to pause, gather one’s thoughts, send a letter to a friend or loved one, imbibe, revive, and enjoy the moment of life that one is living. By resurrecting and recreating forgotten traditions and the little rituals of days past, the Rabbit Box serves as a reminder that the simple pleasures of life are ours to make, and that the power to bring beauty to the world and begin anew lies within us all.

About Eddie Owen Presents & Red Clay
Duluth, Georgia – Eddie Owen Presents is a songwriter's best friend, offering a music venue, music school, and a welcoming space for performing songwriters and their fans. We are located in Duluth, Georgia, at Red Clay, a 260-seat listening room that showcases some of the most talented musicians on the planet.
Music is a universal language that transcends boundaries. Songwriters and their stories traverse time, with lyrics that resonate with people from all walks of life. Nowadays, many people experience the magic of these artists only through their playlists. However, a renaissance of sorts is taking place in historic Duluth, attracting people from near and far. A significant contributor to this phenomenon is the live music at Red Clay, which has become the songwriter's best friend.
Downtown Duluth Georgia
At this music venue, an eclectic group of patrons regularly gathers to appreciate the displayed talent. Founder Eddie Owen often says, "Life is in the song," and he possesses a unique ability to spot emerging artists. He has played a crucial role in launching the careers of musicians such as John Mayer, the Indigo Girls, The Civil Wars, and Sugarland. His deep connection to the grassroots music scene and unmatched industry credibility set him apart.
Moreover, this music destination plays a vital role in the economic development plan for Duluth, which is the second-largest and fourth-oldest municipality in Gwinnett. Through his humble approach, Owen is reaching demographics that would not typically seek entertainment in Duluth, contributing to the vision of transforming this suburban oasis into a regional music Mecca.
Additionally, the lower level of Red Clay houses the newest location of the Gwinnett School of Music. In addition to offering private music lessons, we provide group and ensemble classroom instruction, as well as workshops.

ABOUT US
The creation of The Royal – Glenside’s newest 100-person capacity live music venue – was driven by life experience, daily struggles, and a desire to be inclusive. 
The COVID-19 global pandemic changed our world forever. Over the past few years, many cherished Glenside businesses, including The Blue Comet, The Bike Works, and others were forced to close their doors. The music community was especially hard-hit by the loss of music venues, not just in Glenside but throughout the Philadelphia area.
Our founders – Bridgett Gordon and Jerry Clarke – believe that Glenside and its neighboring communities needed a destination, a home for music, as we inched our way out of the pandemic.
Our vision is to provide a creative home where people of all backgrounds, music of all genres, and musicians of all experience levels feel welcome. We invite members of the community to come together and celebrate the local music and comedy scene, local artists, and local businesses. We want the musicians and comedians who play in our venue to feel they are participating in a “red carpet” event, whether they are novices just starting out or professionals who have toured for years. We want to treat musicians – and their listeners – like the royalty they are.
We chose the name “Royal” as our way of expressing the high status we believe artists deserve in our society. But by no means does the name suggest we expect lofty perfection. Quite the opposite. There is no expectation for being flawless. Some of the best music has come, and will always come, from an artist making a “mistake,” one that leads to a new and surprising path. We embrace that. Make mistakes. Make tons of mistakes - BUT learn from them.
As Miles Davis said, “Do not fear mistakes – there are none.”

Where are you located?
We are located at 1 South Easton Road in Glenside, PA. We are on the corner of Mt. Carmel Avenue and Easton Road. Entrance to the music venue is on Easton Road. Entrance to the event hall is 2278 Mt. Carmel Avenue. 
Where can I park?
Guests of The Royal are permitted to park in the parking lot of St. Paul's Lutheran Church. Enter the lot on Mt. Carmel Avenue, on the other side of Easton Road. Parking is available at the Glenside Train Station, but at your own risk as we are not associated or contracted with SEPTA. Street parking is also available.

FREQUENTLY ASKED PERFORMER QUESTIONS
How do I book a show at The Ruins?
Start by filling out our booking request form on this page. We review every submission and do our best to respond quickly—especially if your sound aligns with our vibe and community. 
What kinds of acts do you typically host?
We host a wide range: from indie rock and Americana to jazz, folk, experimental, and global music. We prioritize original music, high-energy live sets, and artists who bring something unique to the Gorge. Local and touring acts are both welcome.
What’s the venue capacity?
The outdoor courtyard can accommodate up to 400 standing guests and 220 seated. The indoor space (The Back Room) is more intimate, best suited for shows around 100–120 guests.
What kind of sound and tech support do you offer?
We provide a full PA system, sound technician, lighting, and backline basics for most shows. If you have specific tech needs, just let us know when you submit your inquiry and we’ll confirm what’s available.

The Sapphire Room Experience
Enjoy exceptional live music from your reserved table while dining from our full dinner and bar menu. Whether you're here to discover a new favorite artist or dance the night away, every visit is designed to bring people together through music. The diverse lineup features local and touring artists across a wide variety of genres. With seating for up to 150 guests and clear sight lines from every table, you’ll have a great view of the stage wherever you sit.

Sapphire Room Seating and Frequently Asked Questions.
Sapphire Room Seating
Tables 71-81 are located up a few steps. 
Tables 61-68 and 51-52 have high bar stool seats.
Tables 73-84 have booth seats.
The dance floor area(s) are down a few steps. 
Table positions may vary slightly from their positions on the map.
You will not be able to change tables at an event or add chairs to a table. If you’d like your group to sit together, tickets will need to be purchased at tables together. 
If you have tickets and would like to request a seat change, email music@riversideboise.com
 How do I purchase tickets? 
You can purchase tickets to all Sapphire Room shows on our website or by calling the the Riverside Hotel at 208-343-1871. On the ticking site, please click the button that says “Buy on Map” to select a specific table. 
 Can I choose my seats?
Yes, please choose the “Buy on Map” button to select your seats. Yellow seats are preferred pricing, blue seats are general admission, and gray seats are not available.
 Are all ages welcome?
The Sapphire Room is all-ages and family friendly. Every person that enters must have a ticket.
 Do you serve dinner in the Sapphire Room? 
The Sapphire Room offers a full dinner and bar menu with table-side service beginning when doors open and continuing throughout the show. Ticket price does not include food or beverage. Food and beverage purchase is optional and is a la cart.
 Is there a special rate when you stay overnight?

 Live Music Comedy Theater
Sellersville Theater is an intimate live music and comedy venue with 325 seats featuring national and international performers. Enjoy dinner next door before the show and stay overnight at the hotel after an entertaining evening of music or laughter. It’s the perfect date night or night out with friends. The venue is available to rent for weddings, presentations, meetings and special events and boasts an impressive live show schedule of five or more shows per week.
