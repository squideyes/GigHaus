About Lake Country House Concerts 
Rich Hancock has been hosting live music at his home since 2016. This is not a business. It’s a place where friends and guests can gather to support and enjoy live music. 
Each show has a suggested donation to attend, and 100% of all contributions go directly to the performers. We enjoy bringing in both regional and touring artists to our indoor and outdoor stage, and occasionally also partner with local friends to host shows in their barn venues. 
All shows are general admission and all ages. Indoor shows and barn shows have capacity limitations, so an RSVP is required in order to attend. RSVPs are encouraged for outdoor shows but are not required.
We encourage guests to BYOB. We do not provide or sell beverages.
New shows are announced on our website, on our Facebook page, and via email to our subscribers.

About Valley Fox 
Valley Fox is a Milwaukee-based folk power trio whose music lives somewhere between folk, bluegrass, country, and Americana. Featuring Laura Bomber on guitar, Joe Wais on mandolin, and Tori Yocum on upright bass, the group pairs gritty acoustic instrumentation with rich, haunting three-part harmonies.
There’s an intimacy to Valley Fox’s music that feels especially at home in a house concert setting. Their sound moves from delicate and reflective to driving and energetic, with thoughtfully crafted songs and vocal harmonies at the heart of it all.

About Us
Lakeside Music is located by Tuttle Creek Lake outside Manhattan, KS. We have presented house concerts featuring nationally and internationally touring musicians for several years and welcome artists of all genres. 
We host 8-10 shows/year, with audience numbers ranging from 20 to 40+. Some years we host more concerts, some years less. 
We suggest $25/person donation; 100% proceeds to the musicians. 
 Our goal is to fill our home with beautiful music by promoting some of the most wonderful musicians you may not have had the chance to hear before and to share them with all our friends--old and new. 
We'd love for you to join us!

 Lakeside Music is hosting a rare co-bill with two duos you won’t want to miss. Join us for this joy-filled evening of great music, great stories and great fun!
Tipps and Obermiller 
are Fort Worth folk duo Hilary Tipps and Steve Obermiller. This dynamic musical duo have captivated audiences around the country with their heartfelt songwriting and emotive performances. Their tight vocal and guitar harmonies, folksy wisdom and unabashed emotional vocabulary combine with craft and charisma to create this unexpectedly magnetic experience.
The Shandies 
are Shannon Stine and Natalie Wlodarczyk, a folk duo based in Springfield, Missouri. 
Both lifelong musicians, Shannon and Natalie are accomplished guitarists who create songs that comfortably wander between folk, Americana, pop, and rock influences. Natalie is also a board-certified music therapist who teaches courses in music therapy, music psychology, and guitar, while Shannon is a two-time national thumb-picking champion.

Welcome to Laurel House Concerts, Oceanside CA.
Supporting Local Music
 Hello! Joann and Brian Bourguignon here and we are part of a growing group of music lovers who open their homes to local and national artists. We do this because it’s a wonderful intimate atmosphere for us the audience, and for the artists. We look forward to seeing you at our next show. This is truly a community event- we enjoy bringing our neighbors together and share an evening of music, food, and good company. Tell your friends and neighbors to stop by our website and check us out. We always welcome new friends and new artists. (if you know someone who is talented pass our info along too).
Mission Statement:
“Laurel House Concerts is a nonprofit dedicated to enriching our community by providing an intimate, welcoming venue for musicians to share their art. We bring live music into the heart of the neighborhood, fostering connection, creativity, and cultural vibrancy for all.”
We are located in Oceanside CA. You will receive a ticket confirmation with the address after tickets are purchased.
To keep in the know, join our mailing list, follow us on Facebook and Instagram (laurel house concert series).

Happy Baby Band
(Steph Johnson and friends)
August 29th, 2026 @7:00pm
Steph Johnson is an award-winning recording artist, activist, and the co-founder and CEO of Voices of Our City Choir, an internationally recognized nonprofit that amplifies the voices of individuals experiencing homelessness through music. With a career spanning over two decades and five studio albums, Steph has been honored as a San Diego Music Award winner, a California Arts Champion, and a 2020 Woman of the Year. Her work has been featured on PBS, NPR, and America’s Got Talent, where the choir earned the Golden Buzzer and advanced to the semifinals. Through her leadership, Voices of Our City Choir continues to transform lives, using music as a powerful tool for connection, dignity, and change.
Steph’s new project, Happy Baby, is an electronic funk soul dream with key people from her musical journey that have inspired her evolutionary sound. What started as a 5 song demo, cowritten with Grammy Winning pianist and composer, Rachel Eckroth (St Vincent, Rufus Wainwright, Chris Botti) has expanded into a 12 song debut album from the group titled Cartwheel Galaxy, inspired from the actual Cartwheel Galaxy in our solar system. Steph hopes to travel together with you on August 29th for the debut full band concert.

about us
Ranch Vovo started in 2022 as a production company built for exactly one project: Morse Code, a scripted TV pilot and the first thing Randa Newman and Korby Lenker made together. We make the kind of things we’d want to stumble onto.

why do we host house concerts?
we believe in supporting live music and sharing good food. We believe in a world where musicians can perform in our local community and be able to make a living. Thank you for supporting live music on the Key Peninsula - it's all about the music and the love...
who are we?
richard and cheryl are two people who simply love live music and the magic that happens when it is performed by passionate and talented independent artists in an intimate setting... 
how do the artists get paid?
we simply ask those who attend to make donations - 100% of all money collected goes directly to the artists - the suggested donation is $25 and up per person...
what should I bring?
for our outdoor summer concerts bring lawn chairs or blankets - we provide seating for the indoor concerts. We provide bottled water but you are also welcome to bring a beverage of your choice or a dessert to share...
where are the concerts located?
our home is in Lakebay, Washington, on the beautiful Key Peninsula. The address will be sent to you in an email once you RSVP...
do I need to make a reservation in order to attend?
YES, please. Attendance is by invitation only. We would like to know who's coming and how many to expect. Our indoor listening room capacity is limited to about 50 people. Outdoors, we can accommodate larger groups...
where do we park?
like our name suggests, we are on a lonely road... the last house on a dead-end road... Simply find a spot to park along the dirt road and take the short walk in. Let us know if you have anyone with special needs...

About Lyons Den
We hear all the time: ‘House concert? What is a house concert?’
​
Simply put, house concerts are concerts in homes inside, outside, at the poolside, or anywhere with an intimate audience on private property. Typically, the host invites audience members, and the invitation list grows as new friends are made. The description of a house concert is a music experience with a professional musician at home. Most concerts are an hour and a half to two hours of music with an intermission. Suggested donations range from $10-$25 and 100% of proceeds go to the musician. 
The Lyons Den is not a business. It is our house. Adam and I love all types of music and want to introduce our friends to the musicians we have met. Our shows are normally outdoors in the sandy backyard therefore be prepared for the weather. Leashed pets are welcome when we are outside. On those occasions Mother Nature intervenes, we move the show indoors; please note we have three dogs and three cats if we move inside.
We do not sell anything, therefore, bring whatever you prefer to drink and a comfy chair and a smile. If a show falls over the dinner hour, we have a potluck and ask folks to bring a dish to share (optional to participate). 
All are welcome! Our audiences are friendly and like to meet new people. House concerts allow a deeper connection between the artist and the audience. The Lyons Den is a listening environment and we ask to show respect to the musician and fellow audience members during the show. Every show is different with storytelling, getting to know the artist, sing-alongs, spontaneous jams, and anything goes! 
​
Now, grab a chair, and a beverage and sit back to enjoy the show! ♪ ♫ ♫ ♩
~Karen & Adam Lyons

about macum creek concerts
We lived in Austin, Texas - widely known as "The Live Music Capital of the World" - for eight years, 2008 - 2015. During those years, we fell back in love with live music, attending nearly 350 live shows by over 200 performers, at venues large and small, at solo performances and marathons like SXSW.
At the suggestion of a friend, we attended our first house concert in 2013 and we were hooked. The idea of opening our home to other lovers of live music -- friends and soon-to-be friends -- immediately struck us, and we began thinking about how we could host our own house concert series when we retired and moved back to Maryland.
And so was born Macum Creek Concerts - a private, invitation-only, house concert series on Kent Island, where we can share our love of live Americana and blues music with friends, and where we hope to make a meaningful contribution to the support of live, independent music. We hope you'll join us.

how do macum creek house concerts work?
Last Update: July 19 2023
House concerts aren't new, and the concept is pretty simple. We're hosting concerts (about one a month) at our home on Kent Island, across the Chesapeake Bay from Annapolis, MD.
You get in by accepting our invitation. You get invited by being on our mailing list. This is the simplest, but most important, rule. Our house concerts are private events, not open to the public. You must be invited to attend, and to be invited, you must ask to be added to our mailing list (of course, you can also opt out of the mailing list at any time).
Our house concerts are shared experiences. We are contributing the use of our home, chairs to sit on, a sound system for our artists to use, and some light refreshments. We ask that each of our guests (and that includes us) make a donation to the artist. The minimum suggested amount may vary from one concert to another. These contributions are voluntary, and every penny of your contribution goes to the artists, to help them earn a reasonable living giving music lovers like us an opportunity to hear them perform live in an intimate setting - our living room or back yard. Greetings
 Welcome to our website. If you are here and reading this, the odds are pretty good that we just invited you to one of our private mansion concerts and music parties in Harrisburg, PA. Since 2015, these special gatherings have combined the world-class music played by our guitar-playing friends and mentors inside the River Room of The Manor On Front. The Manor is a beautiful old home in Harrisburg, PA, converted into a bed & breakfast. Polly and Tim stage these gatherings 6 times a year. All guests are pre-registered, and since seating is limited, walk-ins are not accepted. Only by joining our mailing list, meeting us in person, talking to us on the phone, or emailing us are you going to be added to our growing private invitation list. 
See more on page 28 of the June 2023 issue of TheBurg
 Mansion Concerts Inc. is a 501(c)3 non-profit performing arts organization dedicated to the promotion of instrumental guitar players and singer-songwriters whose main instrument is the guitar. Almost all of our guest performers are teachers in their own right, serving as further inspiration to guitarists and songwriters of all skill levels. 
 All Stars by Polly is now supporting these concerts with the sale of hand-folded origami stars as gifts or decorations. See Polly’s Facebook page here and more information here. And we have also begun our first fundraising campaign to support the series. You can learn more about this here.
 Stay tuned to this website to see who’s next, and please join our mailing list so that you are among the absolute first to know. These concerts are intimate, one-of-a-kind "guitar-centric" performances curated by our hosts. With under 100 seats available for any show, being on our email list is the difference between being invited or missing these shows completely. Join our growing tribe, please.
 These private gatherings are by invitation only, smoke-free, and date-friendly musical experiences staged in a most intimate setting. In this age of compressed, streamed, and diminished recorded music, these private gatherings provide a musical experience unavailable in most large or small venues. After the show, our artists stick around, sign CDs, answer questions, and have the chance to meet and greet the entire audience. Again, this is something you won't experience in a larger venue.
 Please check out our concert series, and if you like what you see, you know what to do. 
Thank you.

We hosted Teiku in 2019, and it is Joel's favorite of all our concerts. We are so excited to have them back! 
Chicago pianist Josh Harlow and Detroit percussionist Jonathan Barahal founded Teiku around the concept of using their respective families’ unique Passover melodies as conduits for new forms of spontaneous musical expression. They are joined by two stellar musicians, Jaribu Shahid (upright bass) and Jason Stein (bass clarinet). 
The result is powerful--think John Coltrane's "A Love Supreme" blended with Jewish music. They have been deepening and developing their music and their 2nd album, Klang, is coming out in June. 
Suggested donation is $25 per person, which all goes to the musicians.

About Us
We love great live music. We don’t love going to see shows at venues with bad sound, high volume, and noisy, rude audiences. Our house concerts have the opposite: great sound, reasonable volume, and fun, attentive audiences.
We have about 6 house concerts per year. Most of the artists who have performed fall somewhere in the Americana spectrum. Our tastes are more diverse and we are looking to host a wider variety of artists. We often like hard to describe music that bridges multiple genres. Favorites include Buddy Miller, Kevin Welch, Brigitte DeMeyer, John Hiatt, brown bird, Etta James, the David Wax Museum, Gillian Welch, Willie Mason, Mulatu Astatke, gypsy jazz, and the Art Ensemble of Chicago. We like lively music with a bounce to it, not gentle, nod inducing singer-songwriters.
The life of the touring musician is tough, especially when they go to cities where they haven’t established a fan base. That is why we prefer to host shows for musicians from outside of Northeast Ohio. This also lets us introduce Cleveland audiences to great musicians they may not have heard before.
As you may have guessed, the shows are at our home, which is a large 100-year-old diamond-in-the-rough. It has lovely woodwork, cool leaded glass, an old mural, and – most importantly – great acoustics. It also houses 3 dogs and a cat. It’s in Cleveland’s Ohio City neighborhood, close to I-90 and many great restaurants.
Mechanic Street House Concerts isn't a business. We don't make any money from these private, invitation only events. A donation is requested, 100% of which goes to the artists who have spent a lifetime of hard work honing their skills to provide the best possible entertainment for their audience.

House Concert 101
So just what is a House Concert?
A House Concert is simply a live musical performance hosted in someone’s home. It is the perfect way to experience amazingly talented independent artists in an up-close-and-personal setting. Here's an NPR story about house concerts and a Fresh Water Cleveland story about Mechanic Street House Concerts.
Mechanic Street House Concerts isn’t a business, and we don’t make any money from the performance. They are private, invitation only events. A donation is requested, 100% of which goes to the artists who have spent a lifetime of hard work honing their skills to provide the best possible entertainment for their audience.
 What to Expect 
Our concerts are non-smoking. There will be finger food. Feel free to bring your favorite beverage to share. There are usually two 40 minute sets, with chatting and chewing in between. Just come and plan on having a good time listening to great music, getting to know wonderful artists, and enjoying the company of new and old friends. Typically doors open at 7:30pm and the music starts at 8:00pm.
How to reserve a spot
RSVP by sending an email to mechanicstreetmusic@yahoo.com with your name and the names of up to three people who will be coming with you. If your group is larger than that, each person or couple should make their own reservation. Within 24 hours, we'll send you an email to confirm your seats. Then a day or two before the house concert, we'll send you an email with our address and other details.
What if I RSVP and then can't make it?
You'll never, ever get invited again! Kidding! Really, just kidding!
But please, consider your RSVP a firm commitment. Make sure everyone has it in their calendar, line up a babysitter, check out the artist's website, whatever else you do when planning a great evening out.

Scott and Paula Moore have been presenting their favorite acoustic singer-songwriters since November 1997. That’s more than 175 concerts in the family room, basement and backyard of their Maryland home. (Most concerts are also streamed live on Zoom.) 
Moore Music (In the House) is intimate and comfortable; audiences get to know brilliant, award-winning performers and hear their songs without the noise and distractions of more traditional venues. The house concert series draws artists from throughout the United States, Canada, Australia and the United Kingdom, usually after standout performances at Folk Alliance conferences, regional festivals, or other concerts.

Chuck Brodsky 
 November 28, 2026 
Chuck Brodsky is a storyteller, a songwriter, a troubadour, a modern day bard. His wit and irony, down-to-earth warmth, and quirky, rootsy, finely crafted songs, tell stories of oddball and underdog characters. His songs celebrate the goodness in people, the eccentric, the holy, the profound, the courageous, the inspiring, the beautiful. They poke fun at what needs poking, and sometimes challenge what needs to be challenged. They're sworn to tell the truth. 
For over three decades, Chuck has performed at festivals and in concerts all across the USA, Canada, Ireland, Denmark, England, Israel, Lithuania, Latvia, Wales, and the Shetland Islands of Scotland. He's performed three times at the National Baseball Hall of Fame, and 22 of his Baseball story songs have been enshrined in the Hall of Fame’s sound recording library. 
Born and raised in Philadelphia, a very young Chuck fell in love with the piano. Despite taking lessons, he still managed to teach himself how to play. Years later, on his first day of university orientation, he saw two guys playing guitars. He soon got one of his own, transferred out of the university and into the school of life. Influenced by Bob Dylan, Woody Guthrie, Lowell George, John Hartford, Jackson Browne, Bruce Springsteen, Greg Brown, The Rolling Stones, and Nic Jones, Chuck started writing songs in a style that is very much his own yet pays homage to the traditions. 
After hitchhiking to San Francisco and singing weekly at the Tattoo Rose Cafe’s open mic, Chuck spent a few years singing for tips on the streets of Europe and worked as a fruit picker back in the USA. In the late 1980's, he began performing in small coffeehouses around the San Francisco Bay Area. 
Chuck and his songs have appeared in several films and on tv. His beloved Philadelphia Phillies featured an interview with him as well as his song "Whitey & Harry" in the documentary Richie Ashburn: A Baseball Life. The 2003 Sony Pictures release Radio included a cameo by Chuck as well as his closing title track. "Moe Berg: The Song" is featured in the PBS film Jews and Baseball: An American Love Story (2010). Kathy Mattea's recording of his song "We Are Each Other's Angels" is in the film Dear Mr. Goodlife (1998). "Blow 'em Away" has been recorded by David Wilcox and many others, and also appears on the Christine Lavin produced Laugh Tracks. In April of 2019 Chuck was inducted into the Philadelphia Jewish Sports Hall Of Fame for his songs about Baseball.

 What is a House Concert, Anyway? 
Simply put - it's live acoustic music in a private living room with an audience of friends, both new and old, and great food that everyone brings to share. The host provides beverages. The music is outstanding - these are very talented musicians. Most tour professionally, usually nationally, some even internationally. 
They write their own music. They have CDs. They have great stories to tell. Lee Bowers books the talent and the venue, including her own home. She does this for FUN, for the joy of hearing live music in an intimate atmosphere, and to support independent artists. Every cent of your donation goes to the artist(s). 
 How we got started 
Back in the last century, 1997, to be exact, perennially traveling troubadour, Annie Wenz, was passing through Philadelphia. A friend of Annie's in Massachusetts knew Barb Ginsberg in Devon from a folk music list serve, called Barb, and suggested she put together a house concert for Annie. Barb had never heard of a house concert, but it sounded like fun, so she called a few neighbors and friends from work and the series was born. Barb held another sporadic concert or two, when local folkie Ray Naylor contacted her and offerred to help. They decided on three rules for selecting artists: Firstly they must both really like their music. Secondly, the artists must have good rapport with the audience. Finally, they must be "really nice people" - someone we'd want to invite into our homes. They decided on the name "Musical Lairs" and began to offer more house concerts on a semi regular basis. 
As the crowds got larger, a bigger venue was required, so Lee Bowers' home in Villanova was often used. The series prospered and grew until 2000, when Barb announced that she and her husband, Alan, were moving to Petaluma, CA.  About Us – Our Story 
We’re so glad to have returned to in-person shows as of December 4, 2022. We started off with a sold out show for our annual holiday concert featuring Terri Hendrix and Lloyd Maines. It had been a long time since we’d been together and everybody had a great time.
During Covid, we had some great Zoom shows and were able to present not only some great music but also get a larger audience and have an opportunity to interact with the performers and find out the stories behind their songs. We’ve had some truly amazing writers who have come from many different parts of the world. (Check out our Past Shows page) Each of these artists is on a mission when writing their songs and they are giving voice to all of us. It was a great opportunity to keep our music community together and also a tremendous way to help the musicians. 
NB House Concerts has been running since 2008 and in that time we have hosted many musicians from all over the U.S., Canada, and even New Zealand! NB House Concerts is a certified house concert under the Folk Alliance International PRO agreement. As such, it is considered a private function in a private home open by invitation only. However, I would be glad to extend an invitation to those people interested in attending. You must be invited to attend, and to be invited you must ask to be on our mailing list. (Of course, you may unsubscribe at any time.)
Generally, shows are once a month, on Sundays from 5-8 pm, from October through May. The shows consist of two sets of music with a potluck supper in-between. All the performers who play in my series also play at bigger venues as well but enjoy the intimate setting of a house concert. I also try to focus on bringing in known talent from other parts of the U.S. and even further afield. We have a lot of wonderful Texas musicians but you can see them at other venues. Tony Kamel 
April 12, 2026 
Tony Kamel appeared here in 2019 as the frontman and main songwriter for the Grammy-nominated group, Wood & Wire. Since then he has gone off on his own and has now released three records. His debut solo record Back Down Home saw him team up with legendary songwriter Bruce Robison and his production company, The Next Waltz. That album held a #1 spot on the Alt-Country Radio chart and was on the Americana Charts for several weeks. Produced jointly by Kamel and Robison, the new We’re All Gonna Live is Kamel’s third record for Blue Corn Music. 
His shows, whether they’re solo or with a full band, showcase his versatility as a multi-instrumentalist and compelling story teller – engaging with the audience, playing guitar and clawhammer banjo for a performance beyond some dude simply singing his songs. Whether it’s a raucous festival crowd, a listening room, or a jam session with musician friends, he finds a way to sync up with the folks he’s in the room with. I’m thrilled to welcome him back to NB House Concerts.

North Shore Point House Concerts 
 What is a House Concert? 
Just what it sounds like. We book a performer, set up chairs, sound, and lights, and put out a few snacks. It's an intimate, smoke-free evening of music. 
There are hundreds of house concerts hosted nationwide. Some are small, holding as few as a couple of dozen people; others, like ours, are larger and more ambitious. For many musicians today, house concerts are their lifeblood, especially with the closings of so many small acoustic clubs. Read about the first decade of hosting North Shore Point House Concerts and a recent article about the series. 
In 2017, we began a partnership with the Virginia Arts Festival and are now able to co-host shows in their venues. We also host shows at The Annex, a great place to see a show, and Vessel Craft Coffee, an intimate place to see a show. 
North Shore Point Acoustic became a non-profit in 2016. The shows are permitted under a Norfolk special events ordinance supporting organic cultural events. City permits are secured for each show. Donations are tax deductible. 
Since 2001, we've built a community of people who enjoy getting together to listen to great music they can't hear elsewhere in the area. Please join us. Thanks to everyone for your support.

About 
Where you can see great live music in an intimate setting in the Old North End in Colorado Springs, CO. We began in 2014 in Fort Collins, CO and relocated to the Springs in 2019. We're beyond excited to continue bringing amazing musicians to the unique experience of house concerts.
Imagine seeing great live music up close and personal. You, the musicians, and a small group of fellow music lovers in the house or backyard.
It's about the music, the connection between performer and listener--not about the crowds, the sticky beer floor, or people talking over the music.
Old Town House Concerts is a labor of love, not a business. All donations go the musicians. Our events are private and invitation-only. To get an invitation, contact us and we'll put you on our email list that gets all the news first. If space is available, we'll send you an invitation with further details.
OLD TOWN HOUSE CONCERTS
What's a House Concert? House concerts are how live music traditionally was heard. A group of people coming together in a private home to listen to artists play. It isn't a loud house party where the band is background. The music is front and center. It is a community gathering unlike any other.

Olive Street Presents, Ltd., (Est 2021) is a non-profit organization based in Pittsburg, KS. Our Mission is to be a catalyst for live music presentation and programing in the folk tradition. We support live music, community, and culture by creating safe, inclusive, and respectful environments for musicians and listening audiences. 
Since 2009, we've championed independent singer-songwriters who cross the country in the tradition of the American Troubadour. We create Talk-Free, Smoke-Free Listening Room experiences for national touring musicians who play for their living and for those who appreciate great live music. We've done this more than a hundred times and welcome collaboration with others to broaden, strengthen, and enrich the musical culture of our community. 
Why Do We Do it? 
Small towns are often passed over by national touring musicians in favor of larger venues, more convenient routing, and more potential revenue. Instead of leaving town to hear great musical performances, we aspire to support a live music culture in Pittsburg! Typically we open our doors for a house concert at 6 PM for a social hour, music starts at 7 PM and the evening winds down before 10 p.m. Performances are generally two 45 minute sets with a short break in between. At every concert, you can meet the musicians. Great food and friendship are part of the fun, so come early and share in our pot-luck meal and social hour before the show. 
Come to a Show! You'll see a donation button on the upcoming show page to make your donation. House concerts are private events and your donation is your reservation to attend. All donations, 100 percent go to the “artist in residence.” We limit seating to 36 chairs to ensure everyone has a great concert experience. 
What is FOLK Music? We define folk broadly as “the music of the people.” We embrace a diverse array of subgenres including, but not limited to, Appalachian, Americana, Blues, Bluegrass, Celtic, Cajun, Country, Global Roots, Old-Time, Singer-Songwriter, Traditional, Zydeco, and various fusions.
Support Our Work! We'd love to have you attend our shows, but if you can't, consider becoming a show sponsor, or gifting tickets to others is a great way to support our mission of bringing great live music to Pittsburg. National touring acts often require hotel lodging and meals, and such in-kind donations are also greatly appreciated. For employers, ask us about purchasing blocks of discounted tickets for use as employee rewards! 
Refunds: Reserved seating assures our musicians are rewarded for their expenses and efforts. If your plans change, please let us know ASAP because we sometimes have a stand-by list of folks who would like to attend. Empty chairs are a bummer and unless we can fill your seat, we generally don’t offer refunds and your donation goes to the musicians.

Built around the simple idea that live music is better when everyone is part of the experience, Over Yonder Concerts brings artists and audiences together in an intimate, welcoming setting. From the first note to the final encore, we’re here to create nights people remember.
We believe the best concert experiences aren’t always found in the biggest rooms. They happen when the crowd is connected, the artists can feel the energy in the room, and everyone shares the same moment.
Since 2016 we have brought talented musicians and appreciative audiences together for special and intimate performances. 
We host open-air concerts in the garden of our private home in Toledo, Ohio during the summer months and partner with a few other spots around town for an indoor season. These are concerts, rather than a parties, and it is important to make this distinction. Our shows provide a listening room atmosphere to both artists and audience members. 
Over Yonder Concert House is in no way a business. 100% of all donations and merchandise sales go directly to the artists, and help make this a viable option for traveling musicians. 
For most shows, we have a potluck beginning one hour before each show and invite all guests to join us with a dish to share. BYOB.

We are always excited when our buddy Jordan Tice makes it to Toledo! 
He’s making a quick run through the Midwest in December as the opening act for Sarah Jarosz, and we are thrilled to have him stop back through town on his way home to Nashville.
Jordan Tice is an award-winning guitarist and songwriter described by the Fretboard Journal as "quite simply, one of our favorite acoustic guitarists performing today." He has 7 projects to his name as a solo artist and 6 as a founding member of the string band, Hawktail. Equally virtuosic as a flatpicker and fingerstylist, and with a casual vocal style, Tice conjures ingredients from far-flung worlds with ease. His performances call upon the repertories of American and British Isles folk, bluegrass, blues and pop-oriented songs, and though his earlier releases are instrumental in focus, he has been carefully honing his craft as a singer-songwriter in his most recent work. About Pirates Cove Music
Pirates Cove Music house concerts are hosted by Paul and Annie Uselton at their home in Granbury, TX. Our backyard has been a hangout for local pirates and privateers for many years. In 2012, we discovered Trop Rock music and have been hosting outdoor house concerts ever since. 
We both retired in 2017, so we decided to pursue hosting with a passion. Because we know many Trop Rockers (and Rockettes), we built the Boat House stage and acquired the necessary sound equipment. We hope to enlarge the footprint of Trop Rock music in the North Central Texas area. We are southwest of the DFW metroplex, kinda in the country but still fairly civilized. 
We have a beach in our backyard and our massive oaks provide shade even on the hottest days. We hope you will join us for a concert soon.
Paul & Annie
We were thrilled to have an article about Pirates Cove Music in the Hood County News!!! 
About House Concerts
Although the concept is not new to Texas, house concerts are more prevalent in Florida. It is a simple formula. Gather some friends together, everyone pitches in for the music and they bring what they personally need (drink, food, chair) for the party. Very similar to what used to be known as “pasture” parties, but a little more refined.
The hosts arrange for a musical entertainer to put on a show at an arranged date and time. Invited friends (and friends of friends) come to the show and contribute a set donation amount to pay for the musician. The hosts make no profit from the donation. The requested donation varies with the artist, but these are professional musicians and songwriters from around the country and are worth every penny!

My husband, Dino Jobe, and I started this series to promote artists we love and to share their talents with you! We loved finding new music and having them play in our home. We also loved the community that House Concerts develop and the friendships that bloom within.
We lost Dino in July of 2025 but I have continued to put on these concerts ​and we are going strong!
If you love meeting new artists and having a chance to actually have a conversation or just the chance to snap a picture with them, this is the place you should be!
Come join us for an evening of pot luck, great people, and amazing music! 
Come casual and ready to be entertained and welcome to our family!
Leslie Cash Jobe

What is a Rawhide Trail House Concert?
House concerts are a wonderful alternative to commercial music venues like bars and restaurants. Hosted right in a private home and attended mostly by friends, neighbors, coworkers, and invited guests, our concerts offer an intimate setting ideal for listening, completely free of loud and rowdy crowds. Best of all, 100 percent of your donations go directly to the artist.

About Red Room House Concerts 
Red Room House Concerts are private, invitation-only events where music lovers enjoy performances by talented musicians in a smoke-free listening room setting. Red Room House Concerts is not a business. A donation is requested, 100% of which goes directly to the musicians we host in our Cleveland, OH home.
Performances generally consist of two 45-minute sets, with a short break in between. You’ll have the opportunity to talk with the musicians and purchase their CDs and other merchandise. Seating is limited and reservations are required.
