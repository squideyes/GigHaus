General Info & Ticketing 
 How Do I Buy Tickets?
Purchase Pre-Sale Tickets online at https://tickets.clubgreenroom.com. Day of show tickets may be purchased at the door. 
ALL ACCESS MEMBERS – Show your All Access member card at the door to receive complimentary entry and presale pricing for your guests. (Presale pricing for members available at the door only)
PRIME TIME MEMBERS – Show your Prime Time Member card at the door to receive presale pricing.
 How Do I Reserve A Table?
Tables may be reserved online up to 4pm the day of show at https://tickets.clubgreenroom.com. Unreserved tables are sold first come first served at the door. Reserved tables will be held until 15 minutes after the start of the show unless prior arrangements have been made to hold your table. 
 Where can I watch Livestreams?
Livestreams are broadcasted in full for all members on our website at https://tickets.clubgreenroom.com/music . We also show sneak peeks and occasional shows on our facebook, instagram, and youtube channels. @clubgreenroom

Everything worth knowing before a night in the trees. Still stuck? Reach out — we’re friendly.
Before You Come
How do I get tickets?
Head to our Shows page, pick your night, and hit Get Tickets — you’ll check out through Curtain Call, our ticketing partner. Buy ahead when you can; the good ones move.
Do my kids need a ticket?
Nope. Kids 12 and under always get in free — every show, every time.
Is it really all ages?
It is. Bring the whole crew. The only age line is the bar — that’s 21+ with a valid ID.
What does it cost to park?
Nothing. Parking is free and on-site.

Yes — pack a snack and bring it on in. There’s also a food truck (usually more than one) at every show, so come hungry

ABOUT THE VENUE.
What Makes This Room Different.
The Listening Room at Hamilton Studio is Spokane’s most intimate and acoustically rich venue—where music is truly heard. Housed in a beautifully restored 1900s auditorium with vintage charm and modern elegance, it offers a one-of-a-kind experience for both artists and audiences. Originally a film and photo studio, the space boasts near-perfect acoustics and cinematic ambiance. Every show is intentionally curated, from local legends to national talent, The Listening Room is where unforgettable performances and deep community connection come to life.

ABOUT US
Nestled in the heart of Spokane’s West Central neighborhood, the Listening Room at Hamilton Studio is a live music venue unlike any other; a place where sound, space, and story converge. We are a performance space born from a working film and photography studio, offering a concert experience that is acoustically rich, visually stunning, and deeply personal. We are The Room Where It Happens.
Dario Re, Heat Speak
"I love the way in which the layout (and name) sets a tone for deep listening. The aesthetic is gorgeous too, I love the lighting and intimate feel."
The building dates back to the early 1900s when it served as the auditorium for St. Joseph’s Catholic School. Though the school closed in 1969, many original features including the hardwood floors and stage remain, preserved through time. Listed on the Spokane Register of Historic Places, the space has long carried creative energy in its bones.

 We're a craft winery firmly committed to tradition--when we're not busy overturning it. 
Long before Hermit Woods Winery was a twinkle in anyone’s eye (or glass), three friends, a photographer, a geologist, and a pilot, would hang out.
Together, they’d bike, boat, and hike across the Lakes Region, all while experimenting with new ways to slake their thirst for craft beverages.
Years later, only one thing has changed. They’re founders and owners of an innovative craft winery. They drink for a living.
Before I became a vintner, I was a geologist. Terroir is way more than the geology but it begins there. The geology sets in motion the shape of the landscape – the soil, the water flow, the air flow – which in turn determines the flora and fauna.
You can taste our distinctive terroir in the fruits, flowers, and vegetables once the microbes have turned them into wine – with a little help from me.
 Photo: New Hampshire Historical Society 
When we founded our winery, it was illegal in New Hampshire to charge for tastings. So we sold wine glasses to our guests and gave the wine away for free.
We ruffled a few feathers but eventually the authorities saw the wisdom of our ways and changed the rules. Now I can tell customers at our tasting bar that it’s a wine experience so good, it used to be illegal.
 Photo: Library of Congress, https://www.loc.gov/ 
My roots here in the Granite State go back 400 years. Ethics and community are important to me.
We believe in saving the planet. We’ve installed solar panels in the winery buildings. We believe in being part of the community. We support local farmers, local arts, and local businesses. We believe in fair pay for all. We create local jobs with a living wage.
We’re also very, very supportive of the palate.

Getting Here: Metra, Parking & Transit
Address: Hey Nonny is located at 10 South Vail Avenue in downtown Arlington Heights across from Harmony Park.
‍Transit: Hey Nonny is two blocks from the Arlington Heights Metra station (Union Pacific Northwest Line), making it one of the most accessible live music venues from downtown Chicago. No car required.
‍Parking: The street in front of Hey Nonny is closed May - September for Arlington Al fresco. During the rest of the year, street parking is available and free. The pro move is to park in the parking garage immediately south of Hey Nonny. The garage offers free evening parking on the 1st, 4th, and 5th levels. Please read the signs closely!
Tickets
Purchasing: Tickets are available at HeyNonny.com or in-person at the host stand. The usual ticket service fee applies either way, so for your convenience, buying online in advance is the best move. We admit patrons to shows from our Will Call list. You just give the ticket buyer’s name; you don’t need a physical ticket for admission.

About Us
Founded in 2015, High Cotton Music Hall is the premier venue in Hartwell where talented musicians can perform for people who truly want to enjoy live music. As an intimate listening room, we cater to serious music lovers looking to experience nationally touring artists alongside regional and local musicians. Our programming features a diverse array of genres within American Roots Music, including Blues, Soul, Americana, Southern Rock. We are dedicated to providing the best experience for both the artists and the audience. Come out and enjoy a show at High Cotton Music Hall, savor the warm, eclectic ambiance that only a 100-year-old room can provide, and be prepared to be amazed. Beer, wine and non-alcoholic drinks available. Once you visit, you'll know that High Cotton Music Hall is WHERE MUSIC MATTERS!
Location:
Located on historic Depot Street in Downtown Hartwell, Georgia, High Cotton Music is a restored 100 -year-old former cotton warehouse. There are several walkable restaurants downtown are hotels are just a short drive away. Only 60 miles from Greenville, SC and 100 miles from Atlanta, GA.

About the Auditorium
The Historic Auditorium is a historic performing arts venue located in St. Croix Falls, WI, serving as a cultural and community hub for over a century. Built in 1916, it has hosted a wide array of activities, from silent films and civic events to live theatre and public gatherings. After years of adaptation and preservation, the building has undergone revitalization to continue its legacy as a centerpiece for arts and community engagement.

Who We Are
The Historic Auditorium is a cherished performing arts venue and cultural hub located in St. Croix Falls, WI. Guided by a dedicated group of community members who are passionate about preserving the town's rich history, we strive to honor the vision of those who built this space over a century ago. Our mission is to provide a welcoming place for gathering, celebrating the arts, and fostering community engagement, ensuring that this historic landmark remains a vibrant centerpiece for generations to come.
History
The St. Croix Falls Civic Auditorium, built in 1916, was envisioned as a multipurpose space for civic and cultural activities. Initially intended for vaudeville and opera performances, it became a movie theater during the silent film era and later adapted to accommodate "talkies." The auditorium also served as a community hub, hosting events ranging from basketball games to Red Cross efforts during World War I and the Spanish Flu pandemic. Over the decades, it underwent numerous transformations, including use as a library, city offices, and a sewing factory.
In 1990, Festival Theatre began producing live performances in the space, fulfilling its original purpose. The building was added to the Wisconsin and National Registers of Historic Places in 2006-2007. However, structural challenges led to its vacancy in recent years. In 2023, ownership transferred to 210 Civic, LLC, and restoration efforts began to revitalize this historic structure for modern use, continuing its legacy as a cultural centerpiece.

About Hotel Turkey
A nearly 100-year-old building, a family who took a chance on it, and a town that never stopped showing up.

1927
Built for the Railroad
H.B. Jordan built Hotel Turkey three blocks from the Fort Worth and Denver South Plains Railway depot as a place for ranchers, traveling salesmen, and anyone passing through the Panhandle. However, on June 12, a tornado leveled the building before it could even host its first guest. They got back to work rebuilding and in November, Hotel Turkey was open for business.
1927
The Town Showed Up
From day one, the hotel was more than a place to sleep. Salesmen used the meeting room to spread their wares. Dances and banquets filled the dining room. Turkey, Texas, made it their living room.
1971
Bob Wills Day Begins
Turkey started hosting Bob Wills Day in 1971 to honor the king of Western swing who claimed Turkey as his hometown. Hotel Turkey became the natural home base for the musicians and visitors who made the trip every last Saturday in April. Except for a COVID interruption in 2020 and 2021, it hasn't missed a year since.
1991
Made It Official
Hotel Turkey is added to the National Register of Historic Places. Nearly 65 years of West Texas history — recognized.
1994
A Future President Stops In
George W. Bush visited Turkey on October 29, 1994, during his campaign for Texas Governor. He stayed at Hotel Turkey and met with local residents and farmers. He won the election two weeks later.

Iridium FAQ
Are you open when there is no show scheduled?
No. Unfortunately, at this time The Iridium is only open when we have a show on our calendar. All show information can be found on Ticketweb.
Is there a late show?
There is only one show per night at the Iridium, unless noted otherwise. Most, if not all scheduling questions can be answered by visiting The Iridium’s Ticketweb page.
Food? Drinks?
The Iridium has a full service kitchen and bar.
Do I have to make a reservation to eat dinner?
The Iridium is only open to guests who have purchased tickets for that night’s show. All ticket holders will be seated at a table and given the opportunity to order food and drinks through our waitstaff. 
Accessibility information?
The Iridium entrance is located down one flight of stairs, and we will happily escort ticket holders to our elevator entrance upon request. 
What time are doors?
All show information can be found on Ticketweb.
What’s the difference between preferred and general seating?
Preferred seating is the front section of the floor that is closest to the stage, and the railing of the mezzanine. All other seating is general. If there is no option to purchase preferred seating, then it is all general admission. Seating is always first come, first serve within their prospective sections. 
Does The Iridium have a box office? Can I buy tickets for future shows at The Iridium?
The Iridium does not have a formal box office. Guests may purchase tickets for that night's show once the doors are open. All transactions for future shows must be done on Ticketweb. The only tickets sold on-site will be for the performance that night.
​
Is there an age limit? 
The Iridium welcomes guests 12 and older.

About
Ivy Room is a women-owned, all-inclusive venue with an audience spanning different age groups and ethnicities, and a special home for the LGBTQ+ community. Patrons come from the Bay Area and beyond to see artists from all genres of music—rock, punk, indie, jazz, folk, Americana, funk, metal, psych rock & more! — including local acts and touring artists.
Our success is founded on a deeply held mission of creating a safe, friendly, and diverse environment for our community to express their art and enjoy a show. 
Ivy Room is a historic neighborhood bar in Albany, California that was founded in the early 1940’s. Its beloved past owner, Dottie MacBeath, managed the Ivy in the 1990’s. Her son, Bill MacBeath, booked blues, rockabilly, country, rock, and swing acts like Big Sandy & His Fly-Rite Boys, Jim Campilongo, the Loved Ones, Deke Dickerson, and Chuck Prophet. A recurring sentiment that the music community kept telling us when we took over was how we “brought the Ivy Room back to what it was and more”.

Jack of the Wood
Tucked in a small corner at the Jack of the Wood pub, Asheville’s second brewery, Benefit Brewing, opened in 1997. Joe Eckert and Joan Cliney-Eckert co-owned the brew pub operation with Jonas Rembert and Andy Dahm as the brewing team. Jonas and Andy eventually left in 1999 to start the French Broad Brewing Co. The Jack of the Wood brewery then took the name Green Man brewing operating for years until the brewery found new ownership and moved to Buxton Avenue – becoming the first brewery in the now-busy South Slope brewing district. 
Beer is no longer brewed at Jack of the Wood, but many of the pub’s original elements remain. It’s still a downtown hot spot for food, brews and drink, and founders Joan and Joe Eckert who still own it, have maintained the original lively English and Irish vibe. The old-time mountain and Irish music jams continue, including a hot bluegrass jam Thursdays and brunch every Sunday. The venue extends a welcoming feeling to regulars and newcomers to this day.
The history of Jack of the Wood begins when the Eckerts moved to Asheville from Philadelphia in 1991, and opened a vegetarian lunch counter at the downtown YMCA. Two years later, they expanded and built the business into a full-service international vegetarian restaurant located on historic Wall Street. While the Laughing Seed Café was an immediate hit, the Eckerts wanted something more. Back then, says Joe Eckert, “There were no neighborhood bars in Asheville.” He wanted to do something similar to the ones he’d known in Pennsylvania, but with an atmosphere reflecting his and his wife’s heritage. “We had English and Irish backgrounds,” he explains. “Joan worked in our local pub in Philly. That’s where we met.”
They connected the building underneath Laughing Seed, which fronts on Patton Avenue, and the couple set to work creating the pub. Although local philanthropist Julian Price had helped get Laughing Seed going, Joan Eckert says she and Joe used the restaurant’s profits to build Jack of the Wood. Community Through Music (CTM) is a music enrichment program in NYC public schools that provides folk music, songs and movement to Pre-K-2nd Grade children that are otherwise without an in-school music program. CTM exposes children to folk music from all over the world, which allows them to gain knowledge and appreciation for different musical styles and instruments, as well as encourage group participation. Folk music is the music of the people, and tells the stories of people’s struggles, lives, and communities. These songs speak to the histories of people everywhere and it is one of CTM’s goals to use this music to expose young children to this shared history.

It’s this attitude that’s landed Daniel, Luke, and Jonathan – the Brindley Brothers – atop a growing venue ecosystem that remains a family business through and through.
It all started with now-legendary Jammin Java, a strip mall hole-in-the-wall the brothers fatefully snapped up in 2001. Utterly transformed by the Brindleys from a failing to thriving business, the space would go on to become a community hub for fellow music and entertainment lovers, a music school, as well as a cherished early-career stop for acts who’ve gone on to become household names, including Paramore, Bon Iver, and The Lumineers. 23 years later, it’s still a go-to stop for acts before they hit it big.
Currently, Union Stage Presents’ masthead includes seven venues (Union Stage, Jammin Java, The Howard, Pearl Street, Miracle Theatre, The Theatre at Capital Turnaround, and the brand new, 4000 capacity outdoor venue Plaza Stage at Nationals Park), a pizza place (Union Pie), and a community-centered vision that continues to push the boundaries of how great the show-going experience can be. Frequently Asked Questions 
About Jonathan’s Ogunquit 
 What is Jonathan’s Ogunquit? 
Jonathan’s Ogunquit is a locally owned restaurant and intimate live music venue in Ogunquit, Maine, known for elevated dining and nationally recognized musical performances in a listening-room setting. 
 Where is Jonathan’s Ogunquit located? 
Jonathan’s Ogunquit is located in Ogunquit, Maine, just minutes from Ogunquit Beach and the village center. Jonathan’s Ogunquit is also the closest restaurant to the Ogunquit Playhouse. 
 How long has Jonathan’s Ogunquit been in business? 
Jonathan’s Ogunquit has been a part of the Ogunquit community for nearly 50 years, welcoming locals and visitors alike for memorable food and live music experiences. 
 Is Jonathan’s Ogunquit locally owned? 
Yes. Jonathan’s Ogunquit is proudly locally owned and operated, with deep roots in Ogunquit and southern Maine. 
 What makes Jonathan’s Ogunquit unique? 
Jonathan’s Ogunquit offers a rare combination of a chef-driven restaurant and an intimate live music venue, where guests can enjoy dinner and experience performances by acclaimed artists in a relaxed, listening-focused environment. We are the epitome of the perfect “dinner & a show” experience. 
Reservations, Tickets & Dining 
 Do I need a reservation to dine at Jonathan’s Ogunquit? 
Reservations are strongly recommended, especially on performance nights and during peak season. 
 Are tickets required for live music performances? 
Yes. Most live music events at Jonathan’s Ogunquit require a ticket, which is separate from dinner unless otherwise noted. 
 Is dinner included with a show ticket? 
Our premier ticket includes a dinner credit that can be redeemed in our downstairs dining rooms before a show . Guests are encouraged to arrive early and enjoy dinner before the performance.

FAQ
 Are all of your shows +21? 
Most (but not all) performances are 21+. We can make exceptions based on the circumstances. It’s best to call during our open hours to discuss your situation or email info@thelariatbv.com.
 Is there seating for shows? 
Yes! We do have seating available for most shows. There are also stools lining either side of the dance floor and various tables throughout the space.
 How can I purchase tickets? 
We have three options for ticket purchases:
ONLINE: Hop over to the Live Music page on our website, open the event of your interest, and click the Buy Tickets button.
BOX OFFICE: Visit us during our open hours and purchase a real hard copy ticket from your bartender or server for your keepsake box!
DOOR: Although waiting until the last minute can be risky, we will have tickets for shows at the front door if it’s not sold out. However, you are encouraged to purchase tickets ahead of time to guarantee entrance.
 Can I transfer my ticket? 
Yes! Simply forward the electronic ticket or meet in person to hand over the physical ticket to the new recipient.
 Do you have a lost and found?
Credit cards can be retrieved with a valid photo ID. For all other lost and found items please call 719-966-5065 or show up.
 Do you have an ATM? 
Nope. But there is one conveniently located across the street at Collegiate Peaks Bank.
 Can I purchase band merch? 
When the band has merchandise available, it will be for sale. Each performance is different.
 Does The Lariat sell merchandise? 
We sure do! See the current offerings here. Purchases can be made on-site. Sorry, no online sales at this time.
 Can I watch the game when a concert is happening? 
We love sports too, but all TVs will be off when we have a show. Sorry for the inconvenience.
