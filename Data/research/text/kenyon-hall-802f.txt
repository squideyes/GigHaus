Frequently Asked Questions
 How do I book an event?
If you are a booking agent or want to perform on Kenyon Hall’s stage, please reach out to kenyon@kenyonhall.org. Kenyon Hall does not require radius clauses for touring acts and loves to get artists on as many stages as we can help with! We get many booking requests and may not be able to respond to all emails, but we will keep you in mind for the future.
Alternatively, you may contact us through our contact page with a subject line that includes the name of your act and proposed performance date.
For private rentals please refer to our Rentals Page for more information
 Where can I buy tickets?
You can buy tickets by selecting an event here. There, you can either reserve or purchase tickets through our ticket sales affiliate, donorbox. 
You may also purchase tickets on-site at the door—but with limited capacity, we may not be able to guarantee you a seat.
Kenyon Hall accepts cash, check, and all major credit cards including American Express.
 Where can I park?
Kenyon Hall is located on 35th Avenue Southwest, which is a busy arterial with restaurants on either side, making parking sometimes hectic. There is plenty of parking in the neighborhoods around Kenyon Hall that is safe, quiet, and easy to find.
If needed, there is an ADA-accessible ramp located at the back of the Hall with access from the alleyway off of Kenyon Avenue.
 Can I volunteer?
Definitely, we love our volunteers! Click here to learn more and sign a volunteer application form to begin volunteering now! Our general guidelines for volunteering are here.
 Do you still sell your famous rootbeer floats?
Yes, of course! Despite inflation, they remain at $2 per float.
 Does Kenyon Hall serve alcohol?
Kenyon Hall is currently considered a dry premise. This means that we do not provide alcohol at our shows nor do we allow alcohol on our premises unless the correct permits have been attained in advance.
That being said, Kenyon Hall is happy to support and be supported by the wonderful establishments on either side of us who do serve alcohol. The Locöl has, for years, supported what we do at the Hall and the community that come to shows. Check out what they have to offer here! Grab dinner and a drink before your show, or a quick one during an intermission.

Learn to play
Kenyon Hall currently offers private and group music lessons to kids ranging in age from early elementary to high school. The classes are taught by active musicians in the Seattle music community and are designed to emulate the experience of being in a cool band in our historic music city.
Crashing Tricycles at Kenyon Hall - Day Tripper
We currently offer weekly private lessons in piano, guitar, bass guitar, and drums, as well as songwriting, film scoring, audio engineering, and production. The group classes involve the creation of a band, weekly rehearsals, and quarterly shows. Most of the bands eventually progress to the point of writing songs and recording albums. The program encourages interest in all styles of music and is designed to create a safe and fun space that promotes creativity, collaboration, and fun.
We are thrilled to be able to expand Kenyon Hall’s music program for more students and more educators. For the last six years, Kenyon Hall has been offering group rock band classes to ages 7-18 with a focus on performing fun cover songs, songwriting, and recording and music production.
Learning to make music and be creative is an exciting but vulnerable experience; as such and when possible, we encourage joining in small groups to foster a familiar and comfortable environment to make music and learn together. Some musical experience is encouraged but not necessary. Classes are offered weekly in one-hour blocks as bands work toward refining their set for quarterly showcase concerts held at the Hall. 
For information about pricing and scheduling, please reach out to nshadel@gmail.com. We offer scholarships to families that have financial needs in an effort to expand access to our programs to anyone who is interested.

WAYS To support kenyon hall
We depend on generous supporters like you to keep music thriving, empowering young musicians, and bringing art to your West Seattle neighborhood. Your donations empower local emerging artists as they compose new works and we bring musicians from around the world to our stage.
 Give to Kenyon Hall
Seattle Artists, dba Kenyon Hall is a 501(c)(3) non-profit. 
Kenyon Hall’s vision is to engage the artist in anyone and inspire the arts in everyone. We aim to provide a multi-generational gathering place in West Seattle, promoting community through entertainment and arts-focused classes full of play, joy, and delight. Kenyon Hall is committed to artistic excellence and integrity, diversity of programming, and education for young artists. 
Seattle Artists was founded in 1993 on the belief that art can make an impact on its community. Over the course of three decades, so many audience members and artists have come together to build on that belief. You make Kenyon Hall what it is today: a home for artists and music that values cultural connections, embraces artists from all over the globe, and welcomes all.

 PLANNED GIVING
Become part of Seattle Artists legacy through bequests and planned giving. Consider adding Kenyon Hall to your will, and please inform us if you intend on doing so. Contact our Directors Team at 206-937-3613 or kenyon@kenyonhall.org.
 VOLUNTEER
Volunteers are crucial to Kenyon Hall’s functioning. If you’d like to volunteer, visit our Volunteer page for more information.

Our mission is to provide a multi-generational gathering place in West Seattle, promoting community through entertainment and arts-focused classes full of play, joy, and delight.
Seattle Artists is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization that operates Kenyon Hall, a community center for the arts in West Seattle. We host live music, theatrical performances, dance, and comedy shows, free programming like open mic nights and improv workshops, as well as music lessons for kids and teens in a historic West Seattle setting.
We are committed to ongoing learning in areas of diversity, equity, inclusion, and accessibility. We hope to work with our community to develop safe spaces for their application.
History
What has come to be known and loved as Kenyon Hall was originally built in 1916 as the Olympic Heights Social Hall, a friendly community social club. In 1993, it opened as a performance and education space for music and theater and has since been owned by local nonprofit Seattle Artists, providing an inclusive and welcoming community arts and education space for creatives to express themselves. Despite many iterations of the historic Hall over its century of life, one thing is certain—it is a community staple in our little corner of West Seattle.
The Mighty Wurlitzer
Kenyon Hall is home of the 1929 Mighty Wurlitzer Theatre Organ and features dozens of instruments built into the walls of the building. One of the only organs of its kind on the West Coast, the Wurlitzer is an electro-mechanical synthesizer, centered around the double manual organ console that controls dozens of acoustic instruments lining the theater walls by sending electrical impulses. The back of the theater houses a traditional pipe organ room, which pairs with the instruments that line the walls.
Remembering Lou Magor
A founder and beloved impresario of Kenyon Hall, Lou Magor was the very heart and soul of our organization until his passing in 2021. Lou’s vision and joyful leadership shaped every facet of Kenyon Hall, from booking quality entertainment and attracting talent from around the world to managing the upkeep of an old, quirky building with unflappable cheer. We are especially grateful for his skill and guidance in procuring the wonderful Steinway grand piano and the Mighty Wurlitzer for the Hall. His legacy leads us in all we do, and we endeavor to keep his spirit alive and well at the Hall. 
Learn more about Lou’s legacy and his immense contribution to our community.
