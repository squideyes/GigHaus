Built around the simple idea that live music is better when everyone is part of the experience, Over Yonder Concerts brings artists and audiences together in an intimate, welcoming setting. From the first note to the final encore, we’re here to create nights people remember.
We believe the best concert experiences aren’t always found in the biggest rooms. They happen when the crowd is connected, the artists can feel the energy in the room, and everyone shares the same moment.
Since 2016 we have brought talented musicians and appreciative audiences together for special and intimate performances. 
We host open-air concerts in the garden of our private home in Toledo, Ohio during the summer months and partner with a few other spots around town for an indoor season. These are concerts, rather than a parties, and it is important to make this distinction. Our shows provide a listening room atmosphere to both artists and audience members. 
Over Yonder Concert House is in no way a business. 100% of all donations and merchandise sales go directly to the artists, and help make this a viable option for traveling musicians. 
For most shows, we have a potluck beginning one hour before each show and invite all guests to join us with a dish to share. BYOB.

We are always excited when our buddy Jordan Tice makes it to Toledo! 
He’s making a quick run through the Midwest in December as the opening act for Sarah Jarosz, and we are thrilled to have him stop back through town on his way home to Nashville.
Jordan Tice is an award-winning guitarist and songwriter described by the Fretboard Journal as "quite simply, one of our favorite acoustic guitarists performing today." He has 7 projects to his name as a solo artist and 6 as a founding member of the string band, Hawktail. Equally virtuosic as a flatpicker and fingerstylist, and with a casual vocal style, Tice conjures ingredients from far-flung worlds with ease. His performances call upon the repertories of American and British Isles folk, bluegrass, blues and pop-oriented songs, and though his earlier releases are instrumental in focus, he has been carefully honing his craft as a singer-songwriter in his most recent work. This includes his albums Motivational Speakeasy (produced by Kenneth Pattengale of The Milk Carton Kids and praised by NPR Music as “a really wonderful record”), Wild Rose of Morning (a collaboration with Andrew Marlin and Christian Sedelmyer), and Badlettsville.
He has performed at festivals such as the Grey Fox Bluegrass Festival, Green Mountain Bluegrass and Roots, Red Wing Roots, Wintergrass Music Festival, and the Strawberry Music Festival and opened for artists like Watchhouse, I’m With Her, The Milk Carton Kids, and Madison Cunningham. In addition to his solo and collaborative work, he has performed and recorded as a sideman with Americana artists such as David Rawlings, Yola, Andrew Marlin, and Aoife O’Donovan. 
This show will be at the Flamb-OWE-nce Community House at 2492 Scottwood Ave. For this Sunday show, we’ll start a bit earlier than normal with a potluck at 5:00 and music at 6:00.
$20 cash only at the door. As always, 100% goes directly to the artist.

Bill Edwards is an award-winning singer/songwriter now residing in Ann Arbor, MI. He’s won the Billboard magazine songwriting contest, been chosen by Rodney Crowell as an honorary instructor at his Nashville song camp, had songs in movies and on TV, and played countless venues large and small. Bill has become known for his thoughtful, moving and sometimes hilarious songs, supported by his fine voice and excellent guitar playing. 
Bill's recent recordings are hand-made in his home studio, accompanying himself on guitars, fiddle, mandolin, dobro, and a host of other instruments. His most recent collection, Enough About Me, features songs from the perspectives of several imagined men, women and children. It will be officially released on Bill's birthday, September 15, 2026.

We are music fans first.
We host house concerts because we love live music and we want to support touring musicians. 
Our City has a rich musical history. Art Tatum called Toledo home. Nearly all of the blues legends played at Hines’ Farm, and Ike Stubblefield attended high school just down the road. While we still have a lot of local talent, few touring musicians stop through anymore. Over Yonder Concert House seeks to fill the void and provide traveling artists with a home on the road.
In addition to providing a stage and cultivating a listening room atmosphere, we pride ourselves in offering genuine hospitality to performers who stop in.
We encourage you to build in some time to explore our community while you are here.
Our neighborhood, the Historic Old West End, is one of the best kept secrets in the country with Twenty-five city blocks making up one of the largest collections of late Victorian houses left standing in the United States. Frank Lloyd Wright studied this area in his planning of his Oak Park Project in Illinois. Pristine examples of Colonial, Georgian, Italian Renaissance, Queen Ann, Dutch Colonial, French Second Empire and Arts and Crafts homes fill our streets.
It’s a short walk to the Toledo Museum of Art, which houses an internationally recognized collection of more than 30,000 objects in 45 galleries spread over 280,000 square feet. The museum is free and open to the public year round Wednesday - Sunday.
The National Recreation and Park Association named Metroparks Toledo the #1 park system in the USA. Our 19 Metroparks stretch from the Lake Erie shoreline to the globally rare Oak Openings region and feature more than 200 miles of public recreational trails to explore.
You can probably tell that we love our community … we think you will too.
