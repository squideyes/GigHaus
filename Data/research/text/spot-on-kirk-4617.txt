About The Spot on Kirk
Nestled in the heart of Roanoke, The Spot on Kirk is a lively hub for music enthusiasts. It offers a unique space where local and touring artists can showcase their talents.
The Story
Mission Statement: To serve as an intimate music venue and gathering place in Downtown Roanoke, fostering a vibrant community through diverse live entertainment experiences.
In 2016, The Spot on Kirk started under the non-profit umbrella of Down By Downtown Music (DxDT), which is an annual celebration of Roanoke's ever-growing music scene which takes place every April.
As a very special, much needed music venue, The Spot on Kirk has hosted over 500 shows within nearly a decade — and that's not even including all the history from back when the joint was known as Kirk Avenue Music Hall! Attracting an eclectic cast of artists from around the world, and helping launch the likes of Morgan Wade and Palmyra, The Spot on Kirk is both a listening room and community center for all music lovers. For an exhaustive list of the artists who have played on our stage, visit our archive page, HERE!
This charming room also serves as an event rental space for special events including private parties, meetings, screenings, and performances. To learn more about rentals, visit our rental tab HERE!

FAQ
Frequently Asked Questions
Where are you located?
We’re at 22 Kirk Avenue SW in Roanoke, Virginia just a few doors down from LUCKY Restaurant.
What’s your venue like? I’ve never been.
The Spot on Kirk is a casual atmosphere where the music takes center stage. Musicians don’t have to compete with clanking dishes or screaming bar patrons – they just perform for the sake of performing. All shows are considered general admission, but there’s not a bad seat in the house. The coolest part of our venue? It feels like you’re at a private concert in your living room surrounded by people who are there to appreciate it with you. We don’t serve food, but we do serve drinks including beer, wine, specialty drinks, sodas, and water.
You don’t serve food, so where do I eat?
The Spot is in good company! Some of our favorite restaurants are right here on Kirk Avenue with dozens more just a couple of blocks away in the Historic Roanoke City Market. Come early enough to grab a bite to eat nearby, or hit up some of Roanoke’s favorite late-night spots for signature cocktails. Find specifics in our Around Town section.
Where do I sleep?
While we’d love for you to stay as long as you can, our chairs aren’t really made for sleeping. There are plenty of accommodations in and around Downtown Roanoke. If you haven’t already checked out our Around Town section, you should probably do that now.
How do I get tickets?
You’ll find a “BUY TICKETS” button attached to each of our events on this website that take you to EventBrite. Don’t want to reserve your ticket in advance? You can buy tickets at the door with cash, card, and most mobile payment apps. Just know they may cost more the day of the show or, even worse, the show may be completely sold out.
How do I know if tickets are still available?
Online ticket sales typically end at 5pm on the day of an event. In most cases, tickets will still be available at the door. If a show is sold out, we’ll be announcing it everywhere possible: this website, Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, and from various regional mountain tops (just in case you’re within earshot).
What forms of payment are accepted at The Spot?
All public events hosted by The Spot on Kirk will accept cash, card, and most mobile payment apps for both tickets at the door and drinks at the bar. For mobile app payments, Apple Pay, Google Pay, and Cash App are specifically accepted at this time. Venmo and PayPal not accepted.
What is the parking situation like?
There is free street-parking available in Downtown Roanoke after 5:00 pm. If you can’t tango into a space, there are paid parking lots and garages nearby. Two pay-by-the-hour lots are on the same block as The Spot, and a short walk away you’ll find the Center in the Square Garage or the Church Avenue Garage.
Do you allow smoking inside The Spot on Kirk? Kids? Pets?
No smoking. 
Kids are welcome. 
And pets aren’t allowed (unfortunately).
How do I keep up with what’s happening at The Spot?
We constantly update our website, Facebook and Instagram, but we encourage you to sign up for our newsletter!
I want to perform at The Spot. Who do I need to talk to?
The Spot on Kirk is dedicated to providing audiences with a great listening experience! If you have an audience in Roanoke or are touring through Roanoke and would like to inquire about performing, please contact us at booking@thespotonkirk.org.
I scrolled down this entire page looking for information on renting The Spot for my special event. Can you just tell me about it already?
Sure. The Spot on Kirk can be rented for special events depending on availability. Our full capacity is 130. Inside, we have about 80 chairs and 15 stools if desired, three bistro tables, a few other tables, two bathrooms, a stage, and a projector with a screen. Rental pricing starts around $500, but will vary depending on the length of your rental, the type of event, how many guests you plan to have, and whether or not your event is private or open to the public. If none of this helped, or you have more questions, please email us at info@thespotonkirk.org.
Still have questions?
Send us a direct message and we'll be happy to help clarify everything.

WNRN Presents: 
Dogwood Tales: "Every Star In Rockingham County" Record Release 
with The Concerns 
Purple mountains, empty highways, jet trails as the sun fades; Dogwood Tales paint in valley colors on their forthcoming album, Every Star in Rockingham County. Hailing from Harrisonburg, VA in the heart of Shenandoah, the band is fronted by founders and childhood friends Kyle Grim (guitar/vocals) and Ben Ryan (guitar/vocals), alongside college contemporaries Jake Golibart (drums) and Danny Gibney (bass). With its location in its title, their new album is as much a love letter to the local DIY scene, vast farmlands and surrounding mystical mountains as it is a portrait of present-day small town American life. While tracks like “Mt. Jackson” speak candidly about the experience of songwriter Kyle Grim seeing his once cheerful, tractor-driving uncle hospital bound, it also captures a broader, ever-relatable image of a society drowned in hospital bills and anxieties of what could have been, keeping us at odds with the ever present moment. 
Though heavy and personal in its lyrical content, the album was born out of a spirit of naivety and experimentation that breathes lightness and excitement into the music. Pedal steel chords and delicate harmonization between Ryan and Grim — who’ve at this point been singing together half their lives — accompany jamming power pop drumlines by Golibart, tasteful guitar licks and wistful video game soundbites. Whether it’s song titles like “Princess Peach” and the football games and Coca-Colas of “McMemories” Dogwood Tales speaks in a vocabulary previously unexplored in earlier works, unafraid of showing as much reverence for iconic fast food and video game characters as they do their grandmothers and local basketball legends. Or the achingly specific lyrics in “Angel Dreams”, “We drove to Southern Kitchen, you wouldn’t even play my favourite song. You cried in your fried chicken, the waitress thought I said something wrong,” it’s easy to feel like you’re sitting on a couch in the band’s attic; sharing stories, ambient Gamecube on in the background. 
The humming electric guitars over beds of pedal steel and earnest lyricism find ESiRC in dialogue with indie rock’s current Lo-Fi country romanticism, lyrically pays homage to revered American songwriters in the footsteps of Townes Van Zandt, but ultimately takes listeners on a ride through uncharted territory. It’s a ride through Rockingham county, through the moody valley, the tight bond of four friends coming of age in a rural Virginia town, and a digital age where loneliness is far too prevalent. Thankfully, we’ve got the stars to keep us company. 
Support: 
The Concerns are a super group from Roanoke, Va. consisting of artist and singer songwriter Daniel Cundiff (The Young Sinclairs, Eternal Summers, The Sad Cobras) on guitar. Sam Lunsford (Stimulator Jones, The Young Sinclairs, Cinematheque) on drums. John Pence (The Young Sinclairs, Cinematheque, Decampi) on guitar. And Taylor Lineberry (Woolens) on bass. Their first album, County Blue on WarHen Records, infused elements of early 90's alt. rock, alt. country, indie folk, and a touch of shoegaze and was mixed by Louie Lino of Nada Surf. They have spent the last year recording a follow up album using their special fusion of "spacey alt. county-shoegaze" in a secret location in the Blue Ridge Mountains called the Mystic Fortress.
