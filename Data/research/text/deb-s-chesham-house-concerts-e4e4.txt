"House Concerts build strong friendships between musicians, presenters, and listeners. Most importantly,
House Concerts provide the most intimate and compelling performance environment available."
 - Darryl Purpose
October, 2023 - I am once again happy to invite musicians to play in my living room. It is important for all my music friends to know that 100% of the recommended donation you pay goes directly to the musician. For me this is a labor of love that I started doing in 2002. It is not a business. let's get back to enjoying this wonderful intimate setting with some amazing musician friends. Thank you. ~ Deb
2020 - 2023 - This site remains here at the web site for the history of it all. It was a wonderful run of supporting young musicians to help them expand their careers and to then progress to a Peterborough Folk Music larger venue. The indoor house concerts were on hold during the Pandemic. We were pleased to host outdoor concerts with Tom Rush and Seth Glier, each doing two sold our safely spaced concerts. Thank you for all the support given to the wonderful times we have had enjoying the pot lucks, camaraderie and musicians. See you soon for more Deb's Concerts and at a PFM concert. ~ Deb
Since 2002, Deb's House Concerts in Chesham has presented, alphabetically, performances by Andrew Duhon Trio, Slaid Cleaves, Rachael Davis, Antje Duvekot, Ben Demerath, Stacy Earle & Mark Stuart, Cliff Eberhardt, Mark Erelli, Garnet Rogers, Gandalf Murphy and The Slambovian Circus of Dreams, Seth Gier, John Gorka, Tracy Grammer with Jim Henry, The Hunger Mountain Boys, Lori McKenna, Alastair Moock, Peter Mulvey, Harvey Reid and Joyce Andersen, Mustard's Retreat, Jory Nash, Northern Lights, Off The Cuff, Ellis Paul, Kerri Powers, Darryl Purpose, Red Molly, Garnet Rogers, Les Sampou, Bill Staines, David Jacobs-Strain, The Novel Ideas, We're About 9, Lula Wiles, Brooks Williams The WIYOS, Tom Rush with Matt Nakoa and many many more.
You drive down a dirt road and turn in to Deb’s long driveway that drives thru a field, and you instantly feel welcome. You will park alongside the driveway and enter the house along with up to 50 other people for a pot luck dinner one hour before the concert. You will be greeted by friendly warm people, some regulars and many new to the concept but all there to appreciate some good fine home cooking and the music in a warm loving home that provides the musicians with a listening room full of music lovers. It’s all about the music that brings these fine folks all together for an evening of entertainment.
House concerts bring people together for the love of the music in an intimate, relaxed and self-serve, friendly environment, in a listening room, while always remembering you are in someone’s home and will treat it as if it were your own. These concerts are held in the spring and fall. If the weather is lovely, most folks enjoy eating out doors, roaming around the yard and gardens and some walk down to the stream. It is out of the generosity of your hosts that these concerts happen. Generally the hosts have met these musicians at other venues or at festivals and they want to share them with as many music lovers as they can.
If you have prearranged to attend the pot luck through a phone call or email, you will only need to bring the food and if you like, BYOB. Folks bring everything from soft drinks to wine, beer, etc. Coffee and tea are provided as well as everything else you need to enjoy your dinner. Dessert is held during intermission at which time folks continue to enjoy meeting and talking with the musicians and each other, purchasing musicians product and then settling down for the second half of the concert.
If you have any questions, be sure to ask by emailing Deb at deb@pfmsconcerts.org. We do not publish the exact location. You will be given the address when you reserve your place at the concert. They are held in Harrisville, NH. These concerts often sell out way in advance so if you think you want to come to a specific concert, don’t hesitate to get your tickets. Be sure to email Deb to reserve your spot at the pot luck and to sign up for a main dish or a dessert.
One final thing...100% of the ticket and artists product sales goes to the artists. Your hosts provide this evening as a labor of love, out of a passion for the music and the dersire to share the music and these fine talended people with you.
We look forward to seeing you at a concert.

Dear Friend,
I first became aware of Peterborough Folk Music nearly 25 years ago when, as regular attendees of many Boston-area folk venues and coffeehouses, we would see our favorite artists' upcoming shows in Peterborough, NH, and think, “We wish it were closer – they have some awesome shows!”
Five years ago and two moves later, we landed in PFM’s backyard. Many factors came into play in deciding on the Monanock Region for our new home, but don’t think for a moment that there wasn’t a twinge of excitement about the possibility of culture and folk music right nearby.
After attending several shows, I offered my services – moving chairs or whatever was needed. I didn’t hesitate when Deb called this spring to ask if I’d be interested in volunteering at the door, nor did I hesitate not long after when she asked if I would join the board.
Peterborough Folk Music is a hidden gem in a quiet corner of New Hampshire. There are few rural areas where you can see established legends like Tom Rush, Chris Smither, or Iris DeMent; perennial favorites Rani Arbo & daisy mayhem, Catie Curtis, or Peter Mulvey; or rising indie-folk artists like Low Lily, Antje Duvekot, and Seth Glier. That level of live music in a small community doesn't happen by accident.
Our mission is to support these artists by bringing the most innovative, entertaining, and diverse musicians to the Monadnock Region. Your tax-deductible support allows us to continue bringing quality music to your backyard, support your favorite (and soon-to-be favorite) folk artists, and keep providing those intimate spaces where real community happens.
Will you join us in that mission today? You can make your gift using the enclosed envelope or online at pfmsconcerts.org/donate.
Thanks for being part of the PFM family - I hope to see you at a show real soon! 
In song and community,
Paul Allen-Webber
P.S. Making your gift online is quick and secure at pfmsconcerts.org/donate. While you're there, check out our upcoming lineup to grab your tickets for the next show!

PFMS Board Members: Diane Goodman, Jim Van Valkenburgh, Yvonne Gifford, Kate Coon, Bill Goodwin, and Deb McWethy
The Peterborough Folk Music evolved out of the well-known Folkway, a wonderful coffee house founded in 1975 and unfortunately permanently closed its doors on January 1, 1996. At that time Deb McWethy, a board member, announced her vision to keep the Folkway's legacy alive and to continue bringing folk music to the Monadnock Region. The new Society made up of some Folkway members, got busy acquiring a non-profit status, looking for new board members, corporate sponsors, web and graphic designers, volunteers, and looking for a good place to hold folk concerts. We landed in the Unitarian Universalist Church on Main St. which was a wonderful venue for us for the first few years. Our first concert, only 7 days after the closing of The Folkway, was Dar Williams and Peter Mulvey. Attendance was beyond our expectations and so it has continued since 1996. 
Over our 30 years, PFM has hosted concerts at many locations around the Monadnock Region. Currently, 2026, we enjoy Bass Hall at The Monadnock Center, in Peterborough, The UU Church in Peterborough and The Park Theatre in Jaffrey, NH. 
In 2002 Deb McWethy also started her house concert series in order to also provide a venue for rising musicians. After playing many times in her living room or outdoors in her yard, those musicians started drawing larger audiences and performed at the larger venues.
