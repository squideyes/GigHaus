 Cafe 
Food doesn’t have to be fancy to be good, satisfying and healthy. Our menu features a group of simple classics. All our soups and salads are homemade with fresh ingredients and we feature fresh salads using seasonal local grown produce. Other favorites include a classic Reuben on marbled rye, Chicken Brunswick stew and pinto beans with onions and skillet baked cornbread.
Through our relationship with local farms and bakeries we are able to bring you seasonal and delicious food, in a relaxed country atmosphere. Saturday lunch times are a great opportunity to eat fresh local food to the accompaniment of Americana Music on stage. 
The Soda Fountain at the Floyd Country Store is a favorite destination for young and old alike. We serve hand-dipped, high-quality, ice cream and a selection of old- fashioned milkshakes, malts, sodas and sundaes. Try our hand-spun milkshakes, a sundae made your way or a rich mocha malt.

The Floyd Country Store 
Our Story
Nestled just a stone’s throw from Floyd County’s only traffic light, The Floyd Country Store sits at the very heart of our vibrant community. For well over a century, this historic space has served as a gathering place for locals, travelers, and passersby alike.
First opening its doors as a downtown general store in the early 1900s, the space underwent a cultural transformation in the early 1980s. Then known as Cockram’s General Store, it became the epicenter of the local Appalachian music and dance scene when musicians and dancers began gathering weekly to share tunes and camaraderie—a tradition that blossomed into the famous Friday Night Jamboree.
Today, the Store remains a proud beacon of Appalachian culture, celebrated for bringing people together through traditional music, dance, art, and food. Whether you are a lifelong resident or a curious wanderer, we invite you to join a workshop, share a delicious house-made meal, step back in time at our old-fashioned Soda Fountain, or experience one of our many live events.
Owned and operated by Dylan Locke and Heather Krantz since 2014, The Floyd Country Store is dedicated to cultivating and strengthening community through experiences rooted deeply in Appalachian traditions.
As co-owner Dylan Locke describes, “We have so much care for this community—our team, the musicians, the dancers, the makers, and the guests who find us by chance or make planned pilgrimages to see these traditions alive and well. There is power and magic in music-making, dancing, sharing, and living within a small community where you come together more than you separate. The simple act of playing music together, dancing with strangers, and upholding age-old traditions is what brings us all together.”
Plan Your Visit: Check our Event Calendar for upcoming public events and explore our Café Menu for details on our scratch-made food and Soda Fountain treats.

Music & Dance Traditions 
Since the early 1900s, the site of The Floyd Country Store has been a hub for people. Originally called Farmer’s Supply, it was a place where farmers, craftsmen, laborers and travelers all converged to trade wares and discuss the topics of the day. When the workday was done, these people of all ages and backgrounds would converge here to dance, and share the music and lore traditional to the mountainous area known today as the Blue Ridge Plateau.
The music of this time and place — Mountain Music — was prolific in its development. Traditional English, Scots-Irish, German, French, Spanish, African and Indigenous cultures coalesced to invariably trade ideas and culture, producing a music that itself made no delineations between origin, race or gender, absorbing anything in its path. It grew in abundance, creating a richly mixed heritage that is still respected and admired today, in part due to its role in establishing the foundations of earliest recorded Country music. This notion is best encapsulated by Kinney Rorer, who describes this particular region of Southwestern Virginia as “a fertile crescent” for old-time music – one of a number of areas in North America that served as a hub for musical and cultural exchange.
Floyd County, Virginia has maintained a tradition of music and dancing that has its roots in the culture of the Scots-Irish and German settlers who moved into the Blue Ridge Mountains in the mid-1700s and in the culture of enslaved people of African and Afro-Caribbean descent and English farmers, traders, and entrepreneurs who migrated up the rivers from the coast. 
The Scots-Irish and Afro-Carribean traditions of making music, storytelling and dancing still survive at places such as The Floyd Country Store, where crowds gather on Friday nights and throughout the week to dance and play music together. A number of the tunes that are still commonly played for dances at the store, including “Billy in the Lowground”, “Soldier’s Joy”, and “Hop Light Ladies”, would have originated in the Scots-Irish culture of the British Isles — while tunes such as “Old Reuben,” “Shortening Bread,” “Roustabout” and the driving rhythm of clawhammer banjo, appear to have deep roots in Afro-Caribean and African-American culture.
Many local churches have strong musical ministries and the gospel music which has been heard for generations is still alive. You can hear some of these gospel musicians at The Floyd Country Store, or out on the street on a warm Friday evening.
Today, some of the finest old-time and bluegrass musicians around turn up on stage at the store, enjoying the energy and enthusiasm of an audience that truly appreciates traditional music. Families still encourage youngsters to pick up an instrument, and Floyd has many fine teachers of music in the local style. With The Floyd Country Store’s sister organization, The Handmade Music School and the advent of The Crooked Road – Virginia’s Heritage Music Trail, locals have renewed their pride in the skills that have been kept alive over the years in the living rooms and back porches of Floyd County. 
As for dancing, that goes right along with the music! Clogging and flat footing are passed along from mother to daughter, and grandfather to grandson. (You’ll often see dancers from 4 years old to 94 years old on the dance floor at The Floyd Country Store). These styles of dancing are full of energy and often form part of the percussion for the music of a good dance band. The dancing styles along the Blue Ridge Plateau of Southwest Virginia tend to be “caught, not taught” and continue to provide endless opportunities for folks to enjoy themselves on the dancefloor at The Floyd Country Store. Join us!
Events at the Floyd Country Store where you can enjoy these music and dance traditions today:
TUESDAY TUNES
Tuesday Tunes is a celebration of our local bluegrass and old time musicians. Every Tuesday at dinnertime, our musical guests alternate between Jesse Smathers and Raistlin Brabson. Jesse Smathers is an accomplished mandolinist, guitarist, and singer originally from Eden, North Carolin, not making his home here in Floyd County, Virginia. He is a member of Lonesome River Band and he teaches mandolin and guitar for The Handmade Music School. Raistlin Brabson is a skilled fiddle player who plays for a number of celebrated local bands, including his band Raistlin Brabson and Up Jumped Trouble. In addition to being a regular Tuesday Tunes guest, he often plays at the Friday Night Jamboree. 
Tuesday Tunes is free admission, but guests are encouraged to give a donation of $10 to support the musicians. We also welcome you to enjoy dinner from The Floyd Country Store Cafe! 
HONKY TONK THURSDAYS
Put on your boots and get ready to scoot across the dance floor! Every Thursday night from 7:00 to 9:00 PM, we offer highly danceable country music from Redd Volkaert, the legendary telecaster slinger from Merle Haggard’s band, along with different musicians from around Southwest Virginia and the rest of the country. (When Redd isn’t here we’ll have a substitute band that will keep the crowd dancing and smiling). If you enjoy honky tonk, Western swing, and good old classic country, you’ll want to join us on Thursday nights for some good food and a spacious dance floor. 
Honky Tonk Thursday is free admission, but guests are encouraged to give a donation of $10 to support the musicians. We also welcome you to enjoy dinner from The Floyd Country Store Cafe! 
FRIDAY NIGHT JAMBOREE
The Friday Night Jamboree is the heartbeat of The Floyd Country Store. In the early 1980s, a local bluegrass band started holding their band practices in The Floyd Country Store (then known as Cockram’s General Store) every Friday night. Passersby would knock on the doors, asking to be let inside to better hear the music. Pretty soon, so many people were coming by that the band decided to leave the doors open. As the crowds grew, other musicians came to join the fun. Today, the tradition of the Friday Night Jamboree is alive and well as locals and visitors from far and wide come to immerse themselves in the magic of Appalachian music and dance. The evening begins with a 6:30 PM gospel set, and kicks into high gear at 7:30 PM with two back-to-back old time dance sets. Seats can be reserved in advance, but standing/dancing tickets are sold at the door every Friday starting at 4:45 PM. 
Please take note that seating is limited. If a seat is important to you, reserve your tickets in advance or arrive early to the event.
AMERICANA AFTERNOONS
Saturday lunch at the store is a special event. Pick up one of our homemade soups or sandwiches, or a plate of farm-fresh salad and find a table near the stage. From 12 PM to 3 PM, you can enjoy Americana Afternoons. The music is varied, but has the common thread of Americana: traditional acoustic music. Some of it is indigenous, including bluegrass, or the music of contemporary singer-songwriters: you might hear the blues, or some of the Celtic or African sounds that underpin our American folk traditions.
Admission is free with tips appreciated.
SUNDAY MUSIC JAMS
Our Sunday Music Jams are a great way to see traditional music the way it has been played and handed down for centuries, amongst friends and family in a community setting. Every Sunday afternoon, The Floyd Country Store comes alive with community music during our music jams. If you’re just learning to play a musical instrument (or if you’d just like to practice), join us for our Beginner Jam from 12:00 PM to 1:00 PM in the Floyd Country Store Community Room. At 1:30 PM, the Old Time Jam begins in front of the Floyd Country Store Stage. The Old Time Jam is led by a different group of seasoned string band musicians every week. Tunes are played at a moderate speed. Traditional instruments and dancers are welcome! At 4:00 PM, the Bluegrass Jam kicks off, with leaders who guide the group through bluegrass songs and tunes. Song selections and solos pass around the circle. Bring your instrument and join in the music, or sit back and enjoy the show. If you’re hungry, grab a bite to eat from the Cafe! American Heritage Music 
The store is an incubator for local heritage music and dancing. Year-round you can count on music and dancing every Friday evening, Americana music every Saturday afternoon and a traditional mountain music jam every Sunday. Many Saturday evenings we feature an old-time dance or concert.
For a listing of upcoming events check out our calendar. On music nights the store remains open late and we serve soup, sandwiches, ice cream and home baked goods.
Some of the finest old-time musicians around turn up on stage at the store, enjoying the energy and enthusiasm of an audience that truly appreciates traditional music.
The Friday Night Jamboree happens every Friday, rain or shine, all year round. The first hour is gospel music and this is followed by two dance bands. We post our upcoming schedules as soon as they are available, so if you see an empty slot, check back soon for updates.
The Jamboree has limited reserved seating and is often standing room only, so come early for seat. Tickets for the Jamboree are sold online and at the door. We open our ticket desk at approximately 4:45PM after we have had time to move all the store fixtures out of the way and set up the chairs.
On Saturdays we have our Americana Afternoons starting at noon with different performers scheduled to play anything from blues, folk, jazz, rock & roll, bluegrass and country. It’s all American roots music. For information about performing, contact for booking here. This is a free event.
Every Sunday from 1:30pm – 6:00 pm, we host a jam lead by a local Old Time or Bluegrass band. The Old-Time Jams is 1:30pm to 3:30 pm and the Bluegrass Jam is 4PM – 5:30PM. (*If there is a Sunday concert, the Bluegrass Jam may end early, call ahead or ask our team) Please come and join us. This is a free event.
Check our calendar for details of all our events.
