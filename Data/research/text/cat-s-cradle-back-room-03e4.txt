About 
Located less than a mile from the University of North Carolina Chapel Hill campus in downtown Carrboro, the Cat’s Cradle has been the Triangle’s premier live music venue for over 50 years. 
Having hosted bands as diverse as Nirvana, Public Enemy, John Mayer, Joan Baez, and Iggy Pop, the Cat’s Cradle has become a must-stop venue for premier music acts over the past several decades. With a capacity of 750 people, the Cat’s Cradle allows concert-goers a chance to see established and up-and-coming bands alike in an intimate setting.

FAQ 
WHAT IS CAT’S CRADLE’S CURRENT POLICY REGARDING COVID-19?
UPDATED (9/20/22):
Cat’s Cradle and Cat’s Cradle Back Room no longer require proof of COVID-19 vaccination or negative COVID test for entrance to our venues.
Given the close proximity of patrons in an indoor space for potentially an extended period, we continue to strongly recommend and encourage that all who attend concerts wear a mask and be fully vaccinated.
* Masks are always available at our entrances and bars for anyone who needs one.
* Please note that in the event that a specific artist requests patrons be vaccinated or wear masks for their concert, we will enforce these requirements. In these cases, any specific show requirements will be clearly stated along with the show details, and in any correspondence we send to ticket holders.
Thanks to everyone for your cooperation over the past year, while we’ve worked to help end the COVID pandemic. 
GETTING HERE (Location, Parking, Seating, Timing, etc.)
Where is the club located?
We are at 300 E Main St. in Carrboro, NC 27510.
Are there two clubs?
Yes, Cat’s Cradle has two venues that are located adjacent to each other. The larger club is referred to as the Main Room, and the smaller club is referred to as the Back Room. Both venues host shows and events that are All Ages, unless posted otherwise.
Where do I park?
Parking is available in the deck beside the Hampton Inn (be sure you do not park in a spot designated for hotel only), or any parking area designated as a town/municipal lot around Carrboro. The Parking spaces in front of the strip mall are timed limited spaces, so use caution not to use them incorrectly. Parking is prohibited in the back alley, as it is a fire lane.
Please click here for more info on directions & parking.
Where should I enter the clubs?
The Entrances for BOTH Cat’s Cradle venues are behind the building.
If you are facing Cat’s Cradle, from Main Street, head to the left, past the ArtsCenter and loop around to the back of the strip mall building, turn right and continue down the back alleyway to the Cat’s Cradle doors. Once you walk all the way down the alleyway, The Main Room is straight ahead of you with double doors, and the Back Room is tucked away to the right with a single door entrance.
If you are coming from the five story parking deck by the Hampton Inn, you should walk straight down the alleyway behind the Strip Mall towards the Cat’s Cradle doors in the rear of the building. The Main Room entrance is straight ahead of you with double doors, and the Back Room entrance is tucked away to the right with a single door.
Does the Cat’s Cradle have seats?
Unless a show is advertised as seated, our shows are general admission. There is a limited amount of fixed seating along the perimeter of the main floor in the Main Room, which is first come first served. In the Back Room, first come first served seating is available on the balcony. Please arrive early to increase your chances of getting a seat. 
Patrons with special needs should call the box office at 919-967-9053 in advance to make arrangements.
What time should I get there if I want to sit?
We suggest you be in line before doors open. Keep in mind that no matter how early you line up there may already be people ahead of you in line, and you are not guaranteed any particular spots inside.
What time does the band go on?
Door and show times are available on the website. The first band of the evening will go on stage at the show time posted on our website, or very shortly thereafter. We make an effort to keep our shows moving along without unnecessary delay; however, the length of sets and the time required between bands can be unpredictable and it’s not always possible to accurately predict the time that a certain band will take the stage.
ACCEPTED FORMS OF PAYMENT AT CAT’S CRADLE
For tickets purchased at the door on the night of the show (if show is not yet sold out):
As of now, we only accept CASH for tickets at the door for both the Main Room and Back Room. 
BARS:
The bar in the Main Room accepts CASH ONLY.
The bar in the Back Room accepts cash and credit cards.
Merchandise:
The merchandise booth may accept CASH only—it depends on the artist / can vary (some artists arrange for credit card payment, but not all).
Box Office:
The box office accepts CASH, VISA, Mastercard and American Express.
Do you have an ATM?
An ATM is located inside the Main Room club if you need to take out cash. The ATM can be used when buying a ticket for either room.
TICKET-RELATED INQUIRIES
If a show is listed as sold out on the website, can I still get tickets?
Sold out really does mean there are no tickets left. This means there are no tickets available in advance OR at the door on the night of the show. Please do not call the venue with regard to acquiring tickets for a sold out show.
How can I buy tickets?
There are several ways to purchase tickets for Cat’s Cradle shows (see below).
Online:
Find tickets at www.etix.com (unless it is a show presented by Cat’s Cradle at another venue with a different official ticketing partner – see below for more options.)
On our website click the “Buy Tickets” button next to the event you’d like to attend (on the Homepage or the Events page)

 Protomartyr 
 J.R.C.G., Verity Den 
Buy Tickets 
 Gilla Band + Protomartyr Superfan Pass 
 Saturday, November 14
 Doors: 7 pm : Show: 8 pm 
 Cat’s Cradle
Since their 2012 debut No Passion All Technique, the Detroit post-punk band Protomartyr have mastered the art of evoking place: the grinding Midwest humility of their hometown, as well as the x-rayed elucidation of America that comes with their vantage. Protomartyr—vocalist Joe Casey, guitarist Greg Ahee, drummer Alex Leonard, and bassist Scott Davidson—have become synonymous with caustic, impressionistic assemblages of politics and poetry, the literal and oblique. 
The group’s sixth album, recorded at Sonic Ranch in Tornillo, Texas, is called Formal Growth In The Desert. And though frontman Joe Casey did have a humbling experience staring at awe-inspiring Sonoran rock formations and reckoning with his own smallness in the scheme of things – as recounted in the single “Elimination Dances” – the title is not necessarily a nod to the sandy expanses of the southwest. Detroit, too, is like a desert. “The desert is more of a metaphor or symbol,” Casey says, “of emotional deserts, or a place or time that seems to lack life.” The desert brings an existential awareness that is ultimately internal. 
The “growth” came from a period of colossal transition for Casey, including the death of his mother. Now 45, Casey had lived in the family home in northwest Detroit all his life until 2021, when a surge of break-ins signaled that it was time to move out. As with all touring artists, the pandemic years also brought on other inner quandaries about the purpose and feasibility of a musician’s life. 
But life does go on, and Casey describes the great theme of Formal Growth In The Desert as an embrace and acknowledgment of that fact: a 12-song testament to “getting on with life,” even when it feels impossibly hard. “I was trying to find a way forward after some pretty heavy things, without lyrically resorting to, Oh my god, my life sucks,” Casey says. “I was trying to see what was beyond the trouble.” The titles of the two opening songs—the moody “Make Way” followed by the charging ennui of “For Tomorrow”—complete that thought. 
The band’s music—more spacious and dynamic than ever—pulled him up, too. Guitarist Greg Ahee, who co-produced Formal Growth In The Desert alongside Jake Aron (Snail Mail, L’Rain), knew what Casey was going through. Conceptualizing the music, he considered how to make it all “like a narrative film.” Having recently scored a pair of short films, Ahee found himself immersed in the cinematic Spaghetti Western music of Ennio Morricone. “I started to write at home on a piano and on a keyboard and then play along to short films, and watch how you can affect and heighten moods as you play,” Ahee explains. 
The filmic sensibility is manifest in Casey’s storytelling, too, whether he’s critiquing ominous techno-capitalism or processing aging, the future, and the possibility of love. Casey calls the centerpiece, “Graft Vs. Host,” written in the immediate wake of his mother’s death, the heaviest song on the record, but it is also among Protomartyr’s most beautiful. It opens with an ominous sprawl before Casey’s sweet, coiling melody buoys the subject matter: “Sadness running through my mind/She wouldn’t want to see me live this way,” he sings, an earnest inquiry into how grief manages to eventually make way for other emotions.
