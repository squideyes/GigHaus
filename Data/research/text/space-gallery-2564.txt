Support SPACE & Membership
Last year alone, SPACE Gallery worked with 1008 artists on our programs, exhibitions, art studios, creative residencies, and regranting program. You can activate our nonprofit mission of arts, artists, and ideas with a charitable donation. SPACE membership signals your shared investment in nourishing a vibrant Maine, elevating artistic voices, and building creative community.
Our nonprofit organization welcomes donations of any size; special perks come with a full membership, learn about membership here. Gift memberships are available. We gladly accept checks issued to SPACE Gallery and mailed to 538 Congress Street, Portland, ME 04101, or dropped off/purchased at one of our programs.
BECOME A MEMBER
DONATE MONTHLY
Champion Our Mission with a Larger Gift
Beginning at tax-deductible gifts of $250, our Insight Members and major donors build art futures in Maine by creating low-barrier access to the arts, championing artistic communities in Maine, and dedicating resources to the next wave of ideas. With your support we can strengthen our facilities while making meaningful jobs and opportunities in the cultural sector. 
BECOME A DONOR
If you are interested in sponsoring programs, making stock or crypto donations, funding special initiatives, or discussing any special giving please contact our Executive Director, Kelsey Halliday Johnson at director@space538.org. For membership questions please contact our Development Coordinator, Meg Hahn at meg@space538.org.
You can be a donor without cash! In-kind donations make a huge difference and support our programs, production capacity, and artist hospitality. To donate your gently used technology, furniture, equipment, or products that you or your business produce, kindly send us photos or a pitch. We deeply appreciate it. 
What will your legacy be? Planned gifts can be made through bequests, naming SPACE as the beneficiary of a retirement plans or life insurance, or having a living will that generously leaves securities, assets or real estate to our nonprofit organization. Help fuel the next generation of artists with your legacy gift to SPACE’s mission. 
SPACE Gallery is a 501(c)(3) public charity, all donations to SPACE are tax deductible. SPACE’s EIN Tax ID number is 51-0432635. We currently hold a Guidestar/Candid Platinum Seal of Transparency for nonprofit reporting in finances, structures, programs, capacity, and impact.

8 films • 101 min.
SPACE presents a Camden International Film Festival encore of their acclaimed Dirigo Docs program, featuring eight Maine-made shorts.
Maine is not just the gathering place for CIFF, but a living, breathing landscape shaped by the storytellers who tend to its memories. Across this year’s selection of locally made short films, stewardship comes alive as a daily practice. From tending to a wooden boat and caring for farm animals to forging Muslim foodways and preserving Passamaquoddy traditions, these stories explore what it means to inherit and sustain this place—and its histories—with intention.
Films
Steward Ship
dir. Luke Kaneb • 8 min.
A young man new to the water tends a wooden boat through every Maine season, learning from an old boatbuilder how a coast is kept.
Sweetgrass
dir. Mike Sockabasin, Emily Karlik • 14 min.
Guided by a tribal historian, a Passamaquoddy elder honors her mother as she introduces the cultural practice of picking sweetgrass to her granddaughter.
Oxbow
dir. Greg Kohs • 23 mins.
From Maine’s quiet trout streams to Tokyo’s neon currents, brewer Tim Adams traces the oxbows of his journey, where life’s longest detours become beautiful confluences.
For a Moment the Clouds Parted and I Felt the Sun
dir. Jared Lank • 8 min.
A visceral and intimate reflection on a Mi’kmaq family’s multigenerational experience of assimilation, told through a collage of archival footage and sonic exploration.
Fish Film
dir. Emma Baiada • 10 min.
Fish Film dives into the impact of salmon aquaculture on Downeast Maine’s wild fish, and is a clarion call to help restore Maine’s local watersheds.
Claws for Concern
dir. Julia Forsyth • 10 min.
As an invasive species threatens Maine’s iconic ecosystems, a waterman, a chef, a science educator—and plenty of advertisers—share their plans to fight back.
May the Water Not Freeze
dir. Zahira Chaudhry • 16 min.
A farming cooperative in Western Maine prepares for their first winter while stewarding community with other Black farmers & cultivating Muslim foodways.
Children & Animals
dir. Walker Kalan • 12 min.
A 12-year old farmer brings his favorite goat to compete at a country fair in Maine – and experiences an existential awakening.

Second show added by popular demand! Tickets to the November 12 evening show may be switched to the Sunday matinee performance (email nick@space538.org)
After a sold-out performance in Downeast Maine, the Portland-based actor, comedian, and educator Anna Conathan brings her “endearing, heart-opening, and hilarious” original show to SPACE.
Bait & Switch, Anna Conathan’s one-person show, unfurls the circuitous story of how she traded her life as a protagonist in Hollywood for a supporting role as a deckhand on a Maine lobster boat.
Drawing from her background in acting, stand-up comedy, and screenwriting, Conathan reels us into her tale of escaping the trap of hollow success and finding her most authentic self elbow deep in bait fish and live lobsters.
In weaving together the divergent strands of her life — Hollywood, motherhood, a misdiagnosed mystery illness, and a nagging desire to get on the water — Conathan finds a life in Maine that’s a “keeper.”
