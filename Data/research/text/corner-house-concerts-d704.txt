SUNDAY, OCTOBER 4
 THE HIGH & WIDES
 7p doors open | 7:30 music
These guys are apostles of hillbilly boogie.
 —Washington Post
The High & Wides make exuberant string-band music, with one foot in bluegrass and the other happily wandering all over the musical landscape.With roots in Baltimore and Maryland’s rural Delmarva peninsula, the quartet draws on old-time, rockabilly, western swing, new wave, and more—imagining a world in which the string band never ceded popular music to electric guitars and drums.Marc Dykeman (guitar, vocals), Sam Guthridge (banjo, mandolin, vocals), Nate Grower (fiddle), and Mike Buccino (bass) bring fiery instrumentals, tight harmonies, original songs, and plenty of drive to music that feels at once deeply rooted and entirely their own.Learn more about The High & Wides!
WEDNESDAY, NOVEMBER 11
 JOE NEWBERRY
 7p doors open | 7:30 music
His lyrics read like a cross between Longfellow and Johnny Cash. The muse is sometimes gentle and other times rough.
 —Independent Weekly
get on the waiting list!
Known around the world for his clawhammer banjo playing, Joe Newberry is also a powerful guitarist, singer, and award-winning songwriter. A longtime and frequent guest on A Prairie Home Companion, he has performed on the Transatlantic Sessions tour and shared an IBMA Song of the Year Award with Eric Gibson for “They Called It Music.”Raised in a family of singers and dancers, Joe learned fiddle tunes from Missouri musicians before moving to North Carolina, where he became an anchor of the state’s extraordinary traditional-music community. More than forty years later, he continues to perform, collaborate, and teach around the world.Don't miss this rare opportunity to hear Joe Newberry's signature blend of sweetness and spice up close and personal!Learn more about the legendary Joe Newberry!

SUNDAY, FEBRUARY 28
 JASON DEPUE
 4p doors open | 4:30 music
...‘progressive bluegrass,’ bending genres while delivering a powerful mix of classical chops, blazing bluegrass, and rock and roll.
 —Hal Real, founder of World Cafe Live
[The DePue Brothers] may call what they produce ‘grassical,’ but we call it serious fun!
 —Joe Patti, WRTI
save your seat!
Violinist Jason DePue is a musical force of nature whose talents range from classical virtuosity to foot-stomping bluegrass and plenty of territory in between. A graduate of the Curtis Institute of Music and longtime member of The Philadelphia Orchestra's first violin section, Jason brings extraordinary technical brilliance, infectious energy, and a delightfully adventurous spirit to everything he plays.Raised in a family of gifted musicians, Jason is also a member of the DePue Brothers Band, known for its exhilarating blend of classical and bluegrass music—a genre they've dubbed "grassical." Whether he's tackling Bach, tearing into a fiddle tune, or exploring something entirely unexpected, Jason makes a compelling case that great music knows no boundaries.We're thrilled to welcome this remarkable Philadelphia musician to Corner House Concerts for what promises to be a dazzling and wonderfully unexpected musical adventure!Learn more about Jason!

SUNDAY, APRIL 25
 ANGELA EASTERLING
 7p doors open | 7:30 music
Great songs. Great singing. Great guitar playing. Everything is so tasty.
 —Jim Lauderdale
save your seat!
South Carolina singer-songwriter Angela Easterling brings a spirited blend of Americana, folk, and traditional country to songs rich in character, history, and heart.A four-time Kerrville New Folk finalist, she's earned acclaim for her vivid storytelling, expressive vocals, and knack for making the past feel startlingly present.Her latest album, Songs from the Knoxville Project, draws on her own family's remarkable Civil War history, weaving Appalachian folk traditions into tales of courage, survival, and compassion. As fellow musician Jim Lauderdale puts it, “Great songs. Great singing. Great guitar playing. Everything is so tasty.”Learn more about Angela!

What's a house concert?
Welp, it's exactly what's on the label: a live music performance that happens in a private home.
It's an intimate and immediate way to experience live music, and you get to share that experience with other cool people who are there to listen.
You get some time to hang out with the audience and the artists, with the accompaniment of complimentary drinx and snax.
Very cool, right?
back to FAQ
Okay, what are YOUR concerts like?
We offer a wide range of music genres—blues, folk, classical, traditional; acoustic or boosted; soloists or small ensembles—and we love to get suggestions!
Most concerts happen on a Saturday or Sunday evening at 7:30 (sometimes 4:30 instead). And weekdays are always an option—stay tuned!
Come a half hour early for complimentary refreshments and hanging out with other guests.
Performances are usually two 40-45 minute sets with a 20-minute break in the middle—refresh yourself and your drink!
Everybody's welcome to hang out for a bit after the show—always great to chat with the artists, right?
There might be merch from the artists; could also be some cool crafts or artwork for sale by our talented pals.
