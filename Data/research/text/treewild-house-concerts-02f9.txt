Treewild House Concerts is an intimate way to see some world-class performers in a comfortable setting. The audience is invited to a delicious vegetarian potluck at 6pm, and music begins at 7. We have desserts and CD/ merch sales at intermission.
Seating is limited. All the ticket proceeds go directly to the musicians. Treewild is committed to supporting independent musicians making the world better through the art and craft of creating delightful music! 
We usually host one band each month. October is for BORDERLANDS!

November 2nd - Cécilia! 
Traditional music and French-Canadian step dancing that causes a wonderful stir and fills to overflowing at every venue – that is Cécilia! 
Widely recognized as three of Canada’s finest musicians, it is a pleasure to see how much enjoyment Timi Turmel, accordion, Erin Leahy, piano and Louis Schryer on fiddle, find in playing together. Every one of their pieces is skillfully arranged with influences from contemporary, classical, jazz and swing styles. 
Cécilia’s recent debut album Accent is an impressive offering full of vibrant rhythms and melodies, all resonating with a unique warmth and richness of expression. 
Timi Turmel
Around the world, Timi Turmel is considered a master of the accordion. He grew up in a family of talented musicians, including his father, Jean-Marc, and his uncle, Robin, who inspired in Timi a deep passion for the instrument. So deep tha teen as a child, in the minutes waiting for the school bus each morning, Timi would pick up his accordion to play. From a young age, he began performing on stages and in many important competitions throughout Quebec and the US. Crowned Que ecois Champion for three consecutive years, Timi went on to join Mackinaw, the distinguished Quebecois company of traditional dance. For many years, in his role as accordionist, pianist and musical director, Timi played in festivals all over Europe.
Erin Leahy
Canadian musician, composer and arranger, Erin Leahy has earned a reputation for her exceptional talent and versatility. She is a powerhouse on the piano, playing traditional, Celtic, ragtime, classical, and jazz-influenced music. Erin also plays violin with the same command and musicality.
As a member of the internationally renowned Canadian band LEAHY, Erin and her siblings have enjoyed a long and distinguished musical career. They have worldwide sales of more than half a million albums and have received numerous awards, including three JUNOS. With extensive touring, their sound and spirit have garnered fans from around the globe.
 Louis Schryer (May be Tom Fitzgerald or Xavier Leahy on fiddle in lieu of Louis)
Multi-award winning Canadian champion, Louis Schryer stands out as one of this generation’s most respected and sought-after fiddle players. He is both the only 8-time Canadian Fiddle Champion and the only artist to hold the title of 4-time Grand Masters Fiddle Champion.
Known for his soulful touch and exhilarating spontaneity, Louis enchants audiences each time his bow crosses the strings of his violin. With what can only be described as masterful stylistic diversity, Louis’ playing elegantly spans the spectrum of violin music. His foundation in traditional, Québecois, Cape Breton, and Irish fiddle styles, as well as swing and classical genres, has shaped his unique sound. Whether Louis performs as a solo artist, in a small group or with an orchestra, his talent is virtuosic.
“When Cecilia arrived on stage they had the audience in the palm of their hand within the first notes of their playing. Their steady mastery of the music, their warm connection to the crowd, and the smiles and obvious enjoyment in the hall afterward made me feel like I’m doing a good job as a music presenter. They are simply wonderful musicians!”
-Stephen Farish, Polly Plumb Productions
December 19th Dandling 
Fiddler and singer Lissa Schneckenburger teams up with percussive dance innovator Nic Gareiss and multi-instrumentalist Garrett Cameron for a dynamic new collaboration rooted in tradition and injected with playful creativity. The trio calls themselves Dandling, and blends driving fiddle tunes, inventive rhythms, and spontaneous musical interplay as they explore traditional music and original compositions. Dandling draws it’s name from the verb meaning to move an infant up and down on the knee or in the arms in a playful way, or to pamper or please someone, and they hope their music will bring playfulness, solace, and joy to their listeners. Drawing from several of Schneckenburger’s recent albums of new instrumentals and songs, the trio will inspire you with their virtuosic interpretations and unique instrumentation.
Gareiss, named one of Dance Magazine’s “25 to Watch,” brings a dazzling percussive dance style rooted in clogging, flatfooting, and step dance, transforming the stage into both concert and rhythmic playground. Cameron adds rich textures on vibraphone, weaving together influences from Scottish and Cape Breton traditions, Minimalism, and improvisational groove.
The result is a high-energy collaboration that moves effortlessly from foot-stomping, dance-driven excitement to hauntingly beautiful moments of reflection—an unforgettable evening where music and movement spark off one another in real time.

January 17th Aaron Marcus & Sam Sanders 
Aaron Marcus and Sam Sanders CD release
piano and concertina accompanying spoken word.
from 7-Days
That sensation and spirit suffuses Aaron Marcus’ latest collection of piano compositions, Garden Dreams, on which the Montpelier musician collaborated with former Vermont Public Radio host/engineer Sam Sanders. The two curate a collection of work by Vermont poets — plus cameos from Emily Dickinson and Tennessee Williams — which Sanders reads in an easygoing, casually frank tone. The Montpelier-based Sanders wields the poems as if they were his own, his voice resonant with a hint of world weariness.
Though poetry and music…are… intertwined, what Marcus and Sanders achieve … is its own kind of magic. The music is composed and arranged in such a way as to draw attention to the words of the poems, yet it never comes across as background music. It feels more like documenting life as it happens.

February 14th Skylark Quartet 
SKYLARK is an innovative contemporary string ensemble based in northern Vermont. With decades of experience in the professional music community, Skylark’s sound and repertoire challenge the boundaries between classical, folk and modern idioms, creating inspired original works for all audiences. Influenced by the Nordic, Celtic and American regions, Skylark’s programs feature arrangements and compositions by sonic trailblazers such as Väsen, the Danish and Turtle Island String Quartets, Dreamers’ Circus, Pete Sutherland, and many more. At its heart, Skylark’s music is melody-driven with unique energy, irresistible flair and undeniable beauty. 
March 31st Arthur Coates and Kerran Cotterell 
Arthur Coates & Kerran Cotterell are an Anglo-Scottish folk duo whose live shows combine traditional music with original material from our latest album, Controlled Burn. Audiences often comment on the surprisingly big sound that comes from just two musicians, alongside the humour, musicianship and on-stage chemistry that have become a hallmark of our performances. Following one of our industry showcase performances at FOCUS Wales this year, we were described as having “200 festival-goers crammed into the room and in the palm of their hands.”
Arthur Coates & Kerran Cotterell are a two-man trad tornado tearing through the folk world with foot percussion, fiddle, guitar, vocals, and groove to spare. Kitted out with grooving basslines, tight harmonies, and offbeat charm, they’re just a couple of lads with a tangle of strings – yet they somehow summon the sound and swagger of a much bigger band. Their music blends original compositions with tunes and songs they’ve found in the wild, delivered with harmonies, tall tales, and a streak of serious silliness.
Arthur, a fiddler raised in Aberdeenshire, builds rolling, riff-heavy tunes grounded in his family’s musical traditions and shaped further by studies with Jonny Hardie (Old Blind Dogs).

Tickets 
Treewild House Concerts aims to build community around fabulous music. To that end, we begin with a vegetarian potluck. Dinner is at 6, and music starts at 7.
All the ticket proceeds goes directly to the musicians. If you find you are unable to come, feel free to find someone to take your place, or rest with the great feeling that you are supporting the art of music in our community.
Our audiences are limited to 30. If enough people join the waitlist, we’ll ask the musicians if they want to run a second concert. If that happens, we’ll let you know.
You will not receive an actual “ticket” but your PayPal confirmation is your receipt. At the ticket table, we will have the list of registrations.
