About Hotel Turkey
A nearly 100-year-old building, a family who took a chance on it, and a town that never stopped showing up.

1927
Built for the Railroad
H.B. Jordan built Hotel Turkey three blocks from the Fort Worth and Denver South Plains Railway depot as a place for ranchers, traveling salesmen, and anyone passing through the Panhandle. However, on June 12, a tornado leveled the building before it could even host its first guest. They got back to work rebuilding and in November, Hotel Turkey was open for business.
1927
The Town Showed Up
From day one, the hotel was more than a place to sleep. Salesmen used the meeting room to spread their wares. Dances and banquets filled the dining room. Turkey, Texas, made it their living room.
1971
Bob Wills Day Begins
Turkey started hosting Bob Wills Day in 1971 to honor the king of Western swing who claimed Turkey as his hometown. Hotel Turkey became the natural home base for the musicians and visitors who made the trip every last Saturday in April. Except for a COVID interruption in 2020 and 2021, it hasn't missed a year since.
1991
Made It Official
Hotel Turkey is added to the National Register of Historic Places. Nearly 65 years of West Texas history — recognized.
1994
A Future President Stops In
George W. Bush visited Turkey on October 29, 1994, during his campaign for Texas Governor. He stayed at Hotel Turkey and met with local residents and farmers. He won the election two weeks later.
2015
Pat & Tina Take the Reins
Local residents Pat and Tina Carson purchase the hotel from Turkey rancher Cody Bell. Their goal: keep it standing, keep it full, and make it something the town is proud of.
Today
Still at It
Historic rooms, Steel Suites, RV hookups, live music every weekend, and a restaurant worth the drive. The building's almost 100 years old and busier than ever.
The Hotel Turkey Crew
Owners
Pat & Tina Carson
Pat and Tina are locals — between the two of them, they've been part of the Turkey community for over 30 years. Tina grew up here, her family farming cotton, peanuts, and watermelon just down the road in Flomot. Pat found his way to Turkey the way a lot of people do: through family and a town too small to stay strangers in. Proud Red Raiders, they've raised their three kids in Turkey and wouldn’t want to be anywhere else.
A chance conversation in 2015 with then-owner Cody Bell about what it might be like to run a historic hotel turned into reality when Bell called them up a few months later with an offer. They took it, with a simple idea: take care of the building, take care of the guests, and take care of the town.
The Crew
The People Who Make It Feel Like Home
Carley du Menil became a Turkey resident in 2018 and has been an integral part of Hotel Turkey ever since. As the one who keeps the day-to-day humming, she's probably the first face you'll see when you arrive and the reason everything runs as smoothly as it does.
Albert has been at Hotel Turkey since before the Carsons took over, and it's no coincidence that he's the one making breakfast. His personality alone will get your day started on the right foot. If you've had breakfast at Hotel Turkey, you've met Albert — and you probably haven't stopped talking about him since.
Kristy has been waiting tables since day one, and if you've ever eaten here or spent a night on the back patio, there's a good chance she's the one who took care of you!
Every member of the Hotel Turkey team keeps this place running. And there is no doubt that the crew at Hotel Turkey are a big part why guests keep coming back.
Friends of the Hotel
The Ones Who Just Show Up
Hotel Turkey has never been a solo operation. When the Travel Channel called shortly after the Carsons took over, they had 12 days to get the hotel ready for filming. Friends, family, and neighbors showed up and worked long days and nights to make it happen. That spirit never really left.
Family members have been in the mix from the start, helping cook, taking orders, setting up, and lending a hand in whatever way was needed.
And then there's Neal, Ben, Steve, Jose, and Rob — the steak cooking crew. They show up every Saturday night to make sure the grill is running right, not because they have to, but because that's just what you do around here. Hotel Turkey wouldn't be what it is without them.
What We're Known For
Historic Character
A two-story brick prairie school building that's been standing since 1927. Original architecture, wood floors, and walls that have heard a few good tales. On the National Register of Historic Places and still going strong.
Live Music Every Weekend
Bands and songwriters roll through from all over the country, every Friday and Saturday night on the back patio. Cold beer at the Silo Bar and the Turkey, Texas, sky overhead. Come see who's playing.
Good Food, Made Right
From a proper West Texas breakfast to Steak Night on Saturdays, the kitchen does things right. You don't have to be a guest to eat like one.
West Texas Hospitality
Family-owned, community-run, and genuinely glad you made the drive. Everyone who walks through the door gets treated like they've been here before.

WHAT FOLKS ARE SAYING
This century old hotel is NOT a boutique hotel with modern fixtures and a total makeover; it's a sturdy gem built in a bygone era.When you get out of bed at night and tiptoe to the bathroom, the floors creak and the door scrapes shut. The doorknobs jiggle, the hardwood floors have telltale scars where they have been patched, and the quaint Victorian armchair is threadbare.Once you appreciate the authenticity as charm, you'll further appreciate they've made the smart updates: modern HVAC, insulated windows (no noise from the street below), the incredibly comfortable beds, the scented bedsheets, and room darkening shades. We slept soundly! We stayed 3 nights for the Bob Wills Days the last week of April, it was a full house yet staff treated us like special guests. Hotel Turkey served excellent breakfasts and dinner. They bent over backwards to be accommodating as heavy rains altered activities, adapting and adjusting with congeniality!
GEORGIA DUNCAN
A cool, funky hotel with tons of character and an amazing staff.If you enjoy good food, good music and friendly people this is your spot.Expect more of a European style hotel experience, yet uniquely Texan than something mass produced like a Holiday Inn. You'll be on a first name basis and sharing laughs with at least one of the staff members by the time you leave.

The colors were vibrant, along with turquoise mini fridge, and replica Victrola, that we played a George Strait LP. The walk in shower was modern and roomy.
