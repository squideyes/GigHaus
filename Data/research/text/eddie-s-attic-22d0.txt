 About Us 
Where Live Music Matters
Since 1991, Eddie’s Attic has been a popular and intimate live music venue for aspiring and accomplished performing songwriters. Located in Decatur, Georgia, a small artist-friendly town within the metro Atlanta area, the Attic is a true listening room with a superior sound system and acoustics, where customers are asked to silence their phones and actually listen to the songs. Eddie’s Attic draws both fans and artists from all over the world, and continues to be one of the premier music venues in the nation.
And it has been that way from the very beginning.
What started as a bare second-floor space just off Decatur Square became something nobody quite had a name for at the time. A place where the music wasn’t background noise, where the artist on stage deserved your full attention, and where the audience showed up because they genuinely wanted to hear something. Over thirty years and thousands of shows later, that founding idea remains unchanged.
This stage has been an early home to artists who went on to fill arenas, win Grammys, and change the landscape of American music. It has also been the place where countless songwriters you may not have heard of yet played their most honest set to a quiet, full room of people who were really listening. Both things matter equally here.
Our Bi-Annual Open Mic Shootout, now in its 58th edition, has been running for over two decades and remains one of the most beloved traditions in Atlanta’s music community. Each week, new voices earn their shot at the stage. Each Shootout, the room fills with industry professionals, devoted fans, and fellow artists who understand that the next great songwriter could be standing right in front of them.
We do 600 to 700 shows a year. The room holds 177 people. The stage is small, the sound system is exceptional, and the distance between you and the artist performing is measured in feet. That intimacy is not incidental. It is the whole point.
Eddie’s Attic is a community space in the truest sense, rooted in Decatur, open to the world, and devoted to the idea that live music, heard properly, can change a room and sometimes a life.
Come listen. We’d love to have you.

On any given night, music lovers can hear talented performing songwriters and bands playing original music in an intimate setting. The Attic welcomes all varieties of original, live music and embraces the diversity that allows independent music to thrive.
In its first 19 years, Eddie’s Attic has been the springboard for an amazing number of local artists who have gone on to receive national recognition – names like John Mayer, Sugarland, Shawn Mullins, The Civil Wars, and Justin Bieber.
The list of nationally known talents who have played this stage is also impressive – names like the Indigo Girls, John Gorka, John McEuen, Sheryl Crow, Ani DiFranco, Brandi Carlile, The Black Crowes, Al Stewart, David Wilcox, Bill Mallonee, Kevn Kinney, Edwin McCain, Richard Shindell, Ellis Paul, Francine Reed, Patty Larkin, Malcomb Holcombe, Larry Jon Wilson, Eric Taylor, Glen Phillips, Dana Cooper, Billy Joe Shaver, Pierce Pettis, Kate Campbell, Cheryl Wheeler, Cliff Eberhardt, Vance Gilbert, Lowen & Navarro, India Arie, Brian Vander Ark, and Don Conoscenti.
Eddie’s Attic is a true community center for Decatur as a city, and for performing songwriters throughout metro Atlanta. The management and staff are very appreciative of the customers and artists who have supported Eddie’s Attic over the years. Whether it’s for the amazing original live music in the music room, or for the friendliest patio space in Decatur, you should expect outstanding food, drink, and service, and you should expect to have the time of your life. Come see us soon, and see why Live Music Matters.

 Getting Here
 Ticketing
How do I buy tickets for a show?
Advance tickets for all shows can be purchased online at www.eddiesattic.com. When purchasing online, please read the description on your ticket before purchasing.
For day of show tickets, you can come into the venue as early as 5pm to purchase a ticket through our door person. PLEASE NOTE: online prices and tickets purchased in person are exactly the same. THERE IS NO FEE DIFFERENCE.
Is the venue wheelchair accessible?
Because our building is historic, we do not have elevators. The venue is only accessible via the stairs.
I bought tickets, but now I can’t find them.
You won’t need any sort of physical ticket to get into a show – we’ll just look you up by your last name at the door. If you’re concerned about whether you have tickets, you’re welcome to ask us to confirm at info@eddiesattic.com
If I purchased general admission tickets, does that mean there will be seating for me?
General admission tickets DO NOT guarantee seating. It is on a first come first serve basis. If you’ve bought general admission tickets, we recommend getting to the venue as close to 5:00PM (4PM on Sundays) as possible because that is when ticket check-in begins and therefore is your best bet for grabbing seats.
I’m under 21. Can I come to a show?
Yes! Our shows are all ages unless otherwise noted.
If I purchase a table, do I also need to purchase tickets?
No. When you pay to reserve a table, it includes 4 tickets for the 4 seats at the table. If you have more than 4 people in your group, you will need to reserve additional tables or general admission tickets.
How do I purchase a table?
When purchasing tickets, make sure you select the ticketing type titled “Table Seating.” That will automatically add 4 tickets to your cart. This is the ONLY way to reserve a table.
If you purchase 4 general admission tickets, that DOES NOT equate to a table. Your ticket type MUST read “Table Seating.” If you’re wanting to purchase a table, make sure that you have selected “Table Seating” BEFORE you check out. On the Dice ticketing page, General Admission is *typically* listed first, so you must select “Table Seating,” which is listed underneath “General Admission,” to purchase a table.
What if there are two shows happening in one night?
Eddie’s Attic often has two shows happening in one night – an early show and a late show. These shows are completely separate and have nothing to do with the other. If you purchase tickets to the early show, your tickets are valid ONLY for the early show. Therefore if you’d like to attend both early and late shows, you must purchase tickets to BOTH shows.
Is the kitchen open?
The Eddie’s Attic kitchen is open!
Hours of operation:
Monday: 5:00pm – 10pm
Tuesday: 5:00pm – 10pm
Wednesday: 5:00pm – 10pm
Thursday: 5:00pm – 10pm
Friday: 5:00pm – 11pm
Saturday: 5:00pm – 11pm
Sunday 5pm – 9pm
(Kitchen is only open on nights when there is a show)
When can I check in for the show I have tickets for?
Monday-Saturday: 5:00pm
Sunday: 4:00pm
Check-in time is applicable to BOTH early and late shows. If you are attending the late show, you are still able to start checking in at 5PM (or 4PM on Sundays).
If you would like to choose a specific table, you must arrive between check-in and doors. Any tables not checked-in by the time doors open will be chosen for you.
Once you have selected your table or received your general admission number, come back at door time to be let into the music room for the show.
Why is DICE only giving me the waitlist option?
If a ticket link only gives you the option to join the waitlist, that means that show is SOLD OUT. Here’s more info on how the waitlist works: The Wait List Explained
Do I have to download an app to buy tickets?
No. The ticketing platform we use, DICE, will require you to create an account, but it is unnecessary to download their app for our venue. Once you create an account, you should be able to purchase tickets through your web browser.
Can I rent out the venue for a private event?
Yes you can! Please email info@eddiesattic.com with details of your event and the date you’re looking to book.
How do I sign up for the open mic?
Sign-up forms are on this page: https://eddiesattic.com/sign-up/
Sign-ups are posted monthly and fill up quickly. If you’d like a heads up before we post next months, please follow us on Instagram and Facebook @eddiesattic.com because we post a heads up on our stories.
Please pay attention to all details and guidelines before signing up for an open mic.
Where do I send booking requests?
Please direct all booking requests to bookings@eddiesattic.com
Can I reserve a table?
Our tables are reserved, but not assigned and are available only through advanced purchase. If you would like to choose a specific table, you must arrive between check-in and doors. Any tables not checked-in by the times doors open will be chosen for you. Table check-in starts at 5pm Monday-Saturday, and 4pm on Sundays,
NOTE: THE ONLY WAY TO GUARANTEE SEATING IS BY RESERVING A TABLE IN ADVANCE.
Where do I park?
While we don’t have a designated parking lot, Downtown Decatur has plenty of public parking. Please see a full list of options and prices here: https://www.decaturga.com/cd/page/parking
Some city parking is free after 6pm and on the weekends – please pay attention to all signage before parking.
Construction is ongoing on McDonough Street next to us, but the street is NOT fully closed and the venue is still easily accessible. The paid lot behind us (in between our building and Decatur Square) is still open, but we’d like to caution that the lot is electronically enforced and their tickets are hefty. If you do park back there, please pay attention to when your parking time is up.
Can I take pictures during the show?
Absolutely! However, flash photography is not allowed. Please inform us if you are recording video with a professional set-up as we may not have the space to accommodate your equipment during the show. Don’t forget to share your photos & videos with us online!
I bought tickets to a show but now I can’t come. Can I get a refund?
We do not offer refunds unless a show is canceled or rescheduled. All sales are final. If there has been a significant change to the event such as a location change or date change, we will send an announcement to all ticket holders with details regarding refunds. Please check your email for this event announcement before proceeding. If an event has been changed and the announcement includes a deadline for refund requests, we cannot refund orders submitted after that deadline. We cannot offer refunds due to scheduling conflicts, illness, or other personal issues. For any additional questions contact info@eddiesattic.com. 
Is it possible I will have to stand during the show?
Yes. Due to the popularity of our shows, there is a possibility that some of our General Admission patrons will have to stand during the performances. Please remember that General Admission tickets do not guarantee seating. Please plan on arriving early to try to grab a bar stool or stadium seat. Table seating DOES guarantee seating.
What is the music room capacity?
175.
Can I add a 5th person to my table?
We only allow up to 4 people to sit at each table. If your group is larger than 4, we recommend purchasing a second table to ensure everyone has a seat. We will do our best to place multiple tables together but we cannot guarantee it, so checking in early as normal is still advised.
Can I reserve bar stools?
Bar stools are not reserved, but are general admission seating which means that they are first-come-first-serve. If you prefer a specific bar stool, please check in with our door person close to when the doors open. The only way to reserve a seat is to purchase a table, which is available for purchase until 5pm on the day of the show.
Who do I contact if I have additional questions?
info@eddiesattic.com

Elijah Fox Trio live at Eddie’s Attic w/ Kenny Banks Jr.!
Solo Smith on Bass Myles Martin on Drums Elijah Fox is a multi-instrumentalist, singer, and producer based in Los Angeles, CA. He grew up in Durham, NC, and was mentored by Yusuf Salim, a pianist who had played with Charlie Parker. His piano composition “East Village” was sampled by Drake and 21 Savage on “Major Distribution,” reaching number 3 on the Billboard Charts. He has recorded and produced for SZA, Masego, Childish Gambino, Kali Uchis, J Cole, and many others, while his improvisation “Wyoming” went viral on TikTok with over 45 million Spotify streams. His sound blends jazz, Impressionism, and psychedelic soul. Atlanta pianist Kenny Banks Jr. is one of the most dynamic jazz artists in the Southeast, performing and recording with artists including Kurt Elling, Theo Croker, and Queen Latifah. Downbeat Magazine calls his arrangements “thrilling, modern and surprising.”
Important event info: The Purchaser/ ticket holder must bring a photo ID to will-call to check-in for the show. If you transfer your tickets to another patron, we request that they bring their photo ID. Check-in begins at 5 PM.

How did East Nash Grass follow up their 2024 International Bluegrass Music Association (IBMA) award for Best New Artist? By earning a nomination for Entertainer of the Year, IBMA’s highest honor, in 2025, and celebrating fiddler Maddie Denton’s win as Fiddler of the Year. In short, East Nash Grass have quickly established themselves as the fresh new face of Nashville’s storied bluegrass tradition, a supergroup of rising stars: Grand Master Fiddle champion Denton is joined by banjo player Cory Walker (banjo player on the five-time nominated “Carter & Cleveland” album this year, among numerous accolades), 2022 IBMA Momentum Instrumentalist of the Year Harry Clark, and new guitar player Frank Rische. This is an all-star band that harkens back to the sort of talent collected in the original Blue Grass Boys and a powerhouse leader of the next generation in bluegrass excellence.
Rolling Stone had them among the “best of what we saw” at RockyGrass in 2025, praising their “rowdy set of high-octane bluegrass numbers,” and noting, “The group itself is a rapidly-rising force on the national touring scene.”
And “rowdy” is right. They are far more than just a group of impressive instrumental technicians, building on night after night of three-set gigs on Nashville’s East Side, playing tiny stages for the love of it between tours backing bigger acts. It’s a hard-forged bond that translates into an infectious on-stage energy that audiences can’t seem to get enough of.
With their new release, “All God’s Children” (Mountain Fever, August 22, 2025), they show a depth and feel, too, that goes well beyond the traditional bluegrass fair, with nods to Gypsy jazz and East Village folk, and eye toward moving bluegrass forward and keeping it from being too mired in the past. If East Nash Grass isn’t the future of the genre it’s only because they’re already among its leading lights.
