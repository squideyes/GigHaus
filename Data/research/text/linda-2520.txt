WAMC On The Road
WAMC On The Road brings our microphones out of the soundproof booth and into the beating hearts of the communities we serve. This exciting endeavor meets listeners beyond the airwaves, capturing the energy and authenticity of live, on-location events throughout WAMC’s seven-state listening region, from Plattsburgh to Pittsfield to Poughkeepsie. From libraries and colleges to theaters, town halls, festivals, and cultural landmarks, WAMC On The Road is our commitment to meeting listeners where they work, live, and grow.

 WAMC on the Road at Crandell Theatre - Panel on Journalism and the film 'All the President's Men'
 Joe Donahue
Few films have captured the work, responsibility, and power of investigative journalism as vividly as 'All the President's Men.' Released in 1976 and directed by Alan Pakula, the film stars Robert Redford and Dustin Hoffman, as Washington Post reporters Bob Woodward and Carl Bernstein, whose painstaking investigation of the Watergate break-in helped uncover a political scandal that ultimately led to the resignation of President Richard Nixon.Based on Woodward and Bernstein's book, the film received eight Academy Award nominations, it won four Oscars. Fifty years later, it remains a landmark portrait of journalism, of cultivating sources, verifying facts, confronting powerful institutions, and following a story wherever it leads. The Library of Congress added the film to the National Film Registry in 2010.Last weekend WAMC and the Crandell Theatre in Chatham teamed up for a special screening of all the president's men, followed by a conversation about the film and the current state of journalism and investigative reporting. It was a full house, and our panel brought together four people with very different perspectives on the press, politics, and public life.

 Weekdays, 4-6 p.m. and weekends, 5-6 p.m.
All Things Considered is the most listened-to, afternoon drive-time, news radio program in the country. Every weekday the two-hour show is hosted by Ailsa Chang, Mary Louise Kelly and Ari Shapiro. During each broadcast, stories and reports come to listeners from NPR reporters and correspondents based throughout the United States and the world. The hosts interview newsmakers and contribute their own reporting. Rounding out the mix are the disparate voices of a variety of commentators.

This daily module features cutting-edge research from academic institutions around the world and is hosted by AAC&U President Dr. Lynn Pasquerella. The module covers a wide array of subjects to help us better understand the world around us through academic research.
Learn more at academicminute.org.

 The Book Show
 Tuesdays, 11:30am -12:00 p.m.; Thursdays, 8pm - 8:30pm 
 Hosted by Joe Donahue
Each week on The Book Show, host Joe Donahue interviews authors about their books, their lives and their craft. It is a celebration of both reading and writers. Joe holds interesting conversations with a variety of authors including Malcolm Gladwell, Lawrence Wright, and Emily St. John Mandel.
As the son of a librarian, Joe has been part of the book world since childhood. His first job was as a library assistant, during college he was a clerk at an independent book store and for the past 25 years he has been interviewing authors about their books on the radio.
He is also the host of The Roundtable on WAMC Northeast Public Radio, a 3-hour general interest talk show. Notable authors he has interviewed include: Kurt Vonnegut, John Irving, John Updike, Alice Walker, Toni Morrison, Arthur Miller, Stephen King, Amy Tan, Anne Rice, Philip Roth, E.L Doctorow, Richard Russo, David Sedaris and Maya Angelou. 
Joe has won several awards for his interviews, including honors from the Associated Press, the Edward R. Murrow Awards, the New York State Association of Broadcasters, The Headliners, The National Press Club and the Scripps-Howard Foundation.

 Earth Wise
 Weekdays, 11:10 a.m. and 4:04 p.m.
 Hosted by Randy Simon
From green business and new environmental legislation to how nature impacts our environment in ways never before considered, Earth Wise offers a look at our changing environment.
 Earth Wise 
 Battery-powered apartments
 Randy Simon
A Buffalo New York apartment building has installed a battery system that stores excess energy when it is plentiful and can power the building when the grid is strained and electricity is costly. The Peace Bridge Place affordable housing complex has added a 3.75-megawatt lithium-ion battery system that can power the complex for several days.
 Listen • 2:00
 Earth Wise 
 Global wildlife populations
 Randy Simon
The latest “Living Planet” report by the World Wildlife Fund and the Zoological Society of London finds that the populations of vertebrate species across the globe have decreased in size by 73% on average over the past 50 years. Factors contributing to this decline include habitat loss, overexploitation, and climate change.
 Listen • 2:00
 Earth Wise 
 Antarctic ice and tropical warmth
 Randy Simon
Antarctica is known for losing ice. But between 2021 and 2023, something very unusual happened: the Antarctic Ice Sheet gained about 695 billion tons of mass, a historic anomaly spanning more than two decades of continuous satellite monitoring.
 Listen • 2:00
 Earth Wise 
 Electric school buses in New York City
 Randy Simon
The U.S. has nearly half a million school buses, and for most of them, school districts are struggling with record diesel prices. As of this fall, however, 10 new buses in East New York are plugging into chargers instead of filling up their fuel tanks.
 Listen • 2:00
 Earth Wise 
 Alaska's orange rivers
 Randy Simon
More than 200 rivers and streams in Alaska have exhibited bright orange plumes in the water. Since 2019, researchers have found that the water was even more acidic and metallic than the water that leaches from metal mines. Many of these rivers and streams are within national parks and preserves, so these are pristine watersheds, not places where mining activities took place.
 Listen • 2:00
 Earth Wise 
 Turning golf courses into solar farms
 Randy Simon
Japan is mountainous and densely populated and has little land available for large solar farms. As a result, solar developers are building solar arrays on disused golf courses.
 Listen • 2:00
 Earth Wise 
 Next-generation geothermal goes live at utility scale
 Randy Simon
Geothermal energy has historically tapped into natural volcanic reservoirs of heat. Such places are a source of continuous energy that can be used to generate electricity or provide heat. The problem is that these places are few and far between.
 Listen • 2:00
 Earth Wise 
 Lake Powell at a record low
 Randy Simon
Lake Powell is the second-largest man-made reservoir in the United States. It stretches 186 miles along the Colorado River, straddling the border of southeastern Utah and northeastern Arizona. It was formed behind Glen Canyon Dam, which was built in the 1960s.
 Listen • 2:00
 Earth Wise 
 The New World screwworm is on the move
 Randy Simon
A flesh-eating fly that was once eradicated from the United States is moving north again, raising concerns for livestock, wildlife, pets, and in rare cases people.
 Listen • 2:00
 Earth Wise 
 Disappearing beaches
 Randy Simon
Beaches form when waves and currents deposit loose sediment like sand, gravel, and shell fragments along a shoreline. They erode when strong wave action or rising water strips that material away. Beaches erode; it’s what they do. For them not to disappear entirely, there needs to be a continuing supply of sediment. Rivers carry sediment and sand from inland areas down to the coast. Ocean waves contribute by breaking down offshore reefs and coastal cliffs into small particles.
 Listen • 2:00
