Along the banks of the Cacapon River, you’ll find a little orange house filled with a community inspired by the arts. Step inside our Art Café, where you’re greeted with warm smiles, the aroma of freshly brewed West Virginia–roasted coffee, and the taste of seasonal food specials made with care.
Stay awhile to enjoy our rotating gallery and artist consignment spaces, and explore your own creativity by making table art or trying out our wall of instruments.
Our indoor and outdoor stages host performances that bring people together; from live music and storytelling, to open mics and festivals. Through free and low-cost programming like our weekly Art-for-All program and monthly volunteer-led clubs, we invite everyone to create, learn, and connect. Surrounded by a talented community of artists, musicians, and makers, inspiration is never far away.
We look forward to meeting you, sharing stories, and hearing what inspires you. Because at The River House, every visit, every meal, and every shared song strengthens the creative spirit that connects us all.

Scottish-born, Appalachian-based singer-songwriter and guitarist Ali Tod returns to The River House for a special hometown show celebrating the release of her debut solo album.
Produced by acclaimed guitarist Kaki King, the new record marks a major new chapter for Ali, combining powerful songwriting and raw vocals with the virtuosic, percussive guitar playing and live looping that have become the signature of her electrifying live shows.
After a year of touring in the US and Europe, Ali brings it all back home to the Cacapon River for an intimate night at The River House, joined by special guest Isaac McCarthy.
Raised in Berkeley County, West Virginia, Isaac McCarthy is an award-winning Appalachian performer, educator, and writer. ￼ His artistry has won accolades from MidAtlantic Arts, WV Arts, and first place at The River House Open Mic Contest. As a dance caller and multi instrumentalist specializing in three finger banjo, he has shared stages with artists like The Wild Shoats and The Mighty Troubadours,￼ bringing his unique blend of Appalachian sound to audiences across the region.

The River House Writer’s Club is a monthly informal gathering for all genres of writing including poetry, short stories, prose and so much more. Each month, the club meets to discuss each other’s writings and develop friendships. 
Author Kristin Tesdall will join this months meeting! 
Kristin Tesdall grew up near Winchester, Virginia with a big imagination and no less than seven imaginary friends. She became infatuated with books at a young age and dreamed of writing the kind of books kids like her would want to read. Her debut children’s book, A Bushel of Days, is a (mostly) fictional ode to her childhood. Her second book, Waddling Ways, is a non-fiction title about raising ducks and geese.
When she isn’t writing, Kristin can be found snuggling sheep, collecting more books than her shelves can hold, and beginning new craft projects without finishing the ones she already started. She lives with her husband and three children on their small farm in Virginia’s Shenandoah Valley where they manage unruly flocks of chickens, ducks, geese, and sheep.
Every other month, the club hosts an established writer who shares their experiences and suggestions with the group. We continue to grow in attendance and look forward to new opportunities in the future. In the past few months, we have been invited to attend book festivals and these events have been invaluable to the club. 
Our mission is to foster creative writing on all levels. Everyone is welcome to participate or just come out and listen. 
This club meets the 3rd Sunday of each month from 1-3:00pm and we cannot wait to see you there!

FAQ's
What is The River House?
The River House is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization located in a historic building overlooking the Cacapon River in Capon Bridge, West Virginia.
Our mission is to provide a fun, welcoming space where participation in the Arts inspires deep community connections and nourishes the lives of individuals and families. 
The River House is a community-based, arts and music venue. Also featuring a cafe, it is a place to connect and create together. The River House hosts live performances, art classes, community gatherings and an open workspace. In addition to a wide range of art and music activities for all ages, local artists enjoy a venue for demonstrating and selling their work. Free wi-fi plus a nourishing local pastries and an espresso bar add to the welcoming “sit and stay awhile” feeling.
Why is there a charge for concerts and food if The River House is a nonprofit?
Although many events are free, money generated from the café and some performances covers the cost of expenses and is invested back into more programming. A goal of any nonprofit is to achieve a positive revenue flow to build a reserve fund to ensure sustainability.
How do concert tickets work?
We sell 40 Advance tickets at $18 to ensure indoor seating if needed. If a show is outdoors, additional Door tickets are $22. For uncertain weather, we will decide by 3 PM on show day, with extra tickets available at the venue. Check our event page or social media for updates.
How is The River House funded?
The River House is funded by various sources, including individual donors, businesses, and private and state grants. The largest portion comes from individual donors. Donations are tax-deductible.
The River House is proud of the support from many local people and businesses who give small (and big) donations because they see the importance of The River House as a part of the community and believe in the benefits it brings to the community.
Additional funding comes from the small foundations. The River House also has received support from grants from the State of West Virginia. In addition, during the pandemic, The River House received funding from federal programs designed to keep people employed and operations continuing.
The River House is located on the Cacapon River. Does it get preferential treatment regarding land usage?
No. The River House complies with all land-use laws and regulations.
Can local folks use the river access from The River House parking lot?
Parking space at The River House is extremely limited. People who want to wade in the river or launch a canoe or kayak are welcome to do so from the small access point at The River House. All persons must do so at their own risk. After unloading, we ask that people move their vehicles to another parking area. Fortunately, other local organizations like the Capon Bridge Library, allow The River House patrons to use their parking lots when they are closed. 
There is a new Public Access Site for the Cacapon River just upstream from The River House. This site is only ½ mile south of Route 50 on Christian Church Road. The one-acre site features parking and a canoe launch.
What are some positive impacts that The River House has on the community?
The River House strives to be a positive part of Capon Bridge, Hampshire County and the broader region. It supports local businesses and directly employs 10 local people. In 2020, The River House contributed more than $14,000 to local food producers and provided more than $20,000 in revenue to artists and musicians. 
Is The River House only for artsy folks? 
The River House is for everyone! The River House spotlights and supports LOCAL craftspeople. That is a big part of what we do. 
 Our non-profit program emphasizes the importance of active participation, affordable access, and multi-generational education. The River House offers:
Community Art and Music Experiences
Arts and Music Education Programs
Local Artist and Performer Support and Exposure
 The River House is a fun place for anyone to hang out! Please stop in for a cup of coffee or bring a friend, your children or your grandchildren to one of our programs. Everything is family-friendly and all are welcome.
