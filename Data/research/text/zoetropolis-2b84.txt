About
The Evolution of Zoetropolis Changing Over Time
Zoetropolis is a movie theater and distillery! Our hours of operation follow the showtimes of our movies. Check out our showtimes to learn more about our independent films, live music shows and community events!
The Original Location circa the 1990s
Lemon Street
Zoetropolis started out in the 1990’s as a small Art house theater dreamed up and run by Leigh Lindsay. Even in its early stages Zoe quickly gathered a hefty following of loyal fans who came not only for the films but for music and art.
Zoe Version 2 circa 2014
James Street
Through hard work and a love of the arts Leigh, Cheila and Nate nurtured and expanded Zoetropolis into a venue that has effectively blended film with engaging collaborations with other local businesses such as restaurant partnerships, events for cultural awareness, and social causes. Through these experiences, we broadened our vision of what Zoetropolis is and what it could be.
 Our New Location 2019
Water Street
With the addition of Todd and Matt, our team of 5 owners has come together to further develop Zoetropolis into an all new cinema experience. Zoetropolis is the destination for anyone who loves film, libations, food, and music. All of our energies and creativity have been culminating to this point and we’re beyond thrilled to share our new vision of Zoe with you.
Five Managing Partners
Meet The Team
The Zoetropolis team is a passionate group of people who came together in support of the arts who aim to create a home for high-quality art films, spirits, food, and music. Combining our ideas and skills, we’ve developed the Art House into a Cinema Stillhouse where we enhance the movie-going experience by pairing it with an onsite micro distillery.

2026 Red Rose Film Festival: Dread: Horror & Dystopia
 Coming on November 13 
 Run Time: 72 min.
That creeping feeling of terror that you just can’t shake? It thrives in this block filled with nightmare visions, annhiliation, and the things that lurk in the shadows…
Hide: Short narrative. Still reeling from a devastating loss, a father and daughter cling to a bedtime routine that keeps them safe in a town visited by an unfathomable horror each night.
The Other End of the Hallway: Short narrative. A grieving father, shattered and isolated, withdraws into a house that no longer is a home. The walls close in. Sleep disappears. Then, he hears it…
Hell: Animated short. On the outskirts of Ash Harbor, the patriarch of a dying farm is slowly ground down by despair in this portrait of endurance and collapse.
What Beckons in the Wood: Short narrative. Fleeing through a forest in the 1930s, Edgar encounters a mysterious creature offering liberation. Its bargain asks him to surrender whatever binds him.
Fractured Reality: Short narrative. After an EMP resets humanity, a silent tracker must infiltrate the territory of a ruthless militarized cult to find the source of the disaster.
OnlyFan: Short narrative. A mail carrier awakens restrained in a stranger’s basement. Confusion gives way to obsession, control, and a chilling plea for companionship he may not survive.
