Tommy Prine and Delaney Ramsdell Live in Chattanooga | Songbirds
Doors at 6:00 PM
Show at 7:00 PM
Seated Show
Son of late songwriting legend, John Prine, Tommy Prine grew up in Nashville surrounded by music, art and writing. As a child, he thought all parents were musicians, as his father “going to work” meant performing shows for adoring fans and writing songs. Tommy learned to play guitar by watching his father play, copying the ways his fingers moved and inadvertently developing his own singular style. Summers in Ireland lent their own inspiration, as did 10 straight years camping at Bonnaroo. Prine’s musical tastes grew to become decidedly eclectic, spanning John Mayer, Outkast, Bon Iver, the Strokes and more.
It wasn’t until Prine reached his mid-twenties, though, that he considered a career of his own in music and began to share with others the songs he wrote in private. His songs were quickly met with excitement and enthusiasm, which sonically brings together a colorful patchwork of musical influences and lyrically explores existential questions and emotional experiences.
Known for his emotionally raw songwriting and spellbinding live performances, Tommy Prine’s music continues to carve out space beyond the shadow of legacy. As PBS remarked after a recent show in Los Angeles, “Listening on this night at Gold Diggers… you might think Tommy Prine was born to the stage.”
Delaney Ramsdell is an artist and songwriter based in Nashville, TN. Originally from Roosevelt, Texas (population 18), her sound, songwriting and storytelling is steeped in the culture of West Texas.
Her newest single “285” tells the story of Texas oilfields and the road that runs through them – 285, the most dangerous highway in America. Listen to “285” here.
“I have a deep tie to this place: my mom grew up on a gas camp near Coyanosa, Texas while my grandfather worked in oil & gas.”
“I tracked the song live with the band, trying to make it as raw & organic as possible. I started tearing up when we were recording– it felt so real and visceral in the moment and points to the power of storytelling.”
A Grammy nominated songwriter, signed to One Riot Publishing & Reservoir Media, her cuts have amassed over 70 million streams to date with tracks including the Grammy-nominated “Make You Happy” (recorded by Lukas Nelson), “Thankful” (recorded by Paul Cauthen) and “East Texas and You” (recorded by Solon Holt).
Her songwriting portfolio spans artists the likes of Lola Kirke, Josh Abbott, Zoe Clark, Trey Pendley, Taylor Austin Dye, Ian Harrison, Aniston Pate, and more. She was most recently featured on Paul Cauthen’s new record ‘Book Of Paul’ with their duet “Chain Smoking”.

J. Roddy Walston and SOL Parade Live in Chattanooga | Songbirds
Doors at 6:00 PM
Show at 7:00 PM
Standing with Limited Seating
J. Roddy Walston is a force of nature. If his hits like Heavy Bells are a war cry, then the deep cuts like Boys Can Never Tell are a tactical cosmic retreat. For some, his music is a form of religion, psychedelic in its intensity, transformative in its grip on the body, shaking loose something primal that’s been buried too long. It’s not about thinking, It’s about moving, feeling, surrendering. His influences are a riot of contradictions: Kurt Cobain’s fury, Nilsson’s fractured sweetness, Zeppelin’s power, and somewhere in there a perverted nod to William Onyeabor’s lonely calculator funk. Critics try to box him in, but Walston can’t be contained—he does chaos, and his live shows do damage, but fortunately his songs can pay the tab. Catch him and his band before he burns it all down again.
