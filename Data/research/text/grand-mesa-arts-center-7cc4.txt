Donate
​The Grand Mesa Arts & Events Center relies on generous annual support to sustain our operations, keep our programs vibrant, and ensure accessibility to the arts for all.
​
Your donation, regardless of the amount, directly fuels everything we do - from providing essential art education workshops for local students to hosting captivating performances and gallery exhibitions that enrich our entire community.
​
You can also designate all or part of your gift to one of our designated funds (details below).
Supporters whose annual cash gifts total $20,000 or more enjoy benefits in the Masterpiece Circle.
​
We can't do this without you! Invest in the power of the arts.
Donate today!
DONATE NOW
Legacy Giving
A powerful way to support our mission for generations, without affecting your finances today. Create lasting impact by including the Grand Mesa Arts and Events Center Endowment Fund in your will or trust. Your legacy can be a gift for the arts, tomorrow and always.
Learn More
Membership
GMAEC members have a profound impact on our community through their support of dynamic visual and performing arts activities. Your support provides the resources necessary to advance programs that encourage intercultural exchange and promote the education and appreciation of the arts.
Learn More
Designations
Power our mission. Your contribution is vital, and you choose where it makes the greatest impact!
Designate all or part of your gift to:
YEA! — Fueling our most impactful program.
General Operating Funds — Supporting the daily work essential to everything we do.
Capital Improvement Fund — Investing in necessary upgrades and facilities.
Endowment Fund — Ensuring our future sustainability for generations to come.
YEA!
YEA! (Youth Empowerment Through the Arts)
YEA! is the Grand Mesa Arts and Events Center’s (GMAEC) program that provides all classes, camps, and art educational opportunities for Pre K-12 students at absolutely no cost. GMAEC believes that access to the arts is not a luxury, but a fundamental necessity. Our FREE youth programming is a direct investment in the vitality of our community. Your gift helps build the YEA! fund and guarantees FREE youth art opportunities for the current year.
General Operating Funds
Your gift enables GMAEC staff to continue to provide and even raise the bar on the quality of concerts, art exhibits, classes, theatre, film and destination events (Grand Mesa Songwriter Festival, Grand Mesa Short Film Festival and the Grand Mesa Writer’s Symposium). GMAEC attracts sell-out crowds because of our diverse programming that requires funding in our general operating budget. Salaries, utilities and maintenance are also needs within our yearly budget.
Capital Improvements Reserve Fund
Every year we strive to maintain a reserve fund for improvements and maintenance of our historic building. We need your gifts to fund our upcoming needs such as painting the outside of the art center, providing better handicap access from our parking lot, providing safe hardscape access between the art center and the Art Garage and lighting in the Pottery Studio. Unforeseen projects arise throughout the year and the reserve fund is a vital part of maintaining our property.
Endowment Fund
GMAEC has formed an Endowment Fund that will preserve the long-term financial health of the art center. While annual donations help the art center thrive today, contributions to the Endowment Fund ensure our strength for generations to come. Your endowment gifts are a lasting legacy that honors your love of art, your belief in community and your desire to preserve excellence in the arts for future audiences.

When Words Become Breath
A Spoken Word Open Mic Forum
Hosted by Rene' Janiece
In the Starr Family Gallery
Time & Location
Sep 02, 2025, 6:00 PM – 7:30 PM
Grand Mesa Arts & Events Center, 195 W Main St, Cedaredge, CO 81413, USA
Other dates
Tue, Dec 01, 6:00 PM
Details
The Grand Mesa Arts & Events Center invites you to join your host Rene’ Janiece, for an opportunity to share your own work and listen to the work of others, all coming together in love of the written word.
First Tuesday of the month | Free and open to the public | $5 suggested donation
6:00 pm in the Starr Family Gallery | Sign up begins at 5:30 pm | Speaking/Reading times - 5 minutes
Writing tends to be a very solitary endeavor. And while this is necessary to the process, it can become isolating and even discouraging at times. Readings and open mic forums have long offered a place for writers to share their work and works-in-progress in a welcoming environment. This allows writers a chance to experience first-hand reactions to their pieces, to step outside the page, to hear what happens when their words become breath.
About Your Host
Rene’ Janiece is fascinated with the play and power of words - how they string together, how they hold space, how they resonate, how they hang in the air when they are offered the opportunity to breathe. While she is many things, she is a writer, musician, and artist, exploring what it means to meet this world with the heart of a poet. She finds inspiration in what is often overlooked – rusted wire, a fallen bird’s nest, the blooming bindweed, the moment right in front of us. She believes it is her job to be witness to all of it.
Rene’ is a six-year Inflammatory Breast Cancer survivor, still in treatment, doing her best to be present with whatever each day brings. She has released two music albums with her husband, Wayne McKinzie, as part of the duo Bittersweet Highway, and she is the creator of the on-line writing workshop A Moment’s Notice. Her new poetry chapbook, Rosemary and Rust, will be released in July of 2025. She lives in Cedaredge, Colorado with her husband Wayne, dog Naia-Grace, still-sometimes-feral house-cat Sydney, and her rescue cows (steers) Remy and Connor.Read More >

Grand Mesa Story Share
Experience the dynamic and unforgettable journey of three Cedaredge stories through storytellers, videographers, musicians, writers, visual artists and actors.
Tickets are not on sale
See other events
Time & Location
May 07, 2026, 7:00 PM – 9:00 PM
Grand Mesa Arts & Events Center, 195 W Main St, Cedaredge, CO 81413, USA
Details
STORY ONE
Storyteller Carol Ann Rasmussen
Carol Ann Rasmussen moved many times for her husband Dave’s career before they retired and settled in Cedaredge, Colorado in 2004. She is an accomplished alcohol ink artist who says that she is so thankful for her family. She has had a wonderful life filled with love. She is content.
Videographer Phillip Berghauser
Phillip Berghauser is a Colorado-based landscape photographer and videographer currently residing in Eckert, Colorado. Deeply rooted in the rugged beauty of the Western Slope, Phillip focuses on capturing the raw, resilient spirit of the natural world. Through high-definition videography and still photography, Phillip invites viewers to see the world not just as a cinematic backdrop, but as a living story that “doesn’t always end where we thought it would.”
Musician Natalie Spears
Natalie Spears brings listeners in touch with the warmth of community, the quiet awe of wild places, and the intimate corners of humanity. With a quiver of instruments (banjo, piano, guitar), she threads opposites together: love with loss; tradition with innovation; and the human experience with the more-than-human world.
Artist Elizabeth Bonzani
Elizabeth Bonzani grew up on a farm in Connecticut before heading out West to spend nearly 40 years in Flagstaff, AZ, where she studied ceramics under Don Bendel. She is currently the Chief Mudslinger at GMAEC’s Art Garage. 
Writer Kierstin Bridger
Kierstin Bridger is a Colorado writer. She has authored two books, All Ember (Urban Farmhouse Press) and Demimonde (Lithic Press), which won the Women Writing The West's WILLA Award for poetry. Her most recent essay is a finalist for the Dogwood Literary Award in non-fiction. Find her work at Poetry, Swwim, Raleigh Review, Painted Bride Quarterly, Prairie Schooner and New York Quarterly. 
STORY TWO
Storyteller Don Paul Benjamin
Donald Paul Benjamin is an award-winning novelist and freelance cartoonist. He grew up on a Colorado farm and enlisted in the U.S. Army where he served three years as a journalist, including a tour in Korea. He worked at colleges and universities in Arizona until retiring in 2014 to move to Cedaredge.
Videographer Phillip Berghauser
Phillip Berghauser is a Colorado-based landscape photographer and videographer currently residing in Eckert, Colorado. Deeply rooted in the rugged beauty of the Western Slope, Phillip focuses on capturing the raw, resilient spirit of the natural
world. Through high-definition videography and still photography, Phillip invites viewers to see the world not just as a cinematic backdrop but as a living story that "doesn't always end where we thought it would."
Musicians Wayne McKinzie & Martha Ditto
Wayne McKinzie is a musician and fine artist living in Cedaredge. He and his wife Rene’ toured the Midwest and Southwest performing their original music as the duo Bittersweet Highway. His paintings have received awards in group shows and galleries in Colorado, Utah, Texas and New Mexico.
Martha Ditto is a professional cellist from Nashville Tennessee where she worked in the music business for over forty years. She moved to Colorado in 2014, mentoring students in the Valley Youth Orchestra, providing private cello lessons, and continuing to perform and record music. With violinist, Leigh Mahoney, she is half of the duo known as The Mesa Strings. She also raises some pretty spectacular goats!
Artist Jane Dupree
Jane Dupree is a Colorado-based artist specializing in fiber, whose work is deeply influenced by the natural beauty and quiet rhythms of life on the Western Slope. Living and creating in Cedaredge, she draws daily inspiration from the dramatic landscapes of the Grand Mesa, open skies, orchards, and the changing mountain light that define the region.
Writer Judy Fairchild
Judy Fairchild, a Colorado native, grew up in Gunnison, CO. She received her MA from Western State in education. She is married and has two daughters, three grandchildren, and one great grandson. She has lived and worked as an educator for Delta County. Now retired, she writes, hikes, plays pickleball, volunteers for the Grand Mesa Arts and Events Center and enjoys her family and friends. 
STORY THREE
Storyteller Alan Bull
Alan Bull is a fourth-generation rancher with lifelong ties to the Cedaredge community. He and his wife, Linda, continue to live and work on the family homestead on Bull Mesa, settled by Alan’s great-grandfather in the late 1800’s.
Videographer Heidi Weissner
Heidi Weissner is a retired paralegal now enjoying the creative aspects of working as a website/graphic designer and marketing manager for non-profit organizations in Cedaredge, Colorado. She and her husband moved to Cedaredge in 2019 and enjoy hiking, biking, camping, skiing, tennis, kayaking, horseback riding, and pretty much anything outdoors. 
Musician David Starr
David Starr is an Americana/Folk musician with his roots in the blues. He is a critically acclaimed internationally touring musician who has released over 11 albums and shared the stage with acts like America, John Oats, Jim Messina and more. An Arkansas native, he made his way to Cedaredge, Colorado in 2001. He is the owner of Starrs Guitars, a founding member of the Grand Mesa Arts and Events Center, and the producer of the annual Grand Mesa Songwriter Festival.
Artist Jean Stortz
Jean Marie Stortz is a visual artist working with paint, collage, assemblage and drawing since 1992. Nature and abstraction are her “go-tos”. She sees each new piece of art as a path to follow, a process of resolving rather than finishing.
Writer Rene' Janiece
Rene’ Janiece is a wordsmith, a wonder-gatherer, a cow-whisperer and a bittersweet witness, always exploring what it means to meet the world with the heart of a poet. Her self-published poetry chapbook, Rosemary and Rust, was released in 2025. She lives in Cedaredge, Colorado where she is the Curator of Grand Mesa Story Share, and the host of When Words Become Breath – a monthly spoken word open mic forum at the Grand Mesa Arts and Events Center.
