About the Sutter Creek Theatre
The Sutter Creek Theatre Today
Owned by Laura and Byron Damiani Jr., the historic Sutter Creek Theatre is a complete multipurpose entertainment center that hosts events year-round. The theatre features concerts by award-winning artists, films, theater, civic and corporate meetings, and celebrations like reunions and weddings.
A former silent film theatre, the venue comfortably seats 215 people. Because it was designed for viewing film, every seat has a view of the stage. The original art deco ceiling designs are intact and inspired our logo. We have a balcony and the original theatre seats, complete with hat racks underneath. The theatre is renowned for its excellent acoustics and intimate seating.
For traveling artists and theater groups we offer sound engineering and lighting support. We serve beer, wine, other beverages and snacks. The theatre building is also home to Heart & Soul at 42 Main, offering unique home decor, jewelry and more.
 Owners Byron and Laura Damiani
Our Mission
The Sutter Creek Theatre professionally presents contemporary and traditional music, theatrical works, dance, film, and other events that are engaging to the hearts and minds of a diverse regional audience.
To achieve our mission, we strive to:
Present a variety of performance and cinematic experiences for a culturally, generationally and socially diverse audience.
Provide a culturally rich and engaging environment that fosters an appreciation of the arts while providing wholesome and stimulating entertainment.
Educate the community and our young people in an appreciation of the arts.
Serve as a cultural center for the arts in Amador County.
Our values
Commitment to artistic products and programs that enrich people's lives.
Service to the communities in Amador County.
Respect for our audience.
Responsible management of the Theatre as an historic and artistic treasure for the community.
Our vision
The Sutter Creek Theatre seeks to be a vital, regionally recognized theatre of true artistic merit and a business that is acknowledged as being vital to the economic health of Sutter Creek. We pursue this vision by:
Offering a broad range of musical, theatrical, dance, film, and other performance works on our stage.
Developing, diversifying, and increasing our audience base, both locally and regionally.
Improving our physical facilities, including preserving the historic attributes of the theatre.
Offering a progressive and comprehensive arts education program for young and adult audiences, and forging relationships with educational institutions.
Establishing a nonprofit entity to support the theatre and the arts in Amador County.
A Great History: Over 100 Years
 44 Main Street, Sutter Creek
Located in the heart of the Mother Lode, the Sutter Creek Theatre has survived a tumultuous history. Its wooden predecessors rose repeatedly out of the ashes of the town's early-day fires. In the 1860s a saloon and billiard parlor stood on the site, but fires in 1862, 1865, 1868 and 1888 destroyed it and the other wooden shops there. By 1912 the site held a saloon, barber shop and bocce ball court.
Around 1912 John F. Ratto, a Sutter Creek merchant who was also the county's Hupmobile dealer, opened a silent-movie house in the 1850s brick building now known as the Bellotti Inn, across the street. Ratto then leased this site and began building a two-story brick theatre. Sutter Creek has a strong Italian influence, and architect S.F. Dal Proto built the theatre with the traditional arches and stonework found in many Italian buildings of the 18th century.
By the end of the year the (then) 250-seat theatre was practically complete, but the Spanish Influenza epidemic of 1919 closed all of Sutter Creek's public buildings and delayed the grand opening. The records have been lost, but rumor has it the theatre opened its doors on Wednesday, March 5, 1920, playing D. W. Griffith's Hearts of the World, starring Lillian Gish.
For two decades the theatre thrived. The owners lived in the large apartment above the lobby, and Mrs. Ratto played the piano and organ to accompany the silent movies. By the early 1930s talkies had replaced silent films, and the theatre thrived for another two decades. In 1952 it closed, playing Walt Disney's Bambi as its last feature.
The theatre's first renovation came in the 1970s, and later several rows of lower-floor seats were removed to install a stage. A second, major renovation ran from 2000 to 2003, and the current owners continue to upgrade and restore the building. Patrons and artists enjoy modern sound, lighting, heating and air conditioning, and restoration of the original seating continues.
Four "Ratto" theaters were built in Amador County. The Plymouth theater's location has been lost, the Ione theater was torn down for a parking lot in the 1970s, and fire destroyed the Ratto Theater in Jackson on October 14, 1998. The Sutter Creek Theatre is the only remaining original silent-movie house in the region.

Backline
We negotiate artist fees assuming there is no backline. Please tell us when contracting if you have backline needs, since it affects the fee we can pay. Backline generally has to come from out of the area.
Parking and unloading
Park in front of the theatre on Main Street (old Hwy 49), or unload at the back of the theatre in Boston Alley, which runs parallel behind Main Street. Boston Alley is for unloading only; we'll point you to residential parking a block or so away.
Load-in and sound check
Saturday shows: load in about 3:00, sound check 4:00, dinner 5:00, doors 6:30, show 7:00.
Friday shows: load in 4:00, sound check 5:00, dinner 6:00, doors 7:30, show 8:00.
Sunday shows: usually late afternoon with doors at 3 or 4 pm. Contact us for load-in times.
We build in time for flexibility and can usually move times earlier if needed. The stage is about 15 feet deep by 30 feet wide, with a backstage area about 10 feet deep behind the curtain (home to our Steinway grand piano) and a green room behind that. From Boston Alley, come in through the marked stage door via the green room, or through the fire exit.
Meals, guests, merchandise and lodging
Meals: we usually provide the band with a pizza meal.
Guest lists: we're small, so please keep them small and contact us in advance.
Merchandise: we have a lobby table for CDs, t-shirts and more. Let us know if you need us to sell merch.
Lodging: a 3-bedroom apartment above the theatre sleeps 6 (two queen rooms and one room with two twins).
Sound and tech
Byron usually runs sound, but let us know if you're bringing your own engineer or equipment. It's a small, very live room, so we often don't mic drums and have done acoustic shows completely unamplified. It's sound reinforcement more than volume. On-stage amps should be small or run well at low volume, and we prefer to mic them or take a line out into the house mix. Please send a stage plot or input list and any equipment needs.
Mixing: Allen & Heath Qu-32 digital board with a digital stage box; we usually run 24 or fewer inputs.
Monitors: 7 discrete monitor mixes, 10 JBL powered monitors.
House: QSC powered tops and QSC powered subs.
Mics: 6 Shure SM58s, 5 Shure SM57s, a kick drum mic and a Shure condenser, plus passive and active DIs.
Video: projector and 16-foot screen.
Media and promotion
We usually pull bios, photos and promo material from your website. We especially need good color photos (300 dpi minimum) and a short tag line describing the act. We'll send you our list of local media; it helps when artists contact them too, and there may be radio or print interview opportunities. Send links to any official videos you'd like us to post, and mail at least one copy of your latest CD to our P.O. Box. Let us know if you have posters.
Please list the show on your own website and calendars and tell your email list. We contact local media, email our list, put up posters and flyers, list the show on our website and event calendars, and stay very active on Facebook.

Ticket Terms
Last updated October 27, 2026
By buying tickets for a show at the Sutter Creek Theatre, you agree to these terms. They're short, and we've kept them plain.
Prices and fees
Online prices include a per-ticket online service fee. The price you see is the price you pay.
Advance prices apply until online sales close, usually at midnight before show day. After that, tickets are sold at the door at the door price, while they last.
Some shows sell out online. When they do, a small number of tickets may still be available at the door.
All sales are final
Please review your order carefully. All ticket sales are final, and we can't offer refunds or credit toward other shows if you're unable to attend.
If a show is ever cancelled, we'll reschedule the artist or welcome you at another show. Cancellations are very rare. Artists, show times and lineups can occasionally change.
Your tickets
Your tickets are emailed to you, each with its own QR code. Show them on your phone or printed at the door.
Each code can be scanned once. You can send a ticket to a friend, but whoever shows a code first gets in, so keep your codes to yourself until then.
Lost your email? Your name is on the list at the door, or call us and we'll resend your tickets.
Tickets bought on our old website are honored. Your name is on the list at the door.
At the theatre
Seating is open (first come, first seated). Doors usually open 30 minutes before showtime.
Beer and wine are available to guests 21 and over with a valid ID.
We may refuse entry to, or ask to leave, anyone who is disruptive or unsafe, without a refund.
Questions
Call 916.425.0077 or email info@suttercreektheatre.com. See also our Privacy Policy.
